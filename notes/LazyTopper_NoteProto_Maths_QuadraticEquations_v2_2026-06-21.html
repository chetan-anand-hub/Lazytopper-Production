Quadratic Equations

★

What the board actually asks: solving by factorisation & the quadratic formula, the discriminant (nature of roots), and word problems — these carry almost every mark here.

The Big Idea

One question, a few tools.

A quadratic equation is any equation you can write as $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a \neq 0$. The whole chapter answers one question: what values of $x$ make this true? Those values are the roots.

You'll learn to find them (factorisation, the quadratic formula), decide whether they exist (the discriminant), relate them to the coefficients, and turn real situations into equations you can solve.

Definitions the board rewards

State these exactly

CBSE often gives 1 mark for the precise textbook statement. Learn them word-for-word.

Standard form
A quadratic equation in $x$ is of the form $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, where $a, b, c$ are real and $a \neq 0$.
Root
A real number $\alpha$ is a root of $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$ if $a\alpha^2 + b\alpha + c = 0$. The zeroes of the polynomial and the roots of the equation are the same.
Discriminant
For $ax^2 + bx + c = 0$, the quantity $D = b^2 - 4ac$ is the discriminant. It decides whether real roots exist.

Worked CBSE questions — decoded

One example per question-shape

Tap each to expand. Watch which concept does the work at every step.

Shape 1 · Factorisation3 marks▸
Find the roots of $6x^2 - x - 2 = 0$ by factorisation.
1
Find the split. $a\cdot c = 6\times(-2) = -12$. Two numbers with product $-12$, sum $-1$: $-4$ and $+3$.Concept: split the middle term
2
Rewrite & group. $6x^2 - 4x + 3x - 2 = 0 \Rightarrow 2x(3x-2) + 1(3x-2) = 0$.Concept: factor by grouping
3
Factor & solve. $(3x-2)(2x+1) = 0 \Rightarrow x = \dfrac{2}{3}$ or $x = -\dfrac{1}{2}$.Concept: zero-product rule
Mark-scheme logic: 1 for the split, 1 for the factorisation, 1 for both roots. Write both roots — a common lost mark.
Shape 2 · Discriminant (find k)CBSE 2024 · 5 marks▸
Find the value of $k$ for which $(k+1)x^2 - 6(k+1)x + 3(k+9) = 0,\ k \neq -1$, has real and equal roots.
1
Recognise the trigger. "Real and equal roots" → $D = 0$.Concept: nature of roots
2
Write the discriminant. $a=(k+1),\ b=-6(k+1),\ c=3(k+9)$. Set $b^2 - 4ac = 0$: $\;36(k+1)^2 - 12(k+1)(k+9) = 0$.Concept: substitute into D
3
Simplify — don't expand blindly. $12(k+1)\big[3(k+1) - (k+9)\big] = 0 \Rightarrow 12(k+1)(2k-6) = 0$.Concept: factor, don't expand
4
Solve & reject. $k=-1$ is excluded — reject. $2k-6=0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{k = 3}$.Concept: honour the constraint — the trap most miss
Mark-scheme logic: 1 for $D=0$ · 2 for simplification · 1 for solving · 1 for rejecting $k=-1$. Miss the rejection and you lose the final mark.
Shape 3 · Word problem3–4 marks▸
The product of two consecutive positive integers is 306. Find the integers.
1
Name the unknown. Let the integers be $x$ and $x+1$.Concept: set up the variable
2
Build the equation. $x(x+1) = 306 \Rightarrow x^2 + x - 306 = 0$.Concept: translate to standard form
3
Solve. $(x+18)(x-17) = 0 \Rightarrow x = 17$ or $x = -18$.Concept: factorise
4
Reject the invalid root. Positive integers → reject $x=-18$. Answer: $\mathbf{17}$ and $\mathbf{18}$.Concept: reject the impossible root
Mark-scheme logic: 1 for the equation, 1–2 for solving, 1 for the rejection + answer. Forgetting to reject $-18$ costs the last mark.
Shape 4 · Given roots → form the equation2 marks▸
Form the quadratic equation whose roots are $3$ and $-2$.
1
Sum and product. $\alpha+\beta = 1$; $\alpha\beta = -6$.Concept: roots & coefficients
2
Use the standard form. $x^2 - (\alpha+\beta)x + \alpha\beta = 0$.Concept: the form-the-equation identity
3
Substitute. $x^2 - (1)x + (-6) = 0 \Rightarrow \mathbf{x^2 - x - 6 = 0}$.Concept: simplify
Mark-scheme logic: 1 for sum & product, 1 for the equation. Watch the middle-term sign — it's $-(\alpha+\beta)$.
Problem-solving strategies

How to attack any quadratic under pressure

→
"real / equal / no roots" or "find k"? Go straight to $D = b^2 - 4ac$. Don't solve for $x$.
→
"Find the roots"? Factorise first; if the middle term won't split, use the formula.
→
"Roots given / form the equation"? Sum & product → $x^2 - (\alpha+\beta)x + \alpha\beta = 0$.
→
Word problem? Name $x$ → build equation → standard form → solve → reject the impossible root.
→
Coefficient holds the unknown (like $(k+1)x^2$)? Check $a \neq 0$.
Common mistakes that cost marks

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Forgetting to reject the invalid root — negative length / age / count, or an excluded value like $k \neq -1$.
✕
Sign slips in $D$ — when $b$ is negative, $(-6)^2 = +36$, not $-36$.
✕
Sign error in form-the-equation — the middle term is $-(\alpha+\beta)$, not $+$.
✕
Writing "no roots" when $D<0$ — say "no real roots".

Proof / Derivation

The quadratic formula, by completing the square

CBSE can ask you to derive this directly. Every line earns method marks — show all of them.

1
Start from standard form. $ax^2 + bx + c = 0,\quad a \neq 0$.
2
Divide through by $a$. $x^2 + \dfrac{b}{a}x + \dfrac{c}{a} = 0$.
3
Move the constant across. $x^2 + \dfrac{b}{a}x = -\dfrac{c}{a}$.
4
Complete the square. Add $\left(\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2$ to both sides: $\;x^2 + \dfrac{b}{a}x + \left(\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \left(\dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2 - \dfrac{c}{a}$.
5
Write the left as a perfect square. $\left(x + \dfrac{b}{2a}\right)^2 = \dfrac{b^2 - 4ac}{4a^2}$.
6
Take square roots. $x + \dfrac{b}{2a} = \pm\dfrac{\sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}$  (real only when $b^2 - 4ac \ge 0$).
7
Isolate $x$. $x = \dfrac{-b \pm \sqrt{b^2 - 4ac}}{2a}.\quad\blacksquare$
Note on this topic's Proof tab: Quadratics has no formal "theorem to prove" like Geometry — this completing-the-square derivation is the one formal piece the board assesses. (Topics with no board-assessed proof show their key derivation here, or none.)
