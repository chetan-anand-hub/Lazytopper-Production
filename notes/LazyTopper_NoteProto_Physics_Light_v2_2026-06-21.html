Light — Reflection & Refraction

★

What the board actually asks: ray diagrams for mirrors & lenses, the mirror/lens formula numericals (with sign convention), refractive index, and power of a lens — these carry almost every mark here.

The Big Idea

Light bends and bounces — predictably.

Two behaviours explain this whole chapter. Light reflects off mirrors (bouncing back) and refracts through lenses and other media (bending as it changes speed). Once you can trace a couple of standard rays, you can predict exactly where an image forms, how big it is, and whether it's real or virtual — and the mirror/lens formula turns that into numbers.

Definitions the board rewards

State these exactly

1 mark for the precise statement. Learn them word-for-word.

Laws of reflection
(i) The angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection ($\angle i = \angle r$). (ii) The incident ray, the reflected ray, and the normal at the point of incidence all lie in the same plane.
Laws of refraction (Snell's law)
(i) The incident ray, refracted ray, and normal lie in the same plane. (ii) $\dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$ is constant for a given pair of media — the refractive index.
Refractive index
The refractive index of a medium is $n = \dfrac{\text{speed of light in vacuum}}{\text{speed of light in the medium}} = \dfrac{c}{v}$.
Principal focus (concave mirror)
The point on the principal axis where rays parallel to the axis converge after reflection. The distance from the pole to this point is the focal length $f$, and $R = 2f$.

Refraction & refractive index
Light bends toward the normal entering a denser medium. $n = \dfrac{c}{v} = \dfrac{\sin i}{\sin r}$.
2–3 marks
4
Apparent depth & everyday refraction
Because light bends when leaving a denser medium, objects in water look shallower / raised and a pencil looks bent. The relation: $\;n = \dfrac{\text{real depth}}{\text{apparent depth}}$. Real depth is always greater than apparent depth.

Lens formula, magnification & power
$\dfrac{1}{v} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{f}$, $\;m = \dfrac{v}{u}$, and power $P = \dfrac{1}{f\,(\text{metres})}$ in dioptres (D).

One example per question-shape

The sign convention does the work — watch it applied at step 1 every time.

Shape 1 · Mirror formula3 marks▸
An object is placed 10 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15 cm. Find the position, nature and magnification of the image.
1
Apply the sign convention first. Object in front → $u = -10$ cm. Concave mirror → $f = -15$ cm.Concept: New Cartesian signs
2
Mirror formula. $\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} - \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{-15} - \dfrac{1}{-10} = \dfrac{-2+3}{30} = \dfrac{1}{30}$.Concept: substitute with signs
3
Solve & read the sign. $v = +30$ cm. Positive → image is behind the mirror → virtual and erect.Concept: sign tells nature
4
Magnification. $m = -\dfrac{v}{u} = -\dfrac{30}{-10} = +3$. Erect, 3× magnified (the make-up-mirror case).Concept: m sign + size
Mark-scheme logic: 1 for correct signs, 1 for the formula & solving, 1 for nature + magnification. The signs are where most marks are lost.
Shape 2 · Lens formula3 marks▸
An object is placed 25 cm from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. Find the image position and magnification.
1
Signs. $u = -25$ cm; convex lens → $f = +10$ cm.Concept: lens sign convention
2
Lens formula. $\dfrac{1}{v} = \dfrac{1}{f} + \dfrac{1}{u} = \dfrac{1}{10} - \dfrac{1}{25} = \dfrac{5-2}{50} = \dfrac{3}{50}$.Concept: substitute
3
Solve. $v = +16.7$ cm → real image on the far side.Concept: read the sign
4
Magnification. $m = \dfrac{v}{u} = \dfrac{16.7}{-25} = -0.67$ → real, inverted, diminished.Concept: m for lens
Mark-scheme logic: 1 signs · 1 formula+solve · 1 nature. Note lens magnification is $+v/u$ (no minus, unlike the mirror's $m=-v/u$).

Use the definition. $n = \dfrac{c}{v} \Rightarrow v = \dfrac{c}{n}$.Concept: refractive index
2
Substitute. $v = \dfrac{3\times10^8}{1.5} = \mathbf{2\times10^8}$ m/s.Concept: compute

Shape 4 · Power of a lens1–2 marks▸
Find the power of a convex lens of focal length 25 cm.
1
Convert to metres. $f = 25$ cm $= +0.25$ m (convex → positive).Concept: units + sign

Problem-solving strategies

How to attack any Light question

→
Numerical? Write down $u, v, f$ with signs before touching the formula. Most marks are lost at the sign step, not the algebra.
→
"Draw the ray diagram"? Use any two of the three standard rays; the image is where the reflected/refracted rays meet.
→
Mirror vs lens magnification? Mirror $m=-v/u$; lens $m=+v/u$. Don't mix them.
→
Power question? Convert $f$ to metres first, then $P=1/f$ in dioptres.
Common mistakes that cost marks

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Sign convention errors — the #1 lost mark. Object distance $u$ is always negative; concave $f$ negative, convex $f$ positive.
✕
Mixing mirror and lens magnification — mirror $m=-v/u$, lens $m=+v/u$.
✕
Forgetting to convert to metres for power — $P=1/f$ needs $f$ in metres, not cm.
✕
Not stating the nature — a numerical answer needs "real/virtual, erect/inverted, magnified/diminished" for full marks.

Rules & Conventions

The New Cartesian Sign Convention

CBSE does not require the mirror/lens formula derivation for this chapter — so the formal core here isn't a proof, it's the sign convention and ray rules that make every numerical and diagram correct. This is the "Proof" tab, adapted to what the topic actually has.

Distances (along principal axis)

  • •All distances measured from the pole / optical centre.
  • −Distances against the incident light (object side) are negative.
  • +Distances along the incident light are positive.

What this means in practice

  • −Object distance $u$ is always negative (real object).
  • −Concave mirror / concave lens focal length: negative.
  • +Convex mirror / convex lens focal length: positive.
  • ±Heights above axis +, below axis −.
Ray-diagram construction

The three standard rays (use any two)

1
A ray parallel to the principal axis reflects/refracts through the principal focus F.
2
A ray through the focus F reflects/refracts parallel to the principal axis.
3
A ray through the centre of curvature C (mirror) / optical centre O (lens) returns along its path / passes undeviated.
