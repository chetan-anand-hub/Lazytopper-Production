Life Processes

★

What the board actually asks: the four life processes and how each organ/structure is designed for its job — alveoli & nephrons for surface area, double circulation, xylem vs phloem, aerobic vs anaerobic respiration, and labelled diagrams of the heart, alimentary canal and nephron. Most marks sit in process descriptions and well-labelled diagrams.

The Big Idea

Four processes keep a body from falling apart.

A living body is an ordered structure that the environment constantly tries to break down, so it must keep repairing itself — and that needs energy and raw materials. Nutrition brings food in, respiration releases its energy, transportation carries materials around a large body, and excretion removes the harmful by-products. Single cells manage all of this by simple diffusion across their surface; large multicellular bodies cannot, so each job is handed to a specialised organ system.

Definitions the board rewards

State these exactly

One-mark questions ask for these word-for-word — say them the way the textbook does.

Life processes
The maintenance functions that must go on even when an organism is doing nothing in particular — nutrition, respiration, transportation and excretion.
Autotrophic nutrition
Intake of simple inorganic materials (CO₂ and water) and use of an external energy source (the Sun) to synthesise complex high-energy organic material. Seen in green plants and some bacteria.
Heterotrophic nutrition
Intake of complex material prepared by other organisms. The heterotroph’s survival depends, directly or indirectly, on autotrophs. Seen in animals and fungi.
Photosynthesis
The process by which autotrophs take in substances from outside and convert them into stored forms of energy — CO₂ and water become carbohydrate in the presence of sunlight and chlorophyll.
Respiration
Acquiring oxygen from outside the body and using it to break down food sources for cellular needs, releasing energy that is stored as ATP.
Double circulation
Blood passes through the heart twice in each complete cycle — once to the lungs (pulmonary) and once to the body (systemic) — keeping oxygenated and deoxygenated blood from mixing.
Transpiration
The loss of water as vapour from the aerial parts of a plant. The resulting transpiration pull draws water up the xylem.
Excretion
The biological process of removing harmful metabolic wastes (such as nitrogenous urea) from the body.
Must-know concepts

…and where they’re tested

1
Photosynthesis — events & equation
Three events: absorption of light by chlorophyll, conversion of light to chemical energy with splitting of water, and reduction of CO₂ to carbohydrate. CO₂ enters through stomata (guard cells), water comes from the roots.
$6\,\text{CO}_2 + 12\,\text{H}_2\text{O}\;\xrightarrow[\text{Sunlight}]{\text{Chlorophyll}}\;\text{C}_6\text{H}_{12}\text{O}_6 + 6\,\text{O}_2 + 6\,\text{H}_2\text{O}$
3 mark · conditions + by-products
2
Human digestion along the alimentary canal
Each region has its own secretion: salivary amylase (mouth) on starch, HCl + pepsin + mucus (stomach), bile + pancreatic juice (trypsin, lipase) + intestinal juice (small intestine). Final products: amino acids, glucose, fatty acids & glycerol, absorbed by villi.
role of saliva / HCl / bile
3
Respiration pathways
Glucose (6C) → pyruvate (3C) in the cytoplasm. With oxygen, pyruvate is broken in the mitochondria to CO₂ + water (aerobic, more energy); without oxygen it becomes ethanol + CO₂ in yeast, or lactic acid in our muscles (causing cramps).
aerobic vs anaerobic
4
Lungs designed for exchange
Air reaches balloon-like alveoli with a huge surface area and a rich blood supply; oxygen diffuses into blood and is carried by haemoglobin. The large surface and thin walls maximise gas exchange.
how alveoli maximise exchange
5
Heart & double circulation
Four chambers keep oxygen-rich and oxygen-poor blood from mixing; ventricles have thicker walls than atria; valves stop backflow. Blood goes through the heart twice per cycle — efficient for warm-blooded animals with high energy needs.
double circulation · why necessary
6
Transport in plants — xylem vs phloem
Xylem carries water & minerals upward, driven by root pressure and transpiration pull (physical forces). Phloem carries food (translocation) both ways, using energy from ATP.
xylem vs phloem difference
7
Excretion — the nephron
Each kidney has many nephrons. Blood is filtered at the glomerulus into Bowman’s capsule; as filtrate flows along the tubule, glucose, amino acids, salts and much water are selectively re-absorbed; the rest leaves as urine.
nephron structure & urine formation
Worked examples — processes step by step

Trace each process the way the board wants it

Biology rule: give the ordered sequence and name the structure + its feature + the consequence. Tap to expand.

DigestionNCERT 5.2.4 · 3 mk▸
Trace a mouthful of bread through the human alimentary canal and name the digestive change in each region.
1
Mouth. Teeth crush the food; salivary amylase in saliva breaks starch into sugar. salivary amylase
2
Oesophagus. Peristaltic movements push the food down to the stomach. peristalsis
3
Stomach. Gastric glands release HCl (acidic medium), pepsin (digests protein) and mucus (protects the lining). HCl + pepsin
4
Small intestine. Bile emulsifies fat; pancreatic trypsin and lipase plus intestinal juice finish digestion → amino acids, glucose, fatty acids & glycerol. complete digestion
5
Absorption & exit. Villi absorb the digested food into the blood; the large intestine absorbs water; waste leaves via the anus. villi absorb
What earns the marks: each region named, its secretion/enzyme, the final products, and villi for absorption.
Double circulationNCERT 5.4.1 · 3 mk▸
Follow a drop of deoxygenated blood through the human heart and explain why circulation is called “double”.
1
Into the heart. De-oxygenated blood from the body enters the right atrium, which relaxes to receive it. right atrium
2
To the lungs. Right atrium → right ventricle → pumped to the lungs for oxygenation (pulmonary circuit). pulmonary circuit
3
Back & out. Oxygen-rich blood returns to the left atrium → left ventricle → pumped to the whole body (systemic circuit). systemic circuit
4
Why “double”. Blood passes through the heart twice per cycle. The separation gives an efficient oxygen supply — vital for warm-blooded birds and mammals. twice per cycle
What earns the marks: correct chamber order, the two circuits, no mixing of blood, and the efficiency reason.
Urine formationNCERT 5.5.1 · 3 mk▸
How is urine formed in a nephron?
1
Filtration. Blood enters the glomerulus under pressure; water and small solutes are filtered into Bowman’s capsule. glomerular filtration
2
Selective reabsorption. As filtrate flows along the tubule, glucose, amino acids, salts and most water are re-absorbed back into the blood. selective reabsorption
3
Regulation. The amount of water re-absorbed depends on the body’s excess water and the waste to be removed. water balance
4
Out. The remaining urine passes via the ureter to the bladder, and out through the urethra. ureter → bladder
What earns the marks: filtration at the glomerulus, selective reabsorption along the tubule, and that reabsorption is adjusted to the body’s needs.
Glucose breakdownNCERT Fig 5.8 · 3 mk▸
Compare the aerobic and anaerobic breakdown of glucose.
1
Common first step. Glucose (6C) → pyruvate (3C) in the cytoplasm, for both pathways. cytoplasm
2
Aerobic. With oxygen, pyruvate is broken in the mitochondria → CO₂ + water + much energy. mitochondria, +O₂
3
Anaerobic (yeast). Without oxygen, pyruvate → ethanol + CO₂ (fermentation). fermentation
4
Anaerobic (muscle). When our muscles lack oxygen, pyruvate → lactic acid — the build-up causes cramps. lactic acid
What earns the marks: same first step, the site (cytoplasm vs mitochondria), products of each route, and that aerobic releases far more energy.
Strategy

How to attack any Life Processes answer

→
Use exact NCERT terms. “selective reabsorption”, “transpiration pull”, “double circulation” — examiners look for the keyword.
→
For “how is X designed for Y” name the structure + its feature + the consequence (alveoli → large surface → more gas exchange).
→
For a process, give the ordered sequence — stage by stage, never a paragraph blur.
→
Diagrams earn marks. A neat, fully-labelled heart / alimentary canal / nephron (see the Diagrams tab) is often the difference.
Pitfalls

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Swapping xylem (water, upward) and phloem (food, both ways).
✕
Saying veins always carry deoxygenated blood — the pulmonary vein carries oxygenated blood.
✕
Forgetting that glycolysis (glucose → pyruvate) is in the cytoplasm, not the mitochondria.
✕
Writing the photosynthesis equation unbalanced or leaving out chlorophyll / sunlight over the arrow.
✕
Confusing filtration (at the glomerulus) with reabsorption (along the tubule).
