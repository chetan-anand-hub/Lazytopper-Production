🌍 What is an Ecosystem? पारिस्थितिकी तंत्र

An ecosystem is a self-sustaining unit of living organisms (biotic) interacting with their non-living (abiotic) environment. Click each component:

Producers (उत्पादक) – Plants
Sunlight (सूर्य प्रकाश)
Consumers (उपभोक्ता) – Animals
Water (जल)
Decomposers (अपघटक) – Fungi/Bacteria
Soil (मिट्टी)
Parasites & Scavengers
Temperature, Air (CO₂, O₂)

🌊 Natural Ecosystems – forests (वन), ponds (तालाब), oceans, grasslands. Energy flows naturally; self-regulating. Example: A pond ecosystem has algae → insects → frogs → snake → eagle.

🌾 Artificial Ecosystems – aquarium, crop fields, gardens. Maintained by humans. Energy input managed externally. Less stable than natural ecosystems.

⚡ 10% Energy Rule (दस प्रतिशत नियम) Key Concept

Energy at next level = 10% of previous level
Proposed by Lindemann (1942) | Only 10% energy transfers; 90% lost as heat (तापीय ऊर्जा)

👆 Click each level of the pyramid to see energy:

🐍 Snake – T4 1 J
🐸 Frog – T3 10 J
🦗 Grasshopper – T2 100 J
🌾 Grass – T1 (Producer) 1000 J
⚠️ This is why food chains rarely exceed 3–4 trophic levels – too little energy remains at higher levels!

🍄 Decomposers (अपघटक) Saprotrophs

Decomposers break down dead organic matter and return nutrients to soil. They are saprotrophs (मृतजीवी). Click each to learn more:

🍄

Fungi (कवक)

e.g., Mushroom, Rhizopus

🦠

Bacteria (जीवाणु)

e.g., Pseudomonas

🪱

Earthworm (केंचुआ)

Farmers' friend

✅ Role: Recycling nutrients (पोषक तत्वों का पुनर्चक्रण) → keeps the biosphere clean and balanced. Without decomposers, dead matter would accumulate and nutrients would be locked away!
