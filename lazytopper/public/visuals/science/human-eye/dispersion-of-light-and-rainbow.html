💡 Key Rule: Violet light bends the MOST, Red light bends the LEAST through a prism. This is because violet has the shortest wavelength and travels slowest in glass, giving it the highest refractive index (μ).

∵ n = c/v  |  Shorter λ → Lower speed in glass → Higher n → More bending
🌦️ Rainbow Formation (इन्द्रधनुष)
How Rainbow Forms:
1️⃣ Sunlight enters a spherical water droplet
2️⃣ Light refracts (bends) as it enters — different colours bend differently
3️⃣ Light undergoes Total Internal Reflection inside the droplet
4️⃣ Light refracts again as it exits → VIBGYOR spectrum appears!
5️⃣ Primary rainbow: Red outside (42°), Violet inside (40°)
📝 Worked Example — Step by Step

Q: A ray of white light passes through a glass prism (angle A = 60°). The refractive indices for violet and red light are 1.532 and 1.514 respectively. Which colour deviates more and why?

Step 1 — Click to reveal
Identify what determines bending: Refractive Index (μ)
Step 2 — Click to reveal
Compare: μviolet = 1.532 vs μred = 1.514
Step 3 — Click to reveal
Higher μ → More deviation. Using Snell's Law: μ = sin i / sin r
More μ means smaller angle of refraction → more bending toward normal
Step 4 — Click to reveal
∵ μviolet > μred
∴ Violet light deviates MORE than Red light
Angular dispersion = δv − δr ≈ (μv − μr) × A = (1.532−1.514) × 60° = 1.08°

⚡ Key Points to Remember
Newton's Discovery
Isaac Newton first used a prism to show white light is made of 7 colours (1665)
Recombination
An inverted prism can recombine VIBGYOR back into white light
Double Rainbow
2° rainbow: 2 reflections inside droplet; colours are reversed; fainter
Scattering vs Dispersion
Scattering: tiny particles scatter light. Dispersion: refraction splits light by wavelength
