✨ Lustre (धात्विक चमक)

Metals have a shiny surface because free electrons on their surface reflect light strongly. This shiny appearance is called metallic lustre.

📌 Examples: Gold (Au) ring shines, Silver (Ag) mirror, Aluminium (Al) foil reflects light. Mercury (Hg) liquid metal also shows lustre.
⚠️ Exception: Iodine (non-metal) also shows lustre!

🔨 Malleable (आघातवर्ध्यता)

Metals can be beaten into thin sheets without breaking. This property is called malleability. The metallic bonds allow layers to slide over each other.

📌 Most malleable metals: Gold (Au) → can be beaten into sheets 0.0002 mm thin! Silver (Ag) is 2nd most malleable.
🖼️ Aluminium foil used in kitchen — made using this property!

🧵 Ductile (तन्यता)

Metals can be drawn into thin wires without breaking. This property is called ductility.

📌 Most ductile metal: Gold (Au) → 1g of gold can be drawn into 2km long wire! Copper (Cu) and Aluminium (Al) wires used in electrical wiring.
⚡ Tungsten (W) is used to make bulb filament — highest melting point metal.

⚡ Conductor (विद्युत एवं ऊष्मा चालक)

Metals are good conductors of heat and electricity because of free electrons that move easily through the metal lattice.

📌 Best conductor of electricity: Silver (Ag) → then Copper (Cu) → then Gold (Au) → then Aluminium (Al)
⚠️ Exception: Bismuth (Bi) and Mercury (Hg) are poor conductors. Graphite (non-metal) is a conductor!

🔔 Sonorous (ध्वनिक)

Metals produce a ringing sound when struck. This property is called sonority. This happens because metals vibrate for a longer time due to their crystalline structure.

📌 Bells (घंटी) are made of metals — Bronze (Cu+Sn alloy) or Iron. School bells, church bells all use this property!
⚠️ Non-metals produce dull sound (thud) when hit.
