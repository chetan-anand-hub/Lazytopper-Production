💡 Core Concept KEY

Evolution (विकास) — Darwin's Natural Selection

Evolution is the change in inherited characteristics of populations over successive generations. Natural selection acts on variations — individuals with favorable traits survive and reproduce more, passing those traits to offspring.

📚 Darwin's Theory — Step by Step CLICK TO REVEAL

Click each step to explore Darwin's mechanism of evolution:

1
Overproduction (अत्यधिक प्रजनन)
All living organisms produce more offspring than can survive. A single fern can make millions of spores! This creates competition for limited resources like food, space, and mates.

🌱 Example: A single female salmon lays up to 5,000 eggs, but very few survive to adulthood.
2
Variation (विभिन्नता)
Individuals in a population are not identical — they differ in color, size, shape, behavior, etc. These variations arise due to mutations and sexual reproduction. Some variations are inherited.

🧬 Example: Some beetles are green, some are brown — a random mutation caused this difference.
3
Natural Selection (प्राकृतिक चयन)
Individuals with favorable variations survive longer and reproduce more — "Survival of the Fittest" (योग्यतम की उत्तरजीविता). The environment acts as a selector.

🐸 Example: Green frogs survive better in green grass (camouflage), while brown frogs get eaten by birds.
4
Inheritance (वंशागति)
The surviving individuals pass their favorable traits to offspring through genes. Over many generations, the favorable trait becomes more common in the population.

🔬 Favorable variation → More reproduction → More offspring inherit it → Trait becomes dominant
5
Speciation (प्रजातीकरण)
Over a very long time with different selection pressures, populations become so different they can no longer interbreed — a new species forms! Geographic isolation is a key factor.

⏳ Example: Galapagos finches — 13 species evolved from a single ancestor due to different food sources on each island.
