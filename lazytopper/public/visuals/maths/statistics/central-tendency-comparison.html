🎯 Choosing the Right Central Tendency Measure

📌 The Three Measures Click to Explore
📊 Mean
x̄ = Σfᵢxᵢ / Σfᵢ
Average of all values. Affected by extreme values.
✅ Use when: data is symmetric, no outliers
📍 Median
M = l + ((N/2 − cf)/f) × h
Middle value when data is arranged in order.
✅ Use when: data has extreme values/skewed
🏆 Mode
Z = l + ((f₁−f₀)/(2f₁−f₀−f₂)) × h
Most frequently occurring value.
✅ Use when: most common value is needed
🗺 When to Use Which? Decision Guide
🔵Mean — best when all values matter equally and data has no extreme outliers. e.g., average marks of a class, average rainfall.
🟢Median — best when data is skewed or has very high/low outliers. e.g., average income of a city (few very rich people skew Mean upward).
🟡Mode — best when the most common value is most useful. e.g., most popular shoe size, most sold product.
📐Empirical relation: Mode = 3 × Median − 2 × Mean (approx. for moderately skewed data).
