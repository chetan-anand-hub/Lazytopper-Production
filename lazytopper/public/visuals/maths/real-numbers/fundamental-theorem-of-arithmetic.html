📜 The Theorem (प्रमेय)

Fundamental Theorem of Arithmetic

Every composite number can be expressed (factorised) as a product of primes, and this factorisation is unique, apart from the order in which the prime factors occur.

n = p₁ᵃ¹ × p₂ᵃ² × p₃ᵃ³ × ... × pₙᵃⁿ

where p₁, p₂, ... are prime numbers and a₁, a₂, ... are positive integers

✨ Uniqueness (अद्वितीयता) Demo

No matter which path you take, the prime factors are always the same! Try dividing 36 differently:

Path A: 36 = 4×9
Path B: 36 = 6×6
Path C: 36 = 2×18
💡 Key Insight: All three paths give 2² × 3² = 36. The order may differ, but the prime factors and their powers are always identical. This is the uniqueness the theorem talks about!
📝 Worked Example — Step by Step

Find prime factorisation of 3960 and use it to find HCF & LCM with 1800.

HCF (म.स.)
360
= 2³ × 3² × 5
LCM (ल.स.)
19800
= 2³ × 3² × 5² × 11
✅ Verify: HCF × LCM = 360 × 19800 = 71,28,000 = 3960 × 1800 ✓
⚡ Quick Facts to Remember
HCF = Product of smallest powers of common prime factors
LCM = Product of greatest powers of all prime factors
HCF × LCM = Product of two numbers (for any two positive integers)
1 is neither prime nor composite | 2 is the only even prime number
