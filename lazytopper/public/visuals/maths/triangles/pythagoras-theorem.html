📐 Pythagoras Theorem

Statement of the Theorem

In a right-angled triangle, the square of the hypotenuse is equal to the sum of squares of the other two sides.

a² + b² = c²

where c = hypotenuse (longest side, opposite to 90°), a & b = other two sides

📝 Step-by-Step: Verify 3-4-5 Triangle

1

Identify the right angle. The side opposite to it is the hypotenuse. Here, c = 5.

2

Square all sides: a² = 3² = 9, b² = 4² = 16, c² = 5² = 25

3

Check: a² + b² = 9 + 16 = 25 = c² ✅ — Theorem verified!

4

Since a² + b² = c², this is a right-angled triangle (Converse also holds).

⭐ CBSE Board Exam Tips

  • Always identify the hypotenuse first — it's the longest side & opposite to 90°.
  • Memorise Pythagorean triplets: (3,4,5), (5,12,13), (8,15,17), (7,24,25).
  • Converse: If a²+b²=c², then the triangle is right-angled — used to check right angle.
  • In problems, if two sides are given, use c = √(a²+b²) or a = √(c²−b²).
  • This theorem is used in coordinate geometry (distance formula) too!
