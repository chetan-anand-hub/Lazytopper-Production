📐 What is a Compound Event? (यौगिक घटना)

A compound event involves two or more experiments performed together (e.g., tossing two coins, or throwing two dice). The sample space is built as ordered pairs — one outcome from each experiment.

Key Rule: If experiment 1 has m outcomes and experiment 2 has n outcomes, then the combined sample space has m × n outcomes.

🪙🪙 Two Coins n(S) = 2 × 2 = 4
S = {HH, HT, TH, TT}
🎲🎲 Two Dice n(S) = 6 × 6 = 36
Ordered pairs (d1, d2)
🪙🎲 Coin + Die n(S) = 2 × 6 = 12
Pairs like (H,1), (T,4)...
⚠️ Order Matters! (H,T) and (T,H) are different outcomes — both must be listed.

📝 Worked Example — Two Coins Tossed
Question: Two fair coins are tossed simultaneously. Find the probability of getting at least one Head.
1
List the Sample Space
▶

Write all possible outcomes for tossing 2 coins as ordered pairs (Coin1, Coin2):

S = {(H,H), (H,T), (T,H), (T,T)}

∴ n(S) = 4

2
Identify Event E = "at least one Head"
▶

"At least one Head" means 1 or more Heads — include all outcomes except (T,T).

Favourable: {(H,H), (H,T), (T,H)}

∴ n(E) = 3

3
Apply the Formula
▶

P(E) = n(E) / n(S) = 3 / 4

P(at least one Head) = 3/4

4
Verify using Complement
▶

P(not E) = P(no Head) = P(TT) = 1/4

Check: P(E) + P(not E) = 3/4 + 1/4 = 1 ✔

Alternatively: P(at least one H) = 1 − P(no H) = 1 − 1/4 = 3/4 ✔

💡 Key Rules for Compound Sample Spaces
🔹 Multiplication rule: Combined n(S) = n(S₁) × n(S₂) for two independent experiments.
🔹 Always write ordered pairs — (H,T) and (T,H) are different events.
🔹 "At least one" events are easier using complement: 1 − P(none).
🔹 Two dice → 36 outcomes; label them (d1, d2) and use a 6×6 grid to avoid missing any.
🔹 Sum of all probabilities in any sample space = 1.
📝 Exam Tip: For two-dice problems, draw a small 6×6 grid on rough paper. Mark the favourable cells and count — this is much faster than listing all 36 pairs.
