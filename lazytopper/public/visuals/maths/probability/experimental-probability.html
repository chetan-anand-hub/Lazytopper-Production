📐 Theoretical vs Experimental Probability

📘 Theoretical Probability

P(E) = n(E) / n(S)

Calculated using logical reasoning — assuming all outcomes are equally likely. No actual experiment is needed.

🔬 Experimental Probability

P(E) = Frequency / Total Trials

Calculated from actual observations of a repeated experiment. Also called relative frequency.

🔹 Experimental probability varies from trial to trial — it is not fixed like theoretical probability.
🔹 As the number of trials increases, experimental probability gets closer to theoretical probability (Law of Large Numbers).
🔹 Both always lie in the range 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.

💡 Key Facts for the Exam
🔹 Experimental probability formula: P(E) = Number of times event occurred ÷ Total number of trials.
🔹 It is also written as relative frequency in data-based questions.
🔹 Experimental probability may not equal theoretical probability — that's normal.
🔹 More trials = closer to theoretical. This is the Law of Large Numbers.
🔹 Class 10 CBSE: You mainly use theoretical probability; experimental appears in case-based data questions.
