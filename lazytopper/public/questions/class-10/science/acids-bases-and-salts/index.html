Acids, Bases and Salts — Class 10 Science, NCERT Chapter 2

Board weightage: high · 12 step-marked questions · 33 marks in total

Sour or bitter - decoded by H+, OH- and pH. Acids taste sour and turn blue litmus red; bases taste bitter and turn red litmus blue. In water an acid releases hydrogen ions, H+(aq) (shown as the hydronium ion H3O+), and a base releases hydroxide ions, OH-(aq) - and those ions explain everything else: why acid and base solutions conduct electricity, how they react with metals, carbonates and each other, and where a solution sits on the pH scale from 0 to 14. When an acid neutralises a base you get a salt plus water, and the chapter then follows one salt - common salt - as it becomes sodium hydroxide, bleaching powder, baking soda and washing soda, then closes with plaster of Paris (made from gypsum).

Formula sheet

  • Acid + Metal Acid + Metal -> Salt + Hydrogen gas
  • Carbonate / bicarbonate + Acid Metal carbonate / hydrogencarbonate + Acid -> Salt + Carbon dioxide + Water
  • Neutralisation Base + Acid -> Salt + Water . H+(aq) + OH-(aq) -> H2O(l)
  • Acids / bases in water HCl + H2O -> H3O+ + Cl- . NaOH -> Na+(aq) + OH-(aq)
  • pH scale 0 (very acidic) --- 7 (neutral) --- 14 (very alkaline) ; lower pH = more H+
  • Chlor-alkali 2NaCl(aq) + 2H2O(l) -> 2NaOH(aq) + Cl2(g) + H2(g)
  • Baking / washing soda NaHCO3 (baking soda) . Na2CO3.10H2O (washing soda)
  • Plaster of Paris CaSO4.(1/2)H2O + (3/2)H2O -> CaSO4.2H2O (gypsum)

Key definitions

Acid-base indicators
Substances that signal whether something is acidic or basic by changing colour - litmus, methyl orange and phenolphthalein are the common ones. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.33
Olfactory indicators
Indicators you smell rather than see - onion and clove oil lose their smell in one medium, so they can flag an acid or a base. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.18
Neutralisation reaction
Acid and base cancel each other out, producing a salt and water: Acid + Base -> Salt + Water. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.21
Alkali
A base that dissolves in water - like NaOH or KOH. All alkalis are bases, but not every base is an alkali. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.24
pH scale
A 0-14 number line for how acidic or basic a solution is: 0 very acidic, 7 neutral, 14 very alkaline. The higher the H3O+ concentration, the lower the pH. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.25
Strong and weak acids
Strength is about how fully an acid ionises, not how dilute it is - a strong acid produces more H+ ions than a weak acid of the same concentration. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.26
pH of salts
The salt inherits the stronger partner: strong+strong = neutral (7); strong acid + weak base = acidic (<7); strong base + weak acid = basic (>7). NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.29
Water of crystallisation
The water locked into a crystal - CuSO4.5H2O has five, Na2CO3.10H2O has ten; heating drives it off (blue copper sulphate turns white). NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.32
Dilution
Adding water lowers the ion concentration. Always add the acid to water slowly with stirring - the process is highly exothermic. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.24
Family of salts
Salts having the same positive or negative radicals are said to belong to a family. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.29
Universal indicator
The universal indicator shows different colours at different concentrations of hydrogen ions in a solution. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.25
Acid rain
When pH of rain water is less than 5.6, it is called acid rain. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.26
Chlor-alkali process
The process is called the chlor-alkali process because of the products formed– chlor for chlorine and alkali for sodium hydroxide. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.30
Bleaching powder
Bleaching powder is produced by the action of chlorine on dry slaked lime [Ca(OH)2]. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.30
Plaster of Paris
Plaster of Paris is a white powder and on mixing with water, it changes to gypsum once again giving a hard solid mass. NCERT 2026-27 Ch2 p.33

What the board actually asks

identify an acid or a base with indicators (blue/red litmus, methyl orange, phenolphthalein, and olfactory indicators); write and balance the reactions of acids and bases - with metals (Salt + Hydrogen), with metal carbonates/hydrogencarbonates (Salt + Carbon dioxide + Water), acid-base neutralisation (Salt + Water), and metal-oxide / non-metal-oxide reactions; explain what happens to an acid or a base in water (H+(aq)/H3O+ and OH-(aq) ions) and why their solutions conduct electricity; use the pH scale (0-14) and its everyday importance (body pH, soil, digestion and antacids, tooth decay, acid rain); classify salts by family and by the pH of the salt; and give the preparation, properties and uses of the chlor-alkali products, washing soda, baking soda, bleaching powder and plaster of Paris (water of crystallisation). Most marks sit in writing the correct balanced reaction and in the pH reasoning.

Practice questions with worked solutions

Q1. 1 mark Classification of Acids

Which one of the following is NOT an organic acid?

  1. Ethanoic acid
  2. Formic acid
  3. Citric acid
  4. Carbonic acid

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Carbonic acid (H₂CO₃) is a mineral (inorganic) acid formed when CO₂ dissolves in water; ethanoic, formic and citric acids occur in plants/animals and are organic acids.

Answer. Carbonic acid

Q2. 2 marks pH in Everyday Life

The soil in a field is found to be alkaline. Suggest one way to reduce its alkaline nature and justify your suggestion.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Add an acidic material to the soil, for example an acidic salt like ammonium nitrate (or well-decayed organic manure/compost).
  2. [1 mark] Being acidic, it neutralises the excess alkali present in the soil and lowers the pH towards the neutral range suitable for crops.

Answer. Add an acidic salt (e.g. ammonium nitrate) to neutralise the excess base.

Q3. 3 marks Nature of Oxides

Using magnesium ribbon and sulphur powder, describe an activity to show that metal oxides are basic and oxides of non-metals are acidic in nature.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Burn magnesium ribbon in air to form white magnesium oxide; dissolve the ash in water — the solution turns red litmus blue, showing MgO is a basic oxide. 2Mg + O₂ → 2MgO; MgO + H₂O → Mg(OH)₂.
  2. [1 mark] Burn sulphur in a spatula to form sulphur dioxide; pass it into water — the solution turns blue litmus red, showing SO₂ is an acidic oxide. S + O₂ → SO₂; SO₂ + H₂O → H₂SO₃.
  3. [1 mark] Conclusion: metal oxides (MgO) are basic while non-metal oxides (SO₂) are acidic in nature.

Answer. MgO turns red litmus blue (basic); SO₂ turns blue litmus red (acidic).

Q4. 4 marks HCl Formation and Acidic Property of Wet HCl Gas

From the chlor-alkali context (X = Cl2, Y = H2): write a balanced chemical reaction that takes place when 'X' and 'Y' react with each other. The product so produced will turn blue litmus red only when wet — why?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] When hydrogen (Y) and chlorine (X) react, they combine to form hydrogen chloride gas: H2(g) + Cl2(g) → 2 HCl(g).
  2. [1 mark] Dry HCl gas does NOT turn dry blue litmus red, because in the absence of water it cannot release hydrogen ions.
  3. [1 mark] In the presence of moisture (wet litmus), HCl dissolves in the water and dissociates: HCl(g) + H2O → H3O+ + Cl−.
  4. [1 mark] The H+ (hydronium) ions so produced give the solution its acidic character, so only wet HCl turns blue litmus red.

Answer. H2 + Cl2 → 2 HCl; wet HCl furnishes H+ ions ⟹ acidic ⟹ turns blue litmus red.

Q5. 5 marks General

From the 2023 CBSE board paper

(A) (i) Draw the structure of the following compounds : (a) Butanoic acid (b) Chloropentane (ii) How are structure (i) and structure (ii) given below related to one another ? Give reason to justify your answer. Draw one more possible structure for above case. (iii) Differentiate between saturated and unsaturated carbon compounds on the basis of their general formula. OR (B) (i) What happens when a small piece of sodium is dropped in ethanol ? Write the equation for this reaction. (ii) Why is glacial acetic acid called so ? (iii) What happens when ethanol is heated at 443 K in the presence of conc. H2SO4 ? Write the role of conc. H2SO4 in this case. (iv) Write an equation showing saponification.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] (B)(i) When a small piece of sodium is dropped in ethanol, hydrogen gas is evolved (brisk effervescence) and sodium ethoxide is formed.
  2. [0.5 mark] (i) Balanced equation: 2CH₃CH₂OH + 2Na → 2CH₃CH₂ONa + H₂↑
  3. [1 mark] (ii) Glacial acetic acid: pure (100%) ethanoic acid freezes to an ice-like solid just below room temperature (melting point ≈ 290 K), so it is called "glacial" acetic acid.
  4. [1 mark] (iii) On heating ethanol at 443 K with conc. H₂SO₄, ethene is formed: CH₃CH₂OH → CH₂=CH₂ + H₂O; here conc. H₂SO₄ acts as a dehydrating agent (removes water).
  5. [1.5 mark] (iv) Saponification (ester + alkali → soap/salt + alcohol): CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + C₂H₅OH.

Answer. (B)(i) H₂ gas evolved and sodium ethoxide formed: 2CH₃CH₂OH + 2Na → 2CH₃CH₂ONa + H₂; (ii) pure ethanoic acid freezes like ice below ~290 K; (iii) ethene formed, conc. H₂SO₄ is a dehydrating agent; (iv) CH₃COOC₂H₅ + NaOH → CH₃COONa + C₂H₅OH.

Q6. 1 mark Mineral Acids

Aqua regia, which can dissolve gold, is a mixture of concentrated:

  1. 3 parts HCl and 1 part HNO₃
  2. 1 part HCl and 3 parts HNO₃
  3. Equal volumes of HNO₃ and H₂SO₄
  4. H₂SO₄ only

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Aqua regia is a freshly prepared mixture of concentrated hydrochloric acid and concentrated nitric acid in the ratio 3 : 1, and it can dissolve even noble metals like gold and platinum.

Answer. 3 parts HCl and 1 part HNO₃

Q7. 2 marks Nature of Oxides

Explain why oxides of metals are basic in nature while those of non-metals are acidic.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Metal oxides react with water to give hydroxides (bases), e.g. Na₂O + H₂O → 2NaOH; hence they are basic in nature.
  2. [1 mark] Non-metal oxides react with water to give acids, e.g. CO₂ + H₂O → H₂CO₃; hence they are acidic in nature.

Answer. Metal oxides give bases with water; non-metal oxides give acids with water.

Q8. 3 marks Acids with Carbonates

Describe an activity to illustrate that the reaction of a metal carbonate or hydrogencarbonate with an acid produces carbon dioxide. Write the equations and name two forms in which calcium carbonate occurs in nature.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Take marble chips in a flask, add dilute HCl and pass the evolved gas through lime water — the lime water turns milky, showing CO₂ is produced: CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂.
  2. [1 mark] Repeating with sodium hydrogencarbonate gives the same result: NaHCO₃ + HCl → NaCl + H₂O + CO₂; hence carbonates and hydrogencarbonates give CO₂ with acids.
  3. [1 mark] Two natural forms of calcium carbonate: marble and chalk (limestone).

Answer. CO₂ turns lime water milky; CaCO₃ occurs as marble and chalk/limestone.

Q9. 4 marks Indicators

A visually-impaired student in the chemistry lab needs to tell an acid from a base without seeing any colour. Her teacher gives her chopped onion and a bottle of vanilla essence to use as detectors. (i) What name is given to such smell-based detectors? (ii) Name one more substance that works the same way. (iii) How does the smell of onion change when a base is added to it? (iv) Why are these especially useful for a visually-impaired student?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] (i) Substances whose ODOUR changes in acidic/basic media are called olfactory indicators.
  2. [1 mark] (ii) Vanilla essence (or clove oil) is another olfactory indicator.
  3. [1 mark] (iii) With a base, the sharp onion smell is lost/not detected; with an acid the smell persists — so the change of smell signals a base.
  4. [1 mark] (iv) They work by smell not colour, so a visually-impaired person can still distinguish an acid from a base.

Answer. (i) Olfactory indicators (ii) Clove/vanilla (iii) Base removes onion smell (iv) smell-based, no colour needed.

Q10. 5 marks Strong and Weak Acids

Equal volumes of dilute hydrochloric acid (HCl) and dilute acetic acid (CH₃COOH) of the same concentration are taken. (i) Classify each as strong or weak and show how each behaves in water. (ii) Which will react more vigorously with magnesium ribbon? Write the equations. (iii) In which will a bulb-conductivity test glow brighter, and why?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] HCl is a strong acid — it ionises almost completely in water: HCl(aq) + H₂O(l) → H₃O⁺(aq) + Cl⁻(aq), giving a high concentration of H₃O⁺.
  2. [1 mark] CH₃COOH is a weak acid — it ionises only partially: CH₃COOH(aq) + H₂O(l) ⇌ H₃O⁺(aq) + CH₃COO⁻(aq), so fewer H₃O⁺ ions are present.
  3. [1 mark] Magnesium reacts more vigorously with HCl (more H⁺ available): Mg(s) + 2HCl(aq) → MgCl₂(aq) + H₂(g)↑.
  4. [1 mark] With acetic acid the reaction is slower: Mg(s) + 2CH₃COOH(aq) → (CH₃COO)₂Mg(aq) + H₂(g)↑.
  5. [1 mark] The bulb glows brighter in HCl because more ions (higher H₃O⁺) means better electrical conductivity; acetic acid, being weak, has fewer ions so the bulb glows dim.

Answer. HCl strong (fully ionised, brighter bulb, vigorous with Mg); CH₃COOH weak (partly ionised, dim bulb, slower).

Q11. 1 mark Acids in Nature

Which one of the following does NOT inject an acidic liquid into the skin through its sting?

  1. Honey bee
  2. Ant
  3. Wasp
  4. Nettle leaf hair

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] A honey bee, an ant and a nettle-leaf hair inject methanoic (formic) acid, but a wasp injects an alkaline liquid; hence a wasp sting is not treated with a base.

Answer. Wasp

Q12. 2 marks Common Compounds

What is the difference between slaked lime and lime water?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Slaked lime is the solid compound calcium hydroxide, Ca(OH)₂, obtained by adding water to quicklime.
  2. [1 mark] Lime water is a clear, saturated solution of calcium hydroxide in water and is used to test for CO₂.

Answer. Slaked lime = solid Ca(OH)₂; lime water = its clear aqueous solution.

Common mistakes — where marks are lost

  • Thinking 'salt' means only sodium chloride. In chemistry a salt is any product of an acid neutralising a base - ZnSO4, CaCl2 and Na2CO3 are all salts.
  • Forgetting that H+ ions form only in water, so dry HCl gas does NOT turn dry litmus red - the separation of H+ from the HCl molecule needs water.
  • Confusing strength with concentration. A strong acid ionises more fully than a weak acid at the same concentration; strong / weak is about the number of H+ ions produced, not how dilute the solution is.
  • Adding water to a concentrated acid. Always add the acid to water slowly with stirring - dissolving an acid or base is highly exothermic and the mixture can splash out.
  • Mixing up baking soda and washing soda - baking soda is NaHCO3 (sodium hydrogencarbonate) while washing soda is Na2CO3.10H2O (hydrated sodium carbonate).
  • Writing plaster of Paris with a whole water molecule. It is calcium sulphate hemihydrate, CaSO4.(1/2)H2O - two formula units share one water molecule.
