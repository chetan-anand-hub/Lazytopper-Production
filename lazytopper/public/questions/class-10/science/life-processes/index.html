Life Processes — Class 10 Science, NCERT Chapter 5

Board weightage: high · 12 step-marked questions · 33 marks in total

Four processes keep a living body from falling apart - nutrition, respiration, transportation, excretion. Every living body must take in food and energy, break it down to power its cells, move materials to where they are needed, and clear out the waste. In Class 10 these four life processes are examined mostly through labelled diagrams and stage-by-stage explanations, so most marks reward the exact NCERT terms and correctly drawn, correctly labelled structures.

Formula sheet

  • Photosynthesis (standard equation) 6CO2 + 6H2O --(sunlight, chlorophyll)--> C6H12O6 + 6O2 (standard CBSE equation - NCERT states the three events, not this balanced line)
  • Glucose breakdown Glucose (6C) to Pyruvate (3C) in cytoplasm; then aerobic to CO2 + H2O + energy, or anaerobic - yeast to ethanol + CO2, muscle to lactic acid
  • ATP ATP is the energy currency of the cell; breaking its terminal phosphate bond releases about 30.5 kJ/mol
  • Heart chambers 4 chambers - left/right atrium + left/right ventricle. Fish 2-chambered, amphibians/many reptiles 3-chambered, birds & mammals 4-chambered
  • Blood pressure Systolic about 120 mm Hg, diastolic about 80 mm Hg (measured with a sphygmomanometer)
  • Alveolar surface Spread out, the alveolar surface would cover about 80 m2 - maximising gas exchange
  • Kidney filtrate About 180 L of initial filtrate a day, but only 1-2 L excreted - the rest is re-absorbed

Key definitions

Life processes
The basic functions - nutrition, respiration, transportation, excretion - that every living body runs continuously to stay organised and alive. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.80
Nutrition
Taking in food from outside to supply the body with energy and raw materials. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.80
Autotrophic nutrition (photosynthesis)
Green plants and some bacteria make their own food from CO2 and water using sunlight and chlorophyll. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.81
Respiration
Breaking down glucose (usually with oxygen) inside cells to release energy for life activities. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.80
Transportation
The system (blood in animals, vascular tissue in plants) that moves food, oxygen and wastes between the parts that supply them and the parts that need them. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.80
Excretion
Getting rid of the poisonous nitrogenous wastes (like urea) that the body's chemical reactions produce. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.96
Stomata
...stomata ... which are tiny pores present on the surface of the leaves. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.83
Aerobic respiration
Since this process takes place in the presence of air (oxygen), it is called aerobic respiration. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.88
Anaerobic respiration
Since this process takes place in the absence of air (oxygen), it is called anaerobic respiration. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.87
Alveoli
balloon-like structures which are called alveoli (singular - alveolus). The alveoli provide a surface where the exchange of gases can take place. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.90
Haemoglobin
In human beings, the respiratory pigment is haemoglobin which has a very high affinity for oxygen. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.90
Xylem
One, the xylem moves water and minerals obtained from the soil. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.94
Phloem
The other, phloem transports products of photosynthesis from the leaves where they are synthesised to other parts of the plant. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.94
Nephrons
Each kidney has large numbers of these filtration units called nephrons packed close together. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.97

What the board actually asks

label-and-explain diagrams (the human digestive system, heart, nephron and respiratory system), the four life processes traced stage-by-stage, comparison tables (xylem vs phloem, aerobic vs anaerobic, arteries vs veins), and "why is it necessary" reasoning - one of the highest-weight Biology chapters.

Q1. 1 mark Nutrition

From the 2008 CBSE board paper

Define the term 'translocation' as used in the transport of substances in plants.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Translocation is the transport of the soluble products of photosynthesis (mainly sucrose) from the leaves to other parts of the plant through the phloem.

Answer. Transport of food from leaves to other plant parts through phloem.

Q2. 2 marks Respiration

From the 2017 CBSE board paper

Why do we get cramps in our muscles after doing vigorous exercise?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] During vigorous exercise the oxygen supply to the muscle cells becomes insufficient, so they respire anaerobically.
  2. [1 mark] This partial breakdown of glucose produces lactic acid; its accumulation in the muscles causes pain and cramps.

Answer. Anaerobic respiration in oxygen-starved muscles forms lactic acid, whose build-up causes cramps.

Q3. 3 marks Excretion

From the 2016 CBSE board paper

What is excretion? Name any three parts of the human body, other than the kidneys, that help in removing wastes and state the waste each removes.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Excretion is the biological process of removing harmful metabolic waste products from the body.
  2. [1 mark] The lungs remove gaseous waste carbon dioxide and water vapour; the skin removes excess water and salts as sweat.
  3. [1 mark] The liver converts toxic ammonia into urea and removes bile pigments, which are excreted along with bile.

Answer. Excretion removes metabolic wastes; lungs (CO2), skin (sweat), liver (urea/bile pigments) also excrete.

Q6. 1 mark Nutrition

From the 2011 CBSE board paper

What is peristalsis?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Peristalsis is the wave-like rhythmic contraction and relaxation of the muscles of the alimentary canal wall that moves food forward along the gut.

Answer. Rhythmic muscular contractions that push food along the gut.

Q7. 2 marks Nutrition

From the 2016 CBSE board paper

Which digestive secretion contains no enzyme yet is important for digestion? Explain its role.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Bile juice, secreted by the liver, contains no digestive enzyme but is rich in bile salts.
  2. [1 mark] These bile salts emulsify fats — breaking large fat droplets into fine droplets — increasing their surface area so that the enzyme lipase can digest them efficiently.

Answer. Bile juice; its bile salts emulsify fats so lipase can act on them.

Q8. 3 marks Nutrition

From the 2014 CBSE board paper

How is nutrition in Amoeba different from that in Paramecium?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] In Amoeba, which has no fixed shape, food is captured anywhere on the surface by temporary finger-like pseudopodia that surround the particle to form a food vacuole (phagocytosis).
  2. [1 mark] In Paramecium, which has a definite shape, food cannot be taken in anywhere; it is ingested at a specific spot called the oral groove.
  3. [1 mark] The food is swept towards this oral groove by the coordinated beating of cilia covering the body, after which it is enclosed in a food vacuole and digested.

Answer. Amoeba: pseudopodia engulf food anywhere; Paramecium: cilia sweep food to a fixed oral groove.

Q11. 1 mark Respiration

From the 2008 CBSE board paper

Why is respiration considered an exothermic process?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] During respiration, glucose is oxidised in the presence of oxygen and energy is released; because energy is given out, respiration is an exothermic process.

Answer. It releases energy on oxidation of glucose.

Q12. 2 marks Transportation

From the 2016 CBSE board paper

Distinguish between an auricle and a ventricle of the human heart on the basis of their walls and the direction in which they pump blood.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Auricles (atria) are the upper, thin-walled chambers that receive blood returning through veins and push it into the ventricles.
  2. [1 mark] Ventricles are the lower, thick-walled muscular chambers that pump blood out of the heart into the arteries.

Answer. Auricles: thin-walled, receive blood, pass to ventricles. Ventricles: thick-walled, pump blood into arteries.

Common mistakes — where marks are lost

  • Writing "food pipe" instead of oesophagus, or "wind pipe / air sacs" instead of trachea / alveoli - CBSE demands the exact technical term.
  • Confusing xylem and phloem: xylem = water + minerals, roots upward (one-way); phloem = food, both directions. Reversing them loses the whole mark.
  • Mislabelling heart chambers - putting oxygenated blood on the right, or swapping atria and ventricles. Remember: LEFT side = oxygen-rich, and ventricles have thicker walls than atria.
  • Saying anaerobic respiration in muscles gives ethanol - in human muscle cells pyruvate becomes lactic acid (ethanol + CO2 is only in yeast/fermentation).
  • Describing filtration only and forgetting that in the nephron useful substances (glucose, amino acids, salts, water) are RE-ABSORBED.
