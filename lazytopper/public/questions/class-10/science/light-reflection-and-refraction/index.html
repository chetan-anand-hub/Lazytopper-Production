Light – Reflection and Refraction — Class 10 Science, NCERT Chapter 9

Board weightage: high · 12 step-marked questions · 33 marks in total

What the board actually asks

ray diagrams for mirrors & lenses, the mirror/lens formula numericals (with sign convention), refractive index, and power of a lens — these carry almost every mark here.

Definitions to know

Laws of reflection of light
the ray comes in and bounces off at the same angle to the normal (∠ i = ∠ r), and the incident ray, normal and reflected ray are all flat in one plane.
Laws of refraction of light (Snell's law)
sin i / sin r stays the same for a given colour of light and a given pair of media — that constant is the refractive index.
Absolute refractive index
measured against vacuum/air, n_m = Speed of light in air / Speed of light in the medium = c / v (NCERT p.149). A larger n means light slows more — the medium is optically denser.
Principal focus & focal length of a spherical mirror
parallel rays converge at F (concave) — focal length f = pole-to-F distance, and R = 2f.
Magnification produced by a spherical mirror
m = h' / h = -v / u. A negative m means a real, inverted image; positive means virtual, erect.
Power of a lens & the dioptre
P = 1 / f (metres). Convex lens → positive power, concave → negative.
Pole
The centre of the reflecting surface of a spherical mirror is a point called the pole."
Centre of curvature
…This point is called the centre of curvature of the spherical mirror."
Radius of curvature
The radius of the sphere of which the reflecting surface of a spherical mirror forms a part, is called the radius of curvature of the mirror."
Principal axis
[A straight line passing through the pole and the centre of curvature…] This line is called the principal axis."
Aperture
The diameter of the reflecting surface of spherical mirror is called its aperture."
R = 2f
…the radius of curvature is found to be equal to twice the focal length. We put this as R = 2f."
Lens
A transparent material bound by two surfaces, of which one or both surfaces are spherical, forms a lens."
Optical centre
The central point of a lens is its optical centre. … A ray of light through the optical centre of a lens passes without suffering any deviation." (p.150–151)

Formula strip

  • Mirror formula 1 / v + 1 / u = 1 / f
  • Lens formula 1 / v - 1 / u = 1 / f
  • Magnification mirror m=-v / u · lens m=v / u
  • Refractive index n = c / v = sin i / sin r
  • Apparent depth n = real depth / apparent depth
  • Power of lens P = 1 / f (m) (dioptre, D)
  • Mirror relation R = 2f

Where marks are lost

  • ✕Sign convention errors — the #1 lost mark. Object distance u is always negative; concave f negative, convex f positive.
  • ✕Mixing mirror and lens magnification — mirror m=-v/u, lens m=+v/u.
  • ✕Forgetting to convert to metres for power — P=1/f needs f in metres, not cm.
  • ✕Not stating the nature — a numerical answer needs "real/virtual, erect/inverted, magnified/diminished" for full marks.

Step-marked board questions

Every solution below is broken into the steps CBSE awards marks for, so you can see where each mark is earned.

Q1. 1 mark Virtual Images

Explain why the image of the coin is a virtual image.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The light rays do not actually meet, so the image is virtual.

Answer. Because the rays do not actually meet.

Q2. 2 marks Refraction and Speed of Light in Media

Explain why this wave process (refraction) occurs as light passes from water into air.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Air is less dense than water.
  2. [1 mark] Light changes speed (is faster in air), so it changes direction / refracts.

Answer. Air is less dense, so light speeds up and changes direction (refracts).

Q3. 3 marks Lens Formula

The focal length f of a convex lens is 20 cm. The lens forms an image of an object at a distance v = 50 cm. Using the lens equation 1/f = 1/v + 1/u, calculate the distance u of the object from the lens.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Substitute into the lens equation: 1/20 = 1/50 + 1/u.
  2. [1 mark] Rearrange: 1/u = 1/20 − 1/50 = 3/100.
  3. [1 mark] Solve: u = 100/3 ≈ 33 cm.

Answer. u ≈ 33 cm

Q4. 4 marks Spherical Mirrors

Read the following passage and answer the following questions. A student wants to project the image of a candle flame on the walls of the school laboratory by using a mirror. (i) Which type of mirror should he use and why? (a) Convex mirror, it forms virtual image (b) Concave mirror, it forms virtual image (c) Concave mirror, it forms real image (d) Convex mirror, it forms real image (ii) To get the diminished image of the candle flame, the object must be placed at: (a) infinity (b) at C (c) between F and C (d) at F (iii) A concave mirror produces a 10 cm long virtual image of an object of 2 cm height. What is the magnification produced?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] (i) Option (c): a concave mirror, because only a real image (formed when the object is beyond the focus of a concave mirror) can be received/projected on a wall; a convex mirror forms only virtual images.
  2. [1 mark] (ii) Option (a): with the object at infinity, the concave mirror forms the image at the focus, highly diminished (point-sized) - among the given positions this is the one giving a diminished image.
  3. [1 mark] (iii) Magnification m = height of image / height of object = h'/h; the image is virtual and erect, so h' = +10 cm with h = 2 cm.
  4. [1 mark] m = +10/2 = +5. The magnification is +5 (positive sign shows the image is virtual and erect).

Answer. (i) Concave mirror, it forms real image; (ii) infinity; (iii) m = +5.

Q5. 5 marks Convex Lens

From the 2023 CBSE board paper

(a) An object is placed in front of a convex lens of focal length f. If the distance of the object from the lens is 2f, draw a ray diagram to show the formation of the image. Write the value of magnification in this case. (b) A student has focussed the image of a candle flame on a white screen using a convex lens. The situation is as given below : Length of the flame = 2 cm Focal length of the lens = 12 cm Distance of the flame from the lens = 16 cm If the flame is perpendicular to the principal axis of the lens, calculate the values of the following : (i) Distance of the image from the lens (ii) Length of the image formed

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] (a) Ray diagram: When the object is placed at 2F (distance 2f) in front of a convex lens, a real, inverted image of the same size forms at 2F on the other side — draw one ray parallel to the axis refracting through F and one ray through the optical centre, meeting to locate the image.
  2. [1 mark] (a) Magnification: Since the image is the same size as the object, magnification m = −1 (image real and inverted).
  3. [1 mark] (b) Setup: Given object distance u = −16 cm and focal length f = +12 cm; apply the lens formula 1/v − 1/u = 1/f.
  4. [1 mark] (b)(i) Image distance: 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/12 + 1/(−16) = (4 − 3)/48 = 1/48, so v = +48 cm — the image forms 48 cm from the lens on the far side.
  5. [1 mark] (b)(ii) Image length: magnification m = v/u = 48/(−16) = −3, so image length = m × object length = −3 × 2 cm = −6 cm; the image is 6 cm long, real and inverted.

Answer. (a) Magnification m = −1 (image same size, real and inverted). (b)(i) v = +48 cm. (b)(ii) Image length = 6 cm (real, inverted).

Q6. 1 mark Focal Length of a Lens

Describe the focal length of a lens.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The focal length is the distance from the centre of the lens to the focal point.

Answer. The distance from the centre of the lens to its focal point.

Q7. 2 marks Lenses in Telescopes and Microscopes

Explain why convex lenses are used in telescopes and microscopes.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Convex lenses magnify / enlarge images.
  2. [1 mark] They enlarge distant objects (telescope) or small objects (microscope).

Answer. They magnify/enlarge images — of distant objects (telescope) and small objects (microscope).

Q8. 3 marks Nature of Image Formed by Convex Lens

A convex lens of focal length 20 cm forms an image of an object placed at u ≈ 33 cm, giving an image at v = 50 cm. Describe the nature of the image produced.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The image is real.
  2. [1 mark] The image is inverted (upside down).
  3. [1 mark] The image is magnified (larger than the object).

Answer. Real, inverted and magnified image.

Q9. 4 marks Image Formation by Convex Lens

A student focuses the image of a candle flame, placed at about 2 m from a convex lens of focal length 10 cm, on a screen. After that he moves the flame gradually towards the lens and each time focuses its image on the screen. (i) In which direction does he move the lens to focus the flame on the screen? (ii) What happens to the size of the image of the flame formed on the screen? (iii) What difference is seen in the intensity (brightness) of the image of the flame on the screen? (iv) What is seen on the screen when the flame is very close (at about 5 cm) to the lens?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] (i) As the flame comes closer, the image distance v increases (1/v = 1/f + 1/u), so he moves the lens AWAY from the screen, i.e. towards the flame, to keep the image focused.
  2. [1 mark] (ii) The size of the image on the screen INCREASES, since magnification |m| = |v/u| grows as u decreases and v increases.
  3. [1 mark] (iii) The intensity (brightness) of the image DECREASES, because the same amount of light is now spread over a larger image.
  4. [1 mark] (iv) At about 5 cm the flame is within the focal length (5 cm < 10 cm); no real image can form - only a blurred, diffuse patch of light is seen on the screen (the image formed is virtual and cannot be taken on a screen).

Answer. (i) Away from the screen (towards the flame); (ii) image size increases; (iii) brightness decreases; (iv) no image - only a diffuse patch of light (virtual image cannot be caught on screen).

Q10. 5 marks General

From the 2023 CBSE board paper

(a) Name the type of mirror that should be used to obtain the following types of images : (i) A magnified and virtual image of an object (ii) A diminished and virtual image of an object (b) Draw labelled ray diagrams to justify your answers in each case mentioned above. (c) Which of these mirrors can also be used to obtain a magnified and real image of an object ? State the position of the object in this case.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] (a)(i) A magnified and virtual image is obtained with a concave (converging) mirror when the object is placed between the pole and the focus.
  2. [1 mark] (a)(ii) A diminished and virtual image is always obtained with a convex (diverging) mirror.
  3. [1 mark] (b) Diagram: Ray diagram for a concave mirror with the object between P and F — an erect, magnified, virtual image forms behind the mirror.
  4. [1 mark] (b) Diagram: Ray diagram for a convex mirror — an erect, diminished, virtual image forms behind the mirror between P and F.
  5. [1 mark] (c) A concave mirror can also give a magnified and real image; this happens when the object is placed between the focus (F) and the centre of curvature (C).

Answer. (a)(i) Concave mirror; (a)(ii) Convex mirror. (c) Concave mirror, with the object placed between F and C.

Q11. 1 mark Magnification of a Lens

The height of an object A, H_A is 4 cm. The height of the image of object A, H_B is also 4 cm. Calculate the magnification M of the lens.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Magnification M = height of image / height of object = H_B / H_A = 4 cm / 4 cm = 1.

Answer. M = 1

Q12. 2 marks Lens Formula — Image Distance

Using the lens equation and your value of f (= 15 cm) calculate the image distance for observation 6 (object distance u = −90 cm).

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Substitution: 1/15 = 1/(−90) + 1/v.
  2. [1 mark] Rearrange and calculate: 90/7 = 12.9 cm. (If answer = 12.9 cm, award full 2 marks.)

Answer. v = 12.9 cm
