Coordinate Geometry — Class 10 Mathematics, NCERT Chapter 7

Board weightage: medium · 12 step-marked questions · 33 marks in total

Geometry you can compute. Coordinate geometry turns points into number-pairs so that lengths and dividing-points become arithmetic. Two tools do all the work: the <b>Distance Formula</b> gives the length between any two points P(x_1, y_1) and Q(x_2, y_2), and the <b>Section Formula</b> gives the point that splits the segment AB internally in a chosen ratio m_1 : m_2 (with the mid-point as the special 1:1 case). Everything else here — proving a triangle's type, testing whether three points are collinear, finding a missing vertex — is one of these two formulas applied.

Formula sheet

  • Distance formula PQ=root((x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2)
  • Distance from origin OP=root(x^2+y^2)
  • Section formula (internal, m_1:m_2) left(m_1x_2+m_2x_1 / m_1+m_2, m_1y_2+m_2y_1 / m_1+m_2right)
  • Mid-point left(x_1+x_2 / 2, y_1+y_2 / 2right)

Key definitions

Distance formula
The straight-line distance between two points: square the gap in x, square the gap in y, add them, take the positive square root — PQ=root((x_2-x_1)^2+(y_2-y_1)^2). The order of subtraction doesn't matter because each difference is squared. NCERT 2026-27 Ch7 p.102
Section formula (internal)
Where a point lands when it splits the segment from A to B internally in the ratio m_1 : m_2 — a weighted average of the endpoints. Watch the cross-pattern: the weight m_1 (the PA part) multiplies x_2, the coordinate of B. NCERT 2026-27 Ch7 p.107
Mid-point formula
Just average the two x's and the two y's — left(x_1+x_2 / 2, y_1+y_2 / 2right). It is the section formula at ratio 1:1. NCERT 2026-27 Ch7 p.107 (Special Case)
Abscissa (x-coordinate)
The distance of a point from the y-axis is called its x-coordinate, or abscissa. NCERT 2026-27 Ch7 p.99
Ordinate (y-coordinate)
The distance of a point from the x-axis is called its y-coordinate, or ordinate. NCERT 2026-27 Ch7 p.99
Distance from the origin
In particular, the distance of a point P(x, y) from the origin O(0, 0) is given by OP = √(x² + y²). NCERT 2026-27 Ch7 p.102 (Remark 1)
Section formula in k : 1 form
If the ratio in which P divides AB is k : 1, then the coordinates of the point P will be ((kx₂ + x₁)/(k + 1), (ky₂ + y₁)/(k + 1)). NCERT 2026-27 Ch7 p.107

What the board actually asks

the Distance Formula (length of a side, type of triangle or quadrilateral, collinearity, equidistant points) and the Section Formula for internal division — including the mid-point and the points of trisection. In the rationalised 2026-27 chapter these two formulas carry essentially every mark; area-of-a-triangle by coordinates has been removed from the syllabus.

Q1. 1 mark Distance Formula

If the point (x, 4) lies on a circle whose centre is at the origin and radius is 5, then x =

  1. ±5
  2. ±3
  3. 0
  4. ±4

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The point (x, 4) is at distance 5 from O(0, 0): x² + 4² = 5² ⇒ x² = 25 − 16 = 9 ⇒ x = ±3.

Answer. x = ±3.

Q2. 2 marks Distance Formula

If A(5, p), B(1, 5), C(2, 1) and D(6, 2) are the vertices of a square ABCD, find the value of p.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Sides AB and AD are equal ⇒ AB² = AD²: (5−1)² + (p−5)² = (5−6)² + (p−2)², i.e. 16 + (p−5)² = 1 + (p−2)².
  2. [1 mark] Expand and simplify: 41 − 10p = 5 − 4p ⇒ 6p = 36 ⇒ p = 6.

Answer. p = 6.

Q3. 3 marks Distance Formula

Prove that the distance between the points A(a cos θ + b sin θ, 0) and B(0, a sin θ − b cos θ) is √(a² + b²).

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Apply the distance formula: AB² = (a cos θ + b sin θ)² + (a sin θ − b cos θ)².
  2. [1 mark] Expand both squares: a²cos²θ + 2ab sinθcosθ + b²sin²θ + a²sin²θ − 2ab sinθcosθ + b²cos²θ; the ±2ab sinθcosθ terms cancel.
  3. [1 mark] Group and use sin²θ + cos²θ = 1: a²(cos²θ + sin²θ) + b²(sin²θ + cos²θ) = a² + b². Therefore AB = √(a² + b²).

Answer. AB = √(a² + b²).

Q4. 4 marks Distance Formula

In a classroom whose floor is marked as a coordinate grid, three students sit at Meera A(1, 2), Rohit B(4, 6) and Neha C(8, 9). (i) Find the distance AB between Meera and Rohit. (ii) Find the distance BC between Rohit and Neha. (iii) Find the distance AC between Meera and Neha. (iv) Using the distances, decide whether the three students are seated in a straight line.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] AB = √[(4−1)² + (6−2)²] = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5 units.
  2. [1 mark] BC = √[(8−4)² + (9−6)²] = √(16 + 9) = √25 = 5 units.
  3. [1 mark] AC = √[(8−1)² + (9−2)²] = √(49 + 49) = √98 = 7√2 ≈ 9.90 units.
  4. [1 mark] AB + BC = 5 + 5 = 10 ≠ AC (7√2 ≈ 9.90), so the three points are NOT collinear — they do not sit in a straight line.

Answer. AB = 5, BC = 5, AC = 7√2; not collinear.

Q5. 5 marks Section Formula

Three consecutive vertices of a parallelogram ABCD taken in order are A(1, 2), B(4, 3) and C(6, 6). Using the property that the diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other, find the coordinates of the fourth vertex D.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Property: diagonals of a parallelogram bisect each other ⇒ midpoint of diagonal AC = midpoint of diagonal BD.
  2. [1 mark] Midpoint of AC = ((1+6)/2, (2+6)/2) = (7/2, 4).
  3. [1 mark] Let D = (x, y). Midpoint of BD = ((4+x)/2, (3+y)/2).
  4. [1 mark] Equate x: (4+x)/2 = 7/2 ⇒ x = 3. Equate y: (3+y)/2 = 4 ⇒ y = 5.
  5. [1 mark] D = (3, 5). Verify: B−A = (3, 1) and C−D = (3, 1) so AB ∥ = DC. Hence ABCD is a parallelogram.

Answer. D = (3, 5).

Q6. 1 mark Distance Formula

A circle is drawn with the origin as centre passing through the point (13/2, 0). Which of the following points does NOT lie in the interior of the circle?

  1. (−3/4, 1)
  2. (2, 7/3)
  3. (5, −1/2)
  4. (−6, 5/2)

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Radius = distance of O(0,0) from (13/2, 0) = 6.5. A point is interior ⇔ its distance from O < 6.5. Distances: (−3/4,1)→1.25, (2,7/3)→√(85/9)≈3.07, (5,−1/2)→√(101)/2≈5.02 (all < 6.5), but (−6,5/2)→√(36+25/4)=√(169/4)=6.5 = radius, so it lies ON the circle, not inside.

Answer. (−6, 5/2) — it lies on the circle (distance 6.5 = radius), not in the interior.

Q7. 2 marks Similar Triangles (Coordinates)

State whether the following statement is true or false and justify: ΔABC with vertices A (−2, 0), B (2, 0) and C (0, 2) is similar to ΔDEF with vertices D (−4, 0), E (4, 0) and F (0, 4).

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The sides of ΔABC are AB = 4, BC = 2√2, CA = 2√2; the sides of ΔDEF are DE = 8, EF = 4√2, FD = 4√2.
  2. [1 mark] Each side of ΔDEF is exactly twice the corresponding side of ΔABC, so the triangles are similar (SSS) — the statement is True.

Answer. True — all three pairs of sides are in the ratio 1 : 2.

Q8. 3 marks Distance Formula

If A(5, 3), B(11, −5) and P(12, y) are the vertices of a triangle right-angled at P, find the value(s) of y.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Right angle at P ⇒ by the converse of Pythagoras, PA² + PB² = AB². Compute AB² = 6² + (−8)² = 100.
  2. [1 mark] PA² = 7² + (y−3)² and PB² = 1² + (y+5)². Substitute: 49 + (y−3)² + 1 + (y+5)² = 100 ⇒ (y−3)² + (y+5)² = 50.
  3. [1 mark] Simplify to 2y² + 4y − 16 = 0 ⇒ y² + 2y − 8 = 0 ⇒ (y+4)(y−2) = 0 ⇒ y = 2 or y = −4.

Answer. y = 2 or y = −4.

Q9. 4 marks Distance Formula

During a cricket practice the bowler stands at O(0, 0) and three fielders are positioned at P(6, 8), Q(−9, 12) and R(20, 21) on a coordinate map of the ground. (i) Find the distance of fielder P from the bowler. (ii) Find the distance of fielder Q from the bowler. (iii) Find the distance of fielder R from the bowler. (iv) Which fielder is the farthest from the bowler?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] OP = √[(6−0)² + (8−0)²] = √(36 + 64) = √100 = 10 units.
  2. [1 mark] OQ = √[(−9)² + 12²] = √(81 + 144) = √225 = 15 units.
  3. [1 mark] OR = √[20² + 21²] = √(400 + 441) = √841 = 29 units.
  4. [1 mark] Comparing 10 < 15 < 29, fielder R at (20, 21) is the farthest from the bowler.

Answer. OP = 10, OQ = 15, OR = 29; fielder R is farthest.

Q10. 5 marks Distance Formula

Verify that the points A(−2, −1), B(1, 0), C(4, 3) and D(1, 2), taken in order, form a parallelogram. Using the lengths of the diagonals, decide further whether it is a rectangle.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] AB = √[(1+2)²+(0+1)²] = √10 and CD = √[(1−4)²+(2−3)²] = √10.
  2. [1 mark] BC = √[(4−1)²+(3−0)²] = √18 and DA = √[(−2−1)²+(−1−2)²] = √18.
  3. [1 mark] AB = CD and BC = DA ⇒ both pairs of opposite sides equal ⇒ ABCD is a parallelogram.
  4. [1 mark] Diagonal AC = √[(4+2)²+(3+1)²] = √52; diagonal BD = √[(1−1)²+(2−0)²] = 2.
  5. [1 mark] AC ≠ BD, so the diagonals are unequal ⇒ ABCD is a parallelogram but NOT a rectangle.

Answer. Parallelogram (opposite sides equal); not a rectangle (AC = √52 ≠ BD = 2).

Q11. 1 mark Perimeter via Distance Formula

The perimeter of a triangle with vertices (0, 4), (0, 0) and (3, 0) is

  1. 5
  2. 12
  3. 11
  4. 7 + √5

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The side lengths are 4 (from (0,4) to (0,0)), 3 (from (0,0) to (3,0)) and 5 (from (0,4) to (3,0), since √(3²+4²)=5); perimeter = 4 + 3 + 5 = 12 — option (B).

Answer. 12 — option (B).

Q12. 2 marks Point on a Segment

State whether the following statement is true or false and justify: Point P (−4, 2) lies on the line segment joining the points A (−4, 6) and B (−4, −6).

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] A, B and P all have x-coordinate −4, so they are collinear on the vertical line x = −4.
  2. [1 mark] Since 2 lies between −6 and 6, P is between A and B — the statement is True.

Answer. True — P lies on x = −4 between A and B.

Common mistakes — where marks are lost

  • <b>Swapping the section-formula weights</b> — writing m_1x_1 instead of m_1x_2. The ratio's first part multiplies the SECOND point's coordinate.
  • <b>Sign slips with negative coordinates</b> — (x_2-x_1) with x_1 negative becomes a sum, e.g. 3-(-2)=5, not 1.
  • <b>Dropping the square root</b>, or taking the negative root — distance is always the <i>positive</i> square root.
  • <b>Forgetting the axis condition</b> — a point on the y-axis has x=0 (on the x-axis, y=0); that is the equation you solve for the ratio or the point.
