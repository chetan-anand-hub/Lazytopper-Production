Triangles — Class 10 Mathematics, NCERT Chapter 6

Board weightage: high · 12 step-marked questions · 33 marks in total

Same shape, any size. Two figures are <b>similar</b> when they have the same shape but not necessarily the same size - one is a scaled copy of the other. For triangles this means <b>equal corresponding angles</b> and <b>proportional corresponding sides</b>. The chapter gives you shortcuts (the AAA, SSS and SAS criteria) to decide similarity from just three facts, the <b>Basic Proportionality Theorem</b> to split sides in ratio, and a way to measure the unmeasurable - heights of towers and lengths of shadows - by comparing similar triangles.

Formula sheet

  • Basic Proportionality Theorem DE parallel BC Rightarrow AD / DB=AE / EC
  • Similar triangles - sides triangle ABC sim triangle DEF Rightarrow AB / DE=BC / EF=CA / FD
  • Similar triangles - angles ∠ A=∠ D, ∠ B=∠ E, ∠ C=∠ F
  • Criteria AAA / AA · SSS · SAS (included angle)

Key definitions

Similar figures
same shape, any size. Every pair of congruent figures is automatically similar, but similar figures need not be congruent (a small photo and its enlargement are similar, not congruent). NCERT 2026-27 Ch6 p.97 (Summary 1)
Similar polygons
for polygons you need BOTH conditions: every corresponding angle equal AND every corresponding side in the same ratio. Either one alone is not enough (a square and a rectangle share angles but not ratios; a square and a rhombus share ratios but not angles). NCERT 2026-27 Ch6 p.75
Similar triangles
the same two conditions as polygons - but for triangles, verifying ONE of them automatically forces the other. That is exactly what the AAA/SSS/SAS criteria below exploit, so you never have to check all six pairs. NCERT 2026-27 Ch6 p.85
Basic Proportionality Theorem (Theorem 6.1)
the Thales Theorem: a line parallel to one side of a triangle cuts the other two sides proportionally, AD / DB=AE / EC. This is the ONE theorem in this chapter CBSE can ask you to PROVE - see the Proof tab. NCERT 2026-27 Ch6 p.80
AAA (Angle-Angle-Angle) similarity criterion (Theorem 6.3)
equal angles Rightarrow proportional sides Rightarrow similar. Since a triangle's third angle is fixed once two are known, two equal pairs of angles are already enough - that shorter form is the <b>AA criterion</b>.
SAS (Side-Angle-Side) similarity criterion (Theorem 6.5)
one equal angle that sits BETWEEN two pairs of sides in the same ratio Rightarrow similar. The equal angle must be the <b>included</b> angle - the one between the two proportional sides.
Scale factor
Note that the same ratio of the corresponding sides is referred to as the scale factor (or the Representative Fraction) for the polygons. NCERT 2026-27 Ch6 p.76
Converse of BPT (Theorem 6.2)
Theorem 6.2 : If a line divides any two sides of a triangle in the same ratio, then the line is parallel to the third side. NCERT 2026-27 Ch6 p.82
SSS (Side-Side-Side) similarity criterion (Theorem 6.4)
If in two triangles, corresponding sides are in the same ratio, then their corresponding angles are equal and hence the triangles are similar.
AA similarity criterion
If two angles of one triangle are respectively equal to two angles of another triangle, then the two triangles are similar. NCERT 2026-27 Ch6 p.88

What the board actually asks

the three similarity criteria (AAA/AA, SSS, SAS), the Basic Proportionality Theorem (Thales) and its converse, and similar-triangle applications (heights, shadows, distances) - plus the ONE proof CBSE can ask you to write here: Theorem 6.1 (BPT). The rationalised 2026-27 chapter stops at the SAS criterion - areas of similar triangles and the Pythagoras proofs are no longer in this chapter.

Q1. 1 mark Areas of Similar Triangles

In △ABC, P lies on AB and Q lies on AC with PQ ∥ BC, and PQ divides △ABC into two parts of equal area. The ratio PA : AB is:

  1. 1 : √2
  2. 2 : 3
  3. √2 : 1
  4. 1 : 4

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] PQ ∥ BC ⇒ △APQ ~ △ABC, so ar(APQ)/ar(ABC) = (AP/AB)². Equal-area split ⇒ this ratio = 1/2, giving AP/AB = 1/√2, i.e. PA : AB = 1 : √2.

Answer. 1 : √2 — option (a).

Q2. 2 marks Basic Proportionality Theorem

In △KMN, P lies on KM and Q lies on KN such that PQ ∥ MN. If KP : PM = 4 : 13 and KN = 20.4 cm, find KQ.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] PQ ∥ MN ⇒ by BPT KP/PM = KQ/QN ⇒ KP/KM = KQ/KN = 4/17 (since KP : KM = 4 : 4+13).
  2. [1 mark] KQ = (4/17) × 20.4 = 4.8 cm.

Answer. KQ = 4.8 cm.

Q3. 3 marks Areas of Similar Triangles

In △ABC, DE ∥ BC with D on AB and E on AC. If AD = 1.5 cm and DB = 2·AD, find the ratio of the area of trapezium BCED to the area of △ADE.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] DB = 2 × 1.5 = 3 cm ⇒ AB = 1.5 + 3 = 4.5 cm; DE ∥ BC ⇒ △ADE ~ △ABC.
  2. [1 mark] ar(ADE)/ar(ABC) = (AD/AB)² = (1.5/4.5)² = 1/9.
  3. [1 mark] ar(BCED) = ar(ABC) − ar(ADE) ⇒ ar(BCED) : ar(ADE) = (9 − 1) : 1 = 8 : 1.

Answer. ar(trapezium BCED) : ar(△ADE) = 8 : 1.

Q4. 4 marks Similarity criteria and correspondence

To measure the height of a building, Ria places a small mirror flat on the level ground and steps back until she just sees the top of the building reflected in it. Her eyes are 1.5 m above the ground, she stands 1.2 m from the mirror, and the mirror is 9.6 m from the foot of the building. Because the angle of incidence equals the angle of reflection, the girl-mirror triangle and the building-mirror triangle are similar. (i) State the similarity criterion and the equal angles. (ii) Write the proportion relating the building height H to the given lengths. (iii) Find the height H of the building. (iv) Find the ratio of the areas of the smaller (girl) triangle to the larger (building) triangle.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] (i) Both triangles have a right angle (girl and building are vertical) and equal angles at the mirror (incidence = reflection), so by the AA criterion the triangles are similar.
  2. [1 mark] (ii) Corresponding sides are proportional: H/1.5 = 9.6/1.2.
  3. [1 mark] (iii) H = 1.5 x (9.6/1.2) = 1.5 x 8 = 12 m.
  4. [1 mark] (iv) Ratio of areas = (1.2/9.6)^2 = (1/8)^2 = 1/64, i.e. 1 : 64.

Answer. (i) AA similarity (ii) H/1.5 = 9.6/1.2 (iii) 12 m (iv) 1 : 64

Q5. 5 marks Basic Proportionality Theorem — Statement and Application

State and prove Basic proportionality theorem. In ΔABC, if DE ∥ BC, AD = x, DB = x − 2, AE = x + 2 and EC = x − 1, then using the above result, find the value of x.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Statement: If a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides in distinct points, then the other two sides are divided in the same ratio.
  2. [1 mark] Given and construction: In ΔABC, DE ∥ BC meets AB at D and AC at E. Join BE and CD; draw EM ⊥ AB and DN ⊥ AC.
  3. [1 mark] Proof (areas): ar(ΔADE)/ar(ΔBDE) = AD/DB (triangles on bases AD and DB with the same height EM); similarly ar(ΔADE)/ar(ΔCDE) = AE/EC.
  4. [1 mark] Since ΔBDE and ΔCDE are on the same base DE and between the same parallels DE and BC, ar(ΔBDE) = ar(ΔCDE). Therefore AD/DB = AE/EC.
  5. [1 mark] Application: x/(x − 2) = (x + 2)/(x − 1) ⟹ x(x − 1) = (x + 2)(x − 2) ⟹ x² − x = x² − 4 ⟹ x = 4.

Answer. BPT stated and proved; x = 4.

Q6. 1 mark Pythagoras Theorem

The length of the side of a square whose diagonal is 16 cm is:

  1. 8√2 cm
  2. 16√2 cm
  3. 8 cm
  4. 4√2 cm

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] For a square of side x, diagonal = x√2 (Pythagoras on two equal sides). So x√2 = 16 ⇒ x = 16/√2 = 8√2 cm.

Answer. 8√2 cm — option (a).

Q7. 2 marks Pythagoras Theorem

ABCD is a rectangle with AB = 6 cm and AD = 3 cm. E is a point on AB such that AE = (2/3)AB. Find the length of DE.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] AE = (2/3) × 6 = 4 cm; △ADE is right-angled at A (adjacent sides of a rectangle).
  2. [1 mark] DE = √(AD² + AE²) = √(9 + 16) = √25 = 5 cm.

Answer. DE = 5 cm.

Q8. 3 marks Pythagoras Theorem

In △PQR, ∠QPR = 90°, PQ = 24 cm and QR = 26 cm. A point K is such that ∠PKR = 90° and KR = 8 cm. Find PR and PK.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] In right △PQR: PR = √(QR² − PQ²) = √(676 − 576) = √100 = 10 cm.
  2. [1 mark] In right △PKR (right-angled at K), PR is the hypotenuse: PK = √(PR² − KR²) = √(100 − 64).
  3. [1 mark] PK = √36 = 6 cm.

Answer. PR = 10 cm, PK = 6 cm.

Q9. 4 marks Similarity criteria and correspondence

A student measures the width of a canal without crossing it. AB is the width (A on the near bank, B a post on the far bank, with AB perpendicular to the bank direction). She walks 30 m along the bank from A to a rod at C, a further 10 m to D, then turns inland at right angles to the bank and walks until the post B is exactly in line with the rod at C; she has walked DE = 9 m. This makes triangle ABC similar to triangle EDC. (i) State the similarity criterion that makes the two triangles similar. (ii) Write the proportion for the width AB. (iii) Find the width AB of the canal. (iv) Find the ratio of the areas of triangle ABC to triangle EDC.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] (i) Right angles at A and D are equal, and the angles at C are vertically opposite (equal), so by AA the triangles are similar.
  2. [1 mark] (ii) Corresponding sides: AB/DE = AC/DC, i.e. AB/9 = 30/10.
  3. [1 mark] (iii) AB = 9 x (30/10) = 9 x 3 = 27 m.
  4. [1 mark] (iv) Ratio of areas = (AC/DC)^2 = (30/10)^2 = 9 : 1.

Answer. (i) AA similarity (ii) AB/9 = 30/10 (iii) 27 m (iv) 9 : 1

Q10. 5 marks Basic Proportionality Theorem

In △ABC, D and E lie on sides AB and AC respectively such that DE ∥ BC. It is given that AD = (x + 1) cm, DB = (x − 1) cm, AE = (x + 3) cm and EC = x cm. Find the value of x, and hence write the lengths of AD and AE.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Since DE ∥ BC, by the Basic Proportionality Theorem AD/DB = AE/EC.
  2. [1 mark] Substitute: (x + 1)/(x − 1) = (x + 3)/x.
  3. [1 mark] Cross-multiply: x(x + 1) = (x − 1)(x + 3) ⇒ x² + x = x² + 2x − 3.
  4. [1 mark] The x² terms cancel: x = 2x − 3 ⇒ x = 3.
  5. [1 mark] Hence AD = x + 1 = 4 cm and AE = x + 3 = 6 cm (with DB = 2 cm, EC = 3 cm; ratios 4/2 = 6/3 ✓).

Answer. x = 3; AD = 4 cm, AE = 6 cm.

Q11. 1 mark Similar Figures

Which of the following statements is FALSE?

  1. All isosceles triangles are similar.
  2. All squares are similar.
  3. All circles are similar.
  4. All equilateral triangles are similar.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Isosceles triangles can have different vertex angles, so they need not be equiangular ⇒ not always similar. Squares, circles and equilateral triangles are each always similar. Hence the false statement is 'All isosceles triangles are similar.'

Answer. All isosceles triangles are similar — option (a).

Q12. 2 marks Basic Proportionality Theorem

In △PQR, S lies on PQ and T lies on PR with ST ∥ QR. If PS : SQ = 3 : 5 and PR = 28 cm, find PT.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] ST ∥ QR ⇒ PS/SQ = PT/TR ⇒ PS/PQ = PT/PR = 3/8 (from PS : SQ = 3 : 5).
  2. [1 mark] PT = (3/8) × 28 = 10.5 cm.

Answer. PT = 10.5 cm.

Common mistakes — where marks are lost

  • <b>Wrong vertex order in the similarity</b> - writing triangle ABC sim triangle EDF instead of triangle ABC sim triangle DEF breaks every ratio and angle that follows.
  • <b>Assuming one condition is enough for polygons</b> - a square and a rectangle have equal angles but not equal ratios; a square and a rhombus have equal ratios but not equal angles. Only for TRIANGLES does one condition imply the other.
  • <b>Confusing BPT with its converse</b> - Theorem 6.1 needs the line GIVEN parallel and concludes a ratio; Theorem 6.2 needs the ratio GIVEN equal and concludes parallel. Quote the right direction.
  • <b>Pairing the wrong sides in the ratio</b> - after triangle ABC sim triangle DEF, the ratio is AB / DE=BC / EF=CA / FD, not AB / EF. Follow the vertex correspondence.
  • <b>In SAS, using a non-included angle</b> - the equal angle must sit BETWEEN the two proportional sides, or SAS does not apply.
