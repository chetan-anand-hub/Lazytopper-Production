Pair of Linear Equations in Two Variables — Class 10 Mathematics, NCERT Chapter 3

Board weightage: high · 12 step-marked questions · 33 marks in total

Two conditions, one point. A <b>pair of linear equations in two variables</b> is two straight-line equations a_1x + b_1y + c_1 = 0 and a_2x + b_2y + c_2 = 0 that you solve together. Geometrically each is a line, so the whole chapter answers one question: <b>where do the two lines meet?</b> They can cross once (one solution), run parallel (no solution), or lie on top of each other (infinitely many). You learn to find that meeting point three ways — by graph, by <b>substitution</b>, and by <b>elimination</b> — and to predict the outcome just from the coefficient ratios.

Formula sheet

  • General form a_1x+b_1y+c_1=0, a_2x+b_2y+c_2=0
  • Unique solution (intersecting) a_1 / a_2≠b_1 / b_2
  • No solution (parallel) a_1 / a_2=b_1 / b_2≠c_1 / c_2
  • Infinitely many (coincident) a_1 / a_2=b_1 / b_2=c_1 / c_2

Key definitions

Consistent pair of linear equations
any pair that HAS at least one solution — the lines either cross once (unique) or coincide (infinitely many). “Has a solution” is the whole test. NCERT 2026-27 Ch3 p.25
Inconsistent pair of linear equations
the lines are <b>parallel</b> and never meet, so there is NO common (x,y). In working, a false statement like 0 = 9 signals this. NCERT 2026-27 Ch3 p.25
Dependent pair of linear equations
the two equations are really the SAME line (coincident), so every point on it solves both — infinitely many solutions. A dependent pair is a special case of consistent, never inconsistent. NCERT 2026-27 Ch3 p.25
Substitution method
isolate one variable in one equation (x = dots), then <b>substitute</b> it into the other so only one variable is left. Fastest when a coefficient is already 1. NCERT 2026-27 Ch3 p.31 (Remark, Section 3.3.1)
Elimination method
multiply the equations so one variable’s coefficients match, then <b>add or subtract</b> to cancel that variable. Best when neither variable is already isolated. NCERT 2026-27 Ch3 p.34 (Remark 1, Section 3.3.2)
Graph = two lines
The graph of a pair of linear equations in two variables is represented by two lines. NCERT 2026-27 Ch3 p.37 (Summary 2)
Algebraic methods
Algebraic Methods : We have discussed the following methods for finding the solution(s) of a pair of linear equations : (i) Substitution Method (ii) Elimination Method NCERT 2026-27 Ch3 p.37 (Summary 3)
Reducible to a linear pair
There are several situations which can be mathematically represented by two equations that are not linear to start with. But we alter them so that they are reduced to a pair of linear equations. NCERT 2026-27 Ch3 p.37 (Summary 5)

What the board actually asks

solving a pair by substitution and by elimination, reading a solution off a graph, and classifying a pair as consistent / inconsistent / dependent from the coefficient ratios — the two algebraic methods plus a word-problem application carry almost every mark here.

Q1. 1 mark Conditions for Infinitely Many Solutions

If the system 2x + 3y = 7 and 2ax + (a + b)y = 28 has infinitely many solutions, then:

  1. a = 2b
  2. b = 2a
  3. a + 2b = 0
  4. 2a + b = 0

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Infinitely many ⇒ 2/2a = 3/(a+b) = 7/28 = 1/4, giving a = 4 and a + b = 12 ⇒ b = 8 = 2a.

Answer. b = 2a

Q2. 2 marks Substitution Method

Solve the following pair of linear equations by the substitution method: 2x + 3y = 19 and 5x + 4y = 37.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] From 2x + 3y = 19, x = (19 − 3y)/2; substitute into 5x + 4y = 37: 5(19 − 3y)/2 + 4y = 37.
  2. [1 mark] 95 − 15y + 8y = 74 ⇒ −7y = −21 ⇒ y = 3, then x = (19 − 9)/2 = 5.

Answer. x = 5, y = 3.

Q3. 3 marks Word Problem (Two-digit Number)

The sum of the digits of a two-digit number is 15. The number obtained by reversing the order of the digits exceeds the given number by 9. Find the given number.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Let the number be 10x + y with x + y = 15.
  2. [1 mark] Reversed exceeds it by 9: (10y + x) − (10x + y) = 9 ⇒ y − x = 1.
  3. [1 mark] Solving x + y = 15, y − x = 1 ⇒ x = 7, y = 8; the number is 78.

Answer. 78.

Q4. 4 marks Elimination Method

At a stationery shop, Riya buys 3 notebooks and 2 pens for ₹80. Meera buys 4 notebooks and 3 pens of the same kind for ₹110. Let the cost of one notebook be ₹x and one pen be ₹y. (i) Form the pair of linear equations. (ii) Solve for x and y by elimination. (iii) State the cost of a notebook and a pen. (iv) Find the cost of 5 notebooks and 5 pens.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Form equations: 3x + 2y = 80 …(1); 4x + 3y = 110 …(2).
  2. [1 mark] Eliminate y: (1)×3 ⇒ 9x + 6y = 240; (2)×2 ⇒ 8x + 6y = 220; subtract ⇒ x = 20.
  3. [1 mark] Back-substitute in (1): 60 + 2y = 80 ⇒ y = 10; notebook ₹20, pen ₹10.
  4. [1 mark] Cost of 5 notebooks + 5 pens = 5(20) + 5(10) = 100 + 50 = ₹150.

Answer. Notebook ₹20, pen ₹10; 5 of each cost ₹150.

Q5. 5 marks Word Problems on Linear Equations

The present age of a father is three times the present age of his son. Twelve years from now, the father's age will be twice the son's age. Find their present ages.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Let the father's present age be x years and the son's present age be y years. 'Father is three times the son': x = 3y …(1).
  2. [1 mark] Twelve years hence, father = x + 12 and son = y + 12, with father twice the son: x + 12 = 2(y + 12) → x − 2y = 12 …(2).
  3. [1 mark] Substitute (1) into (2): 3y − 2y = 12 → y = 12.
  4. [1 mark] From (1): x = 3(12) = 36.
  5. [1 mark] Hence father is 36 years and son is 12 years. (Check: in 12 years, 48 = 2 × 24 ✓.)

Answer. Father 36 years, son 12 years.

Q6. 1 mark Conditions for No Solution

The value of k for which the system x + 2y = 5 and 3x + ky + 15 = 0 has no solution is:

  1. 6
  2. 3
  3. 2/3
  4. No such value

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] No solution ⇒ 1/3 = 2/k ≠ −5/15; 1/3 = 2/k gives k = 6, and −1/3 ≠ 1/3 holds, so k = 6.

Answer. k = 6

Q7. 2 marks Word Problem (Geometry)

If the length and the breadth of a rectangle are each increased by 2 units, its area increases by 54 square units. Find the perimeter of the rectangle.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] (l + 2)(b + 2) − lb = 54 ⇒ 2l + 2b + 4 = 54.
  2. [1 mark] l + b = 25, so perimeter = 2(l + b) = 50 units.

Answer. Perimeter = 50 units.

Q8. 3 marks Word Problem (Fraction)

The sum of the numerator and denominator of a fraction is 4 more than twice the numerator. If the numerator and denominator are each increased by 3, they are in the ratio 2 : 3. Determine the fraction.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Let fraction = x/y. Given x + y = 2x + 4 ⇒ y − x = 4.
  2. [1 mark] (x + 3)/(y + 3) = 2/3 ⇒ 3x + 9 = 2y + 6 ⇒ 3x − 2y = −3.
  3. [1 mark] Substitute y = x + 4: 3x − 2(x + 4) = −3 ⇒ x = 5, y = 9; fraction = 5/9.

Answer. 5/9.

Q9. 4 marks Word Problems on Linear Equations

In a T20 match, a batsman scored 72 runs using only boundaries (fours) and sixes, hitting 15 scoring shots in all. Let x = number of fours and y = number of sixes. (i) Write the two equations. (ii) Solve for x and y. (iii) How many fours and sixes did he hit? (iv) How many runs came from sixes alone?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Shots: x + y = 15 …(1); runs: 4x + 6y = 72 …(2).
  2. [1 mark] From (1) x = 15 − y; substitute in (2): 4(15 − y) + 6y = 72 ⇒ 60 + 2y = 72.
  3. [1 mark] 2y = 12 ⇒ y = 6, x = 9; so 9 fours and 6 sixes.
  4. [1 mark] Runs from sixes = 6 × 6 = 36 runs.

Answer. 9 fours, 6 sixes; 36 runs from sixes.

Q10. 5 marks Word Problems on Linear Equations

The sum of the present ages of a mother and her daughter is 60 years. Six years ago, the mother's age was five times the age of the daughter at that time. Find their present ages.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Let mother's present age be x years and daughter's be y years. Sum condition: x + y = 60 …(1).
  2. [1 mark] Six years ago: mother = x − 6, daughter = y − 6, with x − 6 = 5(y − 6) → x − 5y = −24 …(2).
  3. [1 mark] Subtract (2) from (1): (x + y) − (x − 5y) = 60 − (−24) → 6y = 84 → y = 14.
  4. [1 mark] From (1): x = 60 − 14 = 46.
  5. [1 mark] Hence mother is 46 years and daughter is 14 years. (Check: 6 years ago, 40 = 5 × 8 ✓.)

Answer. Mother 46 years, daughter 14 years.

Q11. 1 mark Graphical Method

The graphic representation of the equations x + 2y = 3 and 2x + 4y + 7 = 0 gives a pair of:

  1. parallel lines
  2. intersecting lines
  3. coincident lines
  4. perpendicular lines

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] a₁/a₂ = 1/2 = b₁/b₂ but c₁/c₂ = −3/7 ≠ 1/2, so the lines are parallel (no solution).

Answer. Parallel lines

Q12. 2 marks Word Problem (Ages)

The sum of the ages of a father and his son is 40 years. If the father's age is three times that of his son, find their present ages.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Let father = x, son = y: x + y = 40 and x = 3y.
  2. [1 mark] 3y + y = 40 ⇒ y = 10, x = 30. Father 30 years, son 10 years.

Answer. Father 30 years, son 10 years.

Common mistakes — where marks are lost

  • <b>Misreading a graphical intersection</b> — non-integer coordinates are easy to read wrong; if the crossing isn’t on clean grid points, solve algebraically instead.
  • <b>Swapping the no-solution / infinite-solution verdict</b> — a TRUE no-variable line (18=18) means infinitely many; a FALSE one (0=9) means no solution. Students reverse these.
  • <b>Eliminating without matching coefficients</b> — you must first scale so one variable’s coefficients are numerically equal (and mind the sign: add if opposite, subtract if same).
  • <b>Confusing the ratio conditions</b> — parallel/inconsistent is a_1 / a_2=b_1 / b_2≠c_1 / c_2; coincident/dependent needs ALL three ratios equal.
  • <b>Stopping at one answer in a two-case word problem</b> — e.g. the two-digit number gives 42 AND 24; report every valid solution.
