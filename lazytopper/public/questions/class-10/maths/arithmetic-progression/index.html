Arithmetic Progressions — Class 10 Mathematics, NCERT Chapter 5

Board weightage: high · 12 step-marked questions · 33 marks in total

Add the same step, again and again. An <b>arithmetic progression</b> is a list of numbers where each term is the previous one plus a fixed step d (the <b>common difference</b>). Pin down just two numbers -- the first term a and the common difference d -- and the whole list is fixed: a, a+d, a+2d,dots. The chapter answers three questions: <b>what is the nth term?</b> (a_n = a+(n-1)d), <b>which term / how many terms?</b> (solve that formula for n), and <b>what is the sum of the first n terms?</b> (S_n = n / 2[2a+(n-1)d]).

Formula sheet

  • General form a, a+d, a+2d, a+3d, dots
  • Common difference d = a_k+1 - a_k
  • nth term a_n = a + (n-1)d
  • Sum of n terms S_n = n / 2 [2a + (n-1)d]
  • Sum with last term l S_n = n / 2 (a + l)

Key definitions

Arithmetic Progression (AP)
A running list where you reach the next number by adding the SAME fixed amount every time. To test a list, check that consecutive differences are all equal. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.51
Common difference
d = a_k+1 - a_k -- subtract any term from the one AFTER it (not before). The sign of d tells you whether the AP rises (d>0), falls (d<0) or stays flat (d=0). NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.51
General form of an AP
Every AP is captured by two numbers: the first term a and the common difference d. Know those and you can write the whole list. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.52
nth term of an AP
a_n = a + (n-1)d -- take (n-1) steps of size d from the first term. This single formula answers 'find the 20th term', 'which term is 78?', and 'how many terms?' (rearrange for n). NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.57
Sum of the first n terms
S_n = n / 2[2a+(n-1)d]. If the last term l is already known, the shortcut S_n = n / 2(a+l) is faster. Note S_n needs the number of terms n, not a term's value. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.64
Term
Each of the numbers in the list is called a term. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.51
Finite AP
Note that in examples (a) to (e) above, there are only a finite number of terms. Such an AP is called a finite AP. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.52
Infinite AP
The APs in examples (i) to (v) in this section, are not finite APs and so they are called infinite Arithmetic Progressions. Such APs do not have a last term. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.52
General term and last term (l)
an is also called the general term of the AP. If there are m terms in the AP, then am represents the last term which is sometimes also denoted by l. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.58
Arithmetic mean
If a, b, c are in AP, then b = (a + c)/2 and b is called the arithmetic mean of a and c. NCERT 2026-27 Ch5 p.72 (A Note to the Reader)

What the board actually asks

finding the nth term (or which term / how many terms), the sum of the first n terms, and modelling a real situation as an AP -- these three carry almost every mark here.

Q1. 1 mark nth Term of an AP

The 11th term of the AP: −5, −5/2, 0, 5/2, ... is

  1. −20
  2. 20
  3. −30
  4. 30

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Here a = −5 and d = −5/2 − (−5) = 5/2; so a₁₁ = a + 10d = −5 + 10 × (5/2) = −5 + 25 = 20 — option (B).

Answer. 20 — option (B).

Q2. 2 marks Test for an AP

Justify whether it is true to say that −1, −3/2, −2, 5/2, ... forms an AP as a₂ − a₁ = a₃ − a₂.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] a₂ − a₁ = −3/2 − (−1) = −1/2 and a₃ − a₂ = −2 − (−3/2) = −1/2, so the first two differences are equal — but equality of only these does not make a list an AP.
  2. [1 mark] a₄ − a₃ = 5/2 − (−2) = 9/2 ≠ −1/2, so the differences are not all equal; hence the statement is false.

Answer. False — a₄ − a₃ = 9/2 ≠ a₃ − a₂, so it is not an AP.

Q3. 3 marks Unknowns in an AP

Find a, b and c such that the following numbers are in AP: a, 7, b, 23, c.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Since 7, b, 23 are consecutive AP terms, b is their average: b = (7 + 23)/2 = 15, and the common difference d = 23 − b = 8.
  2. [1 mark] a = 7 − d = 7 − 8 = −1.
  3. [1 mark] c = 23 + d = 23 + 8 = 31.

Answer. a = −1, b = 15, c = 31.

Q4. 4 marks General

From the 2025 CBSE board paper

A school is organizing a charity run to raise funds for a local hospital. The run is planned as a series of rounds around a track, with each round being 300 metres. To make the event more challenging and engaging, the organizers decide to increase the distance of each subsequent round by 50 metres. For example, the second round will be 350 metres, the third round will be 400 metres and so on. The total number of rounds planned is 10. Based on the information given above, answer the following questions : (i) Write the fourth, fifth and sixth term of the Arithmetic Progression so formed. 1 (ii) Determine the distance of the 8th round. 1 (iii) (a) Find the total distance run after completing all 10 rounds. 2 OR (iii) (b) If a runner completes only the first 6 rounds, what is the total distance run by the runner ? 2

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The round distances form the AP 300, 350, 400, … with first term a = 300 m and common difference d = 50 m. (i) a₄ = 450 m, a₅ = 500 m, a₆ = 550 m.
  2. [1 mark] (ii) a₈ = a + 7d = 300 + 7 × 50 = 650 m.
  3. [1 mark] (iii)(a) S₁₀ = (10/2) × (2 × 300 + 9 × 50) = 5 × 1050. [OR (b) S₆ = (6/2) × (2 × 300 + 5 × 50) = 3 × 850.]
  4. [1 mark] S₁₀ = 5250 m. [OR (b) S₆ = 2550 m.]

Answer. (i) 450 m, 500 m, 550 m; (ii) 650 m; (iii)(a) S₁₀ = 5250 m OR (b) S₆ = 2550 m.

Q5. 5 marks General

From the 2023 CBSE board paper

How many terms of the arithmetic progression 45, 39, 33, ........ must be taken so that their sum is 180 ? Explain the double answer.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] For the AP 45, 39, 33, …, the first term a = 45 and common difference d = 39 − 45 = −6.
  2. [1 mark] Use the sum formula Sₙ = n/2[2a + (n − 1)d]: 180 = n/2[2(45) + (n − 1)(−6)].
  3. [1 mark] Simplify: 180 = n/2[90 − 6n + 6] = n/2(96 − 6n) ⇒ 360 = 96n − 6n².
  4. [1 mark] Rearrange: 6n² − 96n + 360 = 0 ⇒ n² − 16n + 60 = 0 ⇒ (n − 10)(n − 6) = 0 ⇒ n = 10 or n = 6.
  5. [1 mark] Both answers are valid: the 7th to 10th terms are 9, 3, −3, −9, whose sum is 0, so the first 6 and first 10 terms have the same sum 180. Hence n = 6 or n = 10.

Answer. n = 6 or n = 10 (both give sum 180, since the 7th–10th terms add to zero).

Q6. 1 mark First Few Terms of an AP

The first four terms of an AP, whose first term is −2 and the common difference is −2, are

  1. −2, 0, 2, 4
  2. −2, 4, −8, 16
  3. −2, −4, −6, −8
  4. −2, −4, −8, −16

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Adding d = −2 successively to a = −2 gives −2, −4, −6, −8 — option (C).

Answer. −2, −4, −6, −8 — option (C).

Q7. 2 marks Difference of Terms without Computing Them

For the AP: −3, −7, −11, ..., can we find directly a₃₀ − a₂₀ without actually finding a₃₀ and a₂₀? Give reasons for your answer.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] Yes. For any AP, aₘ − aₙ = (m − n)d, which uses only the common difference.
  2. [1 mark] Here d = −7 − (−3) = −4, so a₃₀ − a₂₀ = (30 − 20)(−4) = 10 × (−4) = −40.

Answer. Yes; a₃₀ − a₂₀ = 10d = −40.

Q8. 3 marks Common Difference and Number of Terms

The 26th, 11th and the last term of an AP are 0, 3 and −1/5, respectively. Find the common difference and the number of terms.

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] a₂₆ − a₁₁ = 15d = 0 − 3 = −3, so d = −1/5.
  2. [1 mark] a₁₁ = a + 10d = 3 ⇒ a = 3 − 10(−1/5) = 3 + 2 = 5.
  3. [1 mark] Last term = a + (n − 1)d = −1/5 ⇒ 5 + (n − 1)(−1/5) = −1/5 ⇒ (n − 1)(−1/5) = −26/5 ⇒ n − 1 = 26 ⇒ n = 27.

Answer. d = −1/5 and n = 27.

Q9. 4 marks Word Problems on AP

A city taxi charges ₹20 for the 1st kilometre and ₹12 more for each successive kilometre than the previous one. So the amounts charged for the 1st, 2nd, 3rd … km are ₹20, ₹32, ₹44, … (i) What is the first term of this AP? (ii) How much is charged for the 6th kilometre alone? (iii) For which kilometre is the charge ₹128? (iv) After how many kilometres does the total fare become ₹740?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The first term a = ₹20.
  2. [1 mark] a₆ = a + 5d = 20 + 5 × 12 = 20 + 60 = ₹80.
  3. [1 mark] 20 + (n − 1) × 12 = 128 ⇒ (n − 1) × 12 = 108 ⇒ n − 1 = 9 ⇒ n = 10, the 10th km.
  4. [1 mark] n/2 [40 + (n − 1)12] = 740 ⇒ 12n² + 28n − 1480 = 0 ⇒ 3n² + 7n − 370 = 0 ⇒ n = 10 km.

Answer. (i) a = ₹20; (ii) ₹80; (iii) 10th km; (iv) 10 km.

Q10. 5 marks Sum of n Terms

Jaspal Singh repays a total loan of ₹1,18,000 by paying every month, starting with the first instalment of ₹1000, increasing by ₹100 every month. What amount will he pay in the 30th instalment? After paying the 30th instalment, how much loan does he still have to pay?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The monthly instalments form an AP with first term a = 1000 and common difference d = 100.
  2. [1 mark] 30th instalment a₃₀ = a + 29d = 1000 + 29 × 100 = 1000 + 2900 = ₹3900.
  3. [1 mark] Total repaid in 30 instalments S₃₀ = (30/2)[2a + 29d] = 15[2 × 1000 + 29 × 100].
  4. [1 mark] S₃₀ = 15 × (2000 + 2900) = 15 × 4900 = ₹73,500.
  5. [1 mark] Loan still to be paid = 1,18,000 − 73,500 = ₹44,500.

Answer. 30th instalment = ₹3900; loan still owed = ₹44,500.

Q11. 1 mark nth Term of an AP

The 21st term of the AP whose first two terms are −3 and 4 is

  1. 17
  2. 137
  3. 143
  4. −143

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] a = −3, d = 4 − (−3) = 7, so a₂₁ = a + 20d = −3 + 20 × 7 = 137 — option (B).

Answer. 137 — option (B).

Q12. 2 marks Difference of Corresponding Terms

Two APs have the same common difference. The first term of one AP is 2 and that of the other is 7. The difference between their 10th terms is the same as the difference between their 21st terms, which is the same as the difference between any two corresponding terms. Why?

Step-marked solution

  1. [1 mark] The nth terms are (2 + (n − 1)d) and (7 + (n − 1)d); their difference is (7 + (n − 1)d) − (2 + (n − 1)d) = 7 − 2 = 5, independent of n.
  2. [1 mark] So every pair of corresponding terms differs by 5 — exactly the difference of the first terms — which is why the 10th, 21st and all corresponding differences are equal.

Answer. Because the difference of corresponding terms equals the difference of first terms (7 − 2 = 5) for every n.

Common mistakes — where marks are lost

  • <b>Subtracting the wrong way for d</b> -- always (later term - earlier term). For 6, 3, 0, -3, dots the common difference is -3, not +3.
  • <b>Confusing a term's value with its position n</b> -- 'which term is 78?' asks for n, not for a value.
  • <b>Putting a term value where n belongs</b> inside S_n = n / 2[2a+(n-1)d] -- the sum formula uses the number of terms.
  • <b>Forgetting n must be a positive integer</b> -- a fractional or negative n means the number is NOT a term of the AP.
  • <b>Mixing the two sum forms</b> -- n / 2[2a+(n-1)d] needs a and d; n / 2(a+l) needs the last term l.
