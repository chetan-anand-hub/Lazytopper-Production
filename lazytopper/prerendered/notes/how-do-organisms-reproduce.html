How do Organisms Reproduce?

The Big Idea AUTHORED

Every organism makes copies of itself - some need one parent, some need two.

Reproduction is the one life process a single organism can live without, yet it is what keeps a species going. It always begins with a cell copying its DNA - the blueprint of body design - and the small errors in that copying create variation. Simple organisms reproduce asexually from one parent (fission, budding, fragmentation, regeneration, spore formation, vegetative propagation); more complex organisms use sexual reproduction, combining germ-cells from two parents so offspring carry a fresh mix of DNA. In Class 10 this chapter is mostly examined through labelled diagrams and stage-by-stage explanations, so most marks reward the exact NCERT terms and correctly labelled structures.

Definitions the board rewards NCERT VERBATIM

State these in NCERT’s exact words

Reproduction - the basic event (DNA copy)
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 7,
“Therefore, a basic event in reproduction is the creation of a DNA copy.”
What it means: Before a cell can reproduce it must first copy its DNA - the blueprint of body design - so this DNA copy is the starting event of all reproduction.
Fission
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 7,
“For unicellular organisms, cell division, or fission, leads to the creation of new individuals.”
What it means: In single-celled organisms the whole cell divides - binary fission gives two daughter cells (Amoeba, bacteria), while multiple fission gives many at once (Plasmodium).
Regeneration
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 7,
“simple animals like Hydra and Planaria can be cut into any number of pieces and each piece grows into a complete organism. This is known as regeneration.”
What it means: Some simple animals (Hydra, Planaria) can regrow a whole body from a cut piece; specialised cells proliferate and re-form the missing parts. NCERT stresses this is NOT the normal way they reproduce.
Budding
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 7,
“In Hydra, a bud develops as an outgrowth due to repeated cell division at one specific site.”
What it means: In Hydra an outgrowth (bud) forms by repeated cell division at one spot, grows into a tiny Hydra, then detaches to become a new independent individual.
Vegetative propagation
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 7,
“This property of vegetative propagation is used in methods such as layering or grafting to grow many plants like sugarcane, roses, or grapes for agricultural purposes.”
What it means: New plants are raised from a root, stem or leaf of the parent (layering, grafting, cuttings); all offspring are genetically identical to the parent and can bear flowers and fruit earlier than seed-grown plants.
Asexual reproduction
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 7,
“All the modes of reproduction that we have discussed so far allow new generations to be created from a single individual. This is known as asexual reproduction.”
What it means: Reproduction that needs only one parent and makes offspring genetically identical to it - fission, budding, fragmentation, regeneration, spore formation and vegetative propagation are all asexual.
Sexual reproduction
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 7,
“Sexual reproduction involves two individuals for the creation of a new individual.”
What it means: Reproduction that needs two parents (male and female); germ-cells from each combine, so the offspring receive a fresh mix of DNA and show greater variation.
Fertilisation
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 7,
“This fusion of the germ-cells or fertilisation gives us the zygote which is capable of growing into a new plant.”
What it means: The fusion of the male germ-cell with the female gamete (egg) to form a single cell, the zygote, which grows into a new individual.

Must-know concepts AUTHORED FRAMING

…and where they’re tested

1
Why reproduce? DNA copying and variation
Reproduction is not needed to keep an individual alive, but it keeps the species going. Its most basic event is copying the DNA that carries the body-design blueprint. No copying reaction is perfectly accurate, so each DNA copy carries small variations. Consistent copying keeps a population suited to its niche, while the variations give a few individuals a chance to survive if conditions change.
2-3 mark: importance of DNA copying in reproduction; why variation is useful for a species but not necessarily for the individual.
2
Asexual reproduction - modes in single and simple organisms
A single parent creates offspring with no fusion of germ-cells. Fission: unicellular organisms split - binary fission (two daughters, Amoeba) or multiple fission (many daughters, Plasmodium). Budding: an outgrowth in Hydra detaches as a new individual. Fragmentation: Spirogyra breaks into pieces that grow. Regeneration: a cut piece of Hydra/Planaria regrows a whole body (not the normal mode of reproduction). Spore formation: Rhizopus sporangia release spores.
how binary fission differs from multiple fission; how spore formation benefits an organism; why complex organisms cannot reproduce by regeneration.
3
Vegetative propagation and tissue culture
In many plants the root, stem or leaf grows into a new plant. This is used in layering and grafting (sugarcane, roses, grapes) and lets banana, orange, rose and jasmine that no longer make seeds be propagated. Plants raised this way are genetically identical to the parent and flower/fruit earlier. In tissue culture, cells from the growing tip divide to form a callus, which is grown on a hormone medium into disease-free plantlets.
why vegetative propagation is practised for some plants; advantages over growing from seed; the steps of tissue culture.
4
Why sexual reproduction? Gametes and meiosis
Combining DNA from two individuals creates new combinations of variants faster than copying errors alone. To stop the DNA amount doubling each generation, germ-cells (gametes) are made with half the chromosomes by a cell division called meiosis; fusion at fertilisation restores the full number. The motile germ-cell is the male gamete and the larger, food-storing germ-cell is the female gamete.
advantages of sexual over asexual reproduction; role of meiosis in keeping chromosome number constant; male vs female gamete.
5
Sexual reproduction in flowering plants
The flower carries the reproductive parts: the stamen (anther + filament) is male and makes pollen; the pistil (stigma, style, ovary with ovules) is female. Pollination transfers pollen from anther to stigma (self- or cross-pollination, by wind, water or animals). A pollen tube grows through the style to the ovary, the male germ-cell fuses with the egg (fertilisation) to form the zygote; the ovule becomes the seed and the ovary ripens into the fruit.
5-mark: draw and label the longitudinal section of a flower; difference between pollination and fertilisation; formation of seed and fruit.
6
The human reproductive systems and puberty
At puberty, reproductive tissues mature. The male system makes sperms in the testes (in the scrotum, needing a lower temperature); sperms travel through the vas deferens, gain secretions from the prostate and seminal vesicles, and leave via the urethra and penis. The female system makes eggs in the ovaries; an egg travels down the oviduct (fallopian tube) to the uterus, which opens into the vagina through the cervix.
5-mark: label the human male / female reproductive system; functions of the testis, seminal vesicles and prostate; changes at puberty in boys and girls.
7
Fertilisation, menstruation and reproductive health
If a sperm meets the egg in the oviduct, the zygote implants in the thick uterus lining and is nourished through the placenta for about nine months. If the egg is not fertilised, the unneeded lining breaks down and leaves the body as menstruation. Reproductive health covers avoiding sexually transmitted infections (condoms help) and contraception - barrier methods (condom), intra-uterine devices (the copper-T or loop, placed in the uterus), hormonal pills, and surgical methods that block the vas deferens or fallopian tube.
why menstruation occurs; how the embryo is nourished in the mother's body; methods of contraception; how a copper-T does and does not protect.

Problem-solving strategies AUTHORED
→
Diagram question?Draw it large, label with straight ruled lines and use the exact NCERT terms - "vas deferens", "seminal vesicle", "oviduct / fallopian tube", "stigma", not slang. The LS of a flower and the two reproductive systems are the most-drawn figures.
→
"Difference between" asked?Answer in a two-column table - pollination vs fertilisation, binary vs multiple fission, self- vs cross-pollination, asexual vs sexual - one clear point per row scores faster than a paragraph.
→
Naming / one-word term?Pin the exact word to the exact process: fission (Amoeba), multiple fission (Plasmodium), budding (Hydra, Yeast), fragmentation (Spirogyra), regeneration (Planaria), spore formation (Rhizopus). Examiners award the specific term.
→
"Why / how" reasoning add-on?Give the reason, not just the fact - e.g. testes lie in the scrotum because sperm formation needs a lower temperature; menstruation occurs because the prepared uterus lining is not needed when the egg is not fertilised.
Common mistakes that cost marks

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Confusing pollination with fertilisation. Pollination = transfer of pollen from anther to stigma; fertilisation = fusion of the male germ-cell with the egg to form the zygote. They are different steps (exercise Q1 asks exactly this).
✕
Saying budding occurs in Amoeba. Amoeba reproduces by binary fission; budding is seen in Yeast and Hydra (exercise MCQ Q1).
✕
Putting the vas deferens in the female reproductive system. The vas deferens is a male organ; the female system has ovary, oviduct/fallopian tube, uterus and vagina (exercise MCQ Q2).
✕
Mixing up binary and multiple fission. Binary fission gives two daughter cells (Amoeba); multiple fission gives many daughter cells at once (Plasmodium).
✕
Calling regeneration a normal method of reproduction. NCERT states regeneration is NOT the same as reproduction, since organisms do not normally depend on being cut up to reproduce.
✕
Forgetting why menstruation happens - it occurs only because the egg is NOT fertilised, so the thick uterus lining is no longer needed and is shed.

3 competency questions from the CBSE board pattern, with the full step-marked solution for each — the marks are shown per step, as the official scheme awards them.

5 marksSection D

(a) Certain specialised cells in animals called stem cells have the ability to divide and differentiate into different cell types. This helps in the replacement of a damaged organ. Name and explain two methods of asexual reproduction that are similar to stem cells and occur mostly in multicellular organisms. (b) Identify TWO pairs of reproductive organs in males and females that are functionally similar to each other. Justify.

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] (a) Regeneration: in some multicellular organisms (e.g. Planaria, Hydra) the cells of a body fragment divide and differentiate to rebuild all the missing body parts — similar to the way stem cells act.
  2. [1 mark] (a) Budding: a small outgrowth (bud) develops on the parent body (e.g. Hydra); its cells divide and differentiate into all the required tissues, and the bud then detaches as a new individual — also stem-cell-like.
  3. [1 mark] (b) Testes (male) and Ovaries (female) are a functionally similar pair — both produce gametes and secrete reproductive hormones (testosterone / oestrogen).
  4. [1 mark] (b) Vas deferens (male) and Fallopian tube / oviduct (female) are a functionally similar pair — both are ducts that transport the gametes towards the site of fertilisation.
  5. [1 mark] Justification: in each pair the two organs perform the same reproductive function in the two sexes, which is why they are described as functionally analogous.
3 marksSection C

Nivedita has dark brown eyes like her mother but the same shaped nose as her father. Explain why Nivedita has features from both of her parents.

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] Features are passed on by inheritance.
  2. [1 mark] She was produced by sexual reproduction, involving both parents.
  3. [1 mark] Genes are transferred from each parent to the offspring, so she shows features of both.
1 markSection A

Two statements are given - one labelled Assertion (A) and the other labelled Reason (R). Read the statements carefully and choose the option that correctly describes statements A and R. Assertion (A): Oral contraceptive pills and copper-T do not prevent sexually transmitted diseases. Reason (R): Sexually transmitted disease are transmitted by contact with mucous membranes of infected organs.

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] Correct option: (1) Both A and R are true and R is the correct explanation of A. Neither pills nor copper-T forms a barrier at the mucous membranes, and it is exactly that contact route named in R which explains why these methods cannot prevent transmission.
