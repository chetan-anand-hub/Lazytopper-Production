Arithmetic Progressions

★

What the board actually asks: finding the nth term (or which term / how many terms), the sum of the first n terms, and modelling a real situation as an AP -- these three carry almost every mark here.

The Big Idea AUTHORED

Add the same step, again and again.

An arithmetic progression is a list of numbers where each term is the previous one plus a fixed step d (the common difference). Pin down just two numbers -- the first term a and the common difference d -- and the whole list is fixed: a, a+d, a+2d,…. The chapter answers three questions: what is the nth term? (an​=a+(n−1)d), which term / how many terms? (solve that formula for n), and what is the sum of the first n terms? (Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d]).

Definitions the board rewards NCERT VERBATIM

State these in NCERT’s exact words

Arithmetic Progression (AP)
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“So, an arithmetic progression is a list of numbers in which each term is obtained by adding a fixed number to the preceding term except the first term.”
What it means: A running list where you reach the next number by adding the SAME fixed amount every time. To test a list, check that consecutive differences are all equal.
Common difference
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“This fixed number is called the common difference of the AP. Remember that it can be positive, negative or zero.”
What it means: d=ak+1​−ak​ -- subtract any term from the one AFTER it (not before). The sign of d tells you whether the AP rises (d>0), falls (d<0) or stays flat (d=0).
General form of an AP
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“a, a + d, a + 2d, a + 3d, . . . represents an arithmetic progression where a is the first term and d the common difference. This is called the general form of an AP.”
What it means: Every AP is captured by two numbers: the first term a and the common difference d. Know those and you can write the whole list.
nth term of an AP
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“So, the nth term an of the AP with first term a and common difference d is given by an = a + (n – 1) d.”
What it means: an​=a+(n−1)d -- take (n−1) steps of size d from the first term. This single formula answers 'find the 20th term', 'which term is 78?', and 'how many terms?' (rearrange for n).
Sum of the first n terms
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“So, the sum of the first n terms of an AP is given by S = n/2 [2a + (n – 1) d]”
What it means: Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d]. If the last term l is already known, the shortcut Sn​=2n​(a+l) is faster. Note Sn​ needs the number of terms n, not a term's value.
📖Key terms — NCERT verbatim (state word-for-word)
Term
Each of the numbers in the list is called a term. (Ch 5, )
Finite AP
Note that in examples (a) to (e) above, there are only a finite number of terms. Such an AP is called a finite AP. (Ch 5, )
Infinite AP
The APs in examples (i) to (v) in this section, are not finite APs and so they are called infinite Arithmetic Progressions. Such APs do not have a last term. (Ch 5, )
General term and last term (l)
an is also called the general term of the AP. If there are m terms in the AP, then am represents the last term which is sometimes also denoted by l. (Ch 5, )
Arithmetic mean
If a, b, c are in AP, then b = (a + c)/2 and b is called the arithmetic mean of a and c. (Ch 5, )
Must-know concepts AUTHORED FRAMING

…and where they’re tested

1
Recognise an AP
A list is an AP only if the gap between consecutive terms is constant: a2​−a1​=a3​−a2​=⋯=d. Compute one or two differences before assuming -- squares (1,4,9,…) and doubling lists (2,4,8,…) are the classic traps that look regular but are NOT APs.
MCQ / 'which of these forms an AP'
2
The nth term
an​=a+(n−1)d. Read off the first term a and common difference d, then substitute the position n. This is the most-used tool in the chapter.
1-2 mark 'find the term'
3
Sum of the first n terms
Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d], or 2n​(a+l) when the last term l is known. The formula links four quantities Sn​,a,d,n -- given any three you can find the fourth.
3 mark 'find the sum'
4
Solve for n -- which term / how many terms
Set an​ (or Sn​) equal to the target value and solve for n. n MUST come out a positive integer -- a fractional or negative n means the value is simply not a term of the AP. A sum condition gives a quadratic in n, which can yield two valid answers.
2-3 mark
5
Model a real situation as an AP
A fixed-increment salary, uniform monthly savings, rows of plants decreasing by a constant, a penalty rising by a fixed step -- extract the first term a and the constant change d, then apply the term or the sum formula. Reject any answer the situation forbids (you cannot have a fractional number of rows).
3-5 mark applied / case-based

Common mistakes that cost marks

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Subtracting the wrong way for d -- always (later term − earlier term). For 6,3,0,−3,… the common difference is −3, not +3.
✕
Confusing a term's value with its position n -- 'which term is 78?' asks for n, not for a value.
✕
Putting a term value where n belongs inside Sn​=2n​[2a+(n−1)d] -- the sum formula uses the number of terms.
✕
Forgetting n must be a positive integer -- a fractional or negative n means the number is NOT a term of the AP.
✕
Mixing the two sum forms -- 2n​[2a+(n−1)d] needs a and d; 2n​(a+l) needs the last term l.

Sum of the first n terms -- where the formula comes from

NCERT motivates the sum formula with Gauss's schoolboy trick (p.63): to add 1 to 100 he wrote the list forwards, then backwards, and added the two lines so every column gave the same total 101. The same reversal turns the sum of ANY AP into n identical pairs -- that is the whole derivation.

1
Write the sum of the first n terms.
S=a+(a+d)+(a+2d)+⋯+[a+(n−1)d]
2
Write the same sum again, terms reversed.
S=[a+(n−1)d]+[a+(n−2)d]+⋯+(a+d)+a
3
Add the two lines term by term -- each of the n column-pairs gives the same total.
2S=[2a+(n−1)d]+⋯+[2a+(n−1)d](n times)
First+last, second+second-last, and so on each equal 2a + (n-1)d.
4
There are n equal pairs.
2S=n[2a+(n−1)d]
5
Divide by 2 -- the sum formula.
S=2n​[2a+(n−1)d]
6
Regroup a + (n-1)d as the last term to get the a-and-l form.
S=2n​(a+an​)=2n​(a+l)
Useful when the first term a and last term l are known but d is not (NCERT form (4), p.64).

3 competency questions from the CBSE board pattern, with the full step-marked solution for each — the marks are shown per step, as the official scheme awards them.

5 marksSection D

Jaspal Singh repays a total loan of ₹1,18,000 by paying every month, starting with the first instalment of ₹1000, increasing by ₹100 every month. What amount will he pay in the 30th instalment? After paying the 30th instalment, how much loan does he still have to pay?

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] The monthly instalments form an AP with first term a = 1000 and common difference d = 100.
  2. [1 mark] 30th instalment a₃₀ = a + 29d = 1000 + 29 × 100 = 1000 + 2900 = ₹3900.
  3. [1 mark] Total repaid in 30 instalments S₃₀ = (30/2)[2a + 29d] = 15[2 × 1000 + 29 × 100].
  4. [1 mark] S₃₀ = 15 × (2000 + 2900) = 15 × 4900 = ₹73,500.
  5. [1 mark] Loan still to be paid = 1,18,000 − 73,500 = ₹44,500.
5 marksSection D

The sum of the first five terms of an AP and the sum of the first seven terms of the same AP is 167. If the sum of the first ten terms of this AP is 235, find the sum of its first twenty terms.

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] S₅ = (5/2)(2a + 4d) = 5(a + 2d) and S₇ = (7/2)(2a + 6d) = 7(a + 3d).
  2. [1 mark] S₅ + S₇ = (5a + 10d) + (7a + 21d) = 12a + 31d = 167.
  3. [1 mark] S₁₀ = (10/2)(2a + 9d) = 10a + 45d = 235, i.e. 2a + 9d = 47.
  4. [1 mark] Solve 12a + 31d = 167 with 2a + 9d = 47 (×6 ⇒ 12a + 54d = 282): subtract to get 23d = 115 ⇒ d = 5, then a = 1.
  5. [1 mark] S₂₀ = (20/2)(2a + 19d) = 10(2 + 95) = 970.
5 marksSection D

An AP consists of 37 terms. The sum of the three middle most terms is 225 and the sum of the last three is 429. Find the AP.

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] With 37 terms the middle term is the 19th; the three middle terms are a₁₈, a₁₉, a₂₀ with sum 3a₁₉ = 225, so a₁₉ = 75, i.e. a + 18d = 75.
  2. [1 mark] The last three terms a₃₅, a₃₆, a₃₇ have sum 3a₃₆ = 429, so a₃₆ = 143, i.e. a + 35d = 143.
  3. [1 mark] Subtract the two equations: 17d = 68.
  4. [1 mark] d = 4, and a = 75 − 18 × 4 = 3.
  5. [1 mark] The AP is 3, 7, 11, 15, ...
