Areas Related to Circles

★

What the board actually asks: almost every mark here comes from three moves — the length of an arc, the area of a sector (the fraction θ/360 of the circle), and the area of a segment (that sector minus triangle OAB). Get the central angle and the value of π right, and know when to leave √3 in surd form. The 2026-27 chapter is trimmed to sectors and segments only — combinations of plane figures are no longer in it.

The Big Idea AUTHORED

A slice of the circle — measured by its angle.

Every result in this chapter is one idea: a part of a circle is the fraction 360θ​ of the whole, where θ is the angle it makes at the centre. Take that fraction of the circumference 2πr and you get an arc length; take it of the area πr2 and you get a sector; cut a chord across and the leftover piece is a segment, found by subtracting triangle OAB from the sector.

Definitions the board rewards NCERT VERBATIM

State these in NCERT’s exact words

Sector of a circle
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 11,
“the portion (or part) of the circular region enclosed by two radii and the corresponding arc is called a sector of the circle”
What it means: A 'pizza slice' of the disc — the region boxed in by two radii and the arc between them, with its pointed corner at the centre O.
Segment of a circle
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 11,
“the portion (or part) of the circular region enclosed between a chord and the corresponding arc is called a segment of the circle”
What it means: The region cut off by a straight chord and the arc it subtends — like the slice of melon left after one straight cut. No radius is involved.
Area of a sector
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 11,
“Area of a sector of a circle with radius r and angle with degree measure θ is (θ/360) × πr²”
What it means: Take the fraction 360θ​ of the whole circle's area πr2. Convert the central angle to degrees first.
Length of an arc of a sector
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 11,
“Length of an arc of a sector of a circle with radius r and angle with degree measure θ is (θ/360) × 2πr”
What it means: The same fraction 360θ​, but of the whole circumference 2πr — the curved boundary of the sector, measured in length (cm), not area.
Area of a segment
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 11,
“Area of segment of a circle = Area of the corresponding sector – Area of the corresponding triangle.”
What it means: There is no direct formula — always subtract: segment = its sector − triangle OAB. Finding the triangle (the chord and the perpendicular height) is the hard part.

Must-know concepts AUTHORED FRAMING

…and where they’re tested

1
Sector — minor and major
Two radii plus the arc between them cut the disc into two sectors. The smaller one (shaded, OAPB) is the minor sector and carries the angle θ=∠AOB; the larger one (OAQB) is the major sector with angle 360∘−θ.
2-mark 'find the area of the sector' (Section A/B)
2
Segment — minor and major
A chord AB (not a radius) splits the disc into two segments. The piece between the chord and its arc is the minor segment (APB, shaded); the rest is the major segment (AQB). A segment always needs the triangle OAB to measure it.
3-mark 'find the area of the segment' (Section C)
3
Length of an arc
The curved edge of a sector of angle θ is the fraction 360θ​ of the full circumference: ℓ=360θ​×2πr. It is a length (cm), so never square it.
1-2 mark direct, or part (i) of a case-based problem
4
Area of a sector
The same fraction, taken of the area: 360θ​×πr2. For the major sector the fast route is πr2−(minor sector), which avoids re-substituting 360∘−θ.
2-3 mark direct 'find the area of the sector'
5
Area of a segment = sector − triangle
There is no direct formula. Area of the minor segment =360θ​×πr2−area of △OAB. Get the triangle from a perpendicular dropped from O to the chord (using cos and sin of half the angle), or — when θ=60∘ — as an equilateral triangle of side r.
3-5 mark long answer (Section C/D)

Arc, sector or segment?All three start from the fraction 360θ​. Multiply it by 2πr for an arc (length), by πr2 for a sector (area).
→

→
60° at the centre?Two radii with a 60∘ angle make an equilateral triangle — its area is 43​​a2 with a=r.

'Major sector / segment'?Major = whole − minor: πr2−(minor sector), or πr2−(minor segment). Or use the angle 360∘−θ.

Common mistakes that cost marks

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Reaching for a 'segment formula' — there isn't one. A segment is always sector − triangle OAB.
✕
Forgetting to turn a time / fraction into a central angle — 5 minutes on a clock is 30∘, not 5∘.
✕
Mixing up arc length (×2πr) with sector area (×πr2) — check the unit: cm for an arc, cm2 for an area.
✕
Rounding π early or dropping the unit — state cm2 and keep 3​ exact unless a numerical value is supplied.

Where the sector, arc and segment formulae come from

NCERT derives all three results by the Unitary Method (pp.155-156): scale the whole circle down by the fraction of the angle at the centre. This is the reasoning behind the formulae in the formula strip — worth understanding, not just memorising.

1
A full circle subtends 360° at the centre, and the area of the circular region is πr².
area of circle=πr2
2
Unitary Method: when the angle at the centre is 360° the sector area is πr², so when the angle is 1° the sector area is πr²/360.
360πr2​
3
Therefore for an angle θ at the centre, the sector area is θ times the 1° value.
Area of the sector of angle θ=360θ​×πr2
This is the area-of-a-sector formula (NCERT p.155).
4
The same Unitary-Method argument on the whole circumference 2πr (which corresponds to 360°) gives the arc length.
length of an arc of angle θ=360θ​×2πr
5
The minor segment is what remains of the sector after triangle OAB is removed, so its area is the sector minus that triangle.
Area of segment APB=360θ​×πr2−area of △OAB
NCERT p.156 — segment = sector − corresponding triangle.

3 competency questions from the CBSE board pattern, with the full step-marked solution for each — the marks are shown per step, as the official scheme awards them.

5 marksSection D

A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends an angle of 90° at the centre. Find the area of the corresponding major segment. (Use π = 3.14.)

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] Setup: chord AB subtends 90° at centre O, radius r = 10 cm, so the major sector angle = 360° − 90° = 270°.
  2. [1 mark] Area of major sector = (270/360) × πr² = (3/4) × π × 100.
  3. [1 mark] = (3/4) × 3.14 × 100 = 235.5 cm².
  4. [1 mark] Triangle OAB (right-angled at O): area = (1/2) × 10 × 10 = 50 cm².
  5. [1 mark] Area of major segment = area of major sector + area of triangle = 235.5 + 50 = 285.5 cm².
5 marksSection D

With vertices A, B and C of a right triangle ABC (right-angled at B) as centres, arcs of radius 5 cm each are drawn. If AB = 14 cm, BC = 48 cm and CA = 50 cm, find the area of the shaded region inside the triangle but outside the three sectors.

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] Verification: 14² + 48² = 196 + 2304 = 2500 = 50², so ΔABC is right-angled at B.
  2. [1 mark] Area of ΔABC = (1/2) × AB × BC = (1/2) × 14 × 48 = 336 cm².
  3. [1 mark] Each sector contributes (∠/360) × π × 5², and the total angle = ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180° (angle sum of a triangle).
  4. [1 mark] Total sector area = (180/360) × π × 25 = (1/2) × π × 25 = 12.5 × 3.14 = 39.25 cm².
  5. [1 mark] Shaded area = area of triangle − total sector area = 336 − 39.25 = 296.75 cm².
5 marksSection D

Sides of a triangular field are 15 m, 16 m and 17 m. With the three corners of the field a cow, a buffalo and a horse are tied separately with ropes of length 7 m each to graze in the field. Find the area of the field which cannot be grazed by the three animals.

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] Semi-perimeter s = (15 + 16 + 17)/2 = 24 m.
  2. [1 mark] By Heron's formula, area of the field = √(24 × 9 × 8 × 7) = √12096 = 24√21 m².
  3. [1 mark] The three grazed sectors have radius 7 m and their angles are the three interior angles, summing to 180°.
  4. [1 mark] Total grazed area = (180/360) × (22/7) × 7² = (1/2) × 154 = 77 m².
  5. [1 mark] Area that cannot be grazed = (24√21 − 77) m² ≈ 32.0 m².
