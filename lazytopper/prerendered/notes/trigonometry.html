Introduction to Trigonometry

★

What the board actually asks: finding all six ratios from one, evaluating expressions at the specific angles (0/30/45/60/90), and proving trigonometric identities. The identity manipulations and the specific-angle table carry almost every mark here.

NCERT VERBATIM sourced, word-for-wordAUTHORED LazyTopper teaching scaffolding
The Big Idea AUTHORED

Sides become numbers.

Fix an acute angle in a right triangle and the ratios of its sides stop depending on how big the triangle is - they depend only on the angle. Those six fixed numbers are the trigonometric ratios (sin, cos, tan and their reciprocals cosec, sec, cot). This chapter does three things: defines the ratios, pins down their exact values at 0°,30°,45°,60°,90°, and proves the identity sin2A+cos2A=1 (with its two cousins) that turns messy expressions into 1.

Definitions the board rewards NCERT VERBATIM

State these in NCERT’s exact words

Trigonometry
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 8,
“Trigonometry is the study of relationships between the sides and angles of a triangle.”
What it means: the word comes from the Greek tri (three), gon (sides), metron (measure). In Class 10 we study it for the acute angles of a right triangle only.
Opposite, adjacent, hypotenuse
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 8,
“We call it the side opposite to angle A. AC is the hypotenuse of the right triangle and the side AB is a part of ∠A. So, we call it the side adjacent to angle A.”
What it means: name the three sides relative to the angle you are working with: the side facing the angle is opposite, the longest side (facing the right angle) is the hypotenuse, the third is adjacent. Switch to angle C and opposite/adjacent swap.
Trigonometric ratios
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 8,
“So, the trigonometric ratios of an acute angle in a right triangle express the relationship between the angle and the length of its sides.”
What it means: sinA=hypopp​, cosA=hypadj​, tanA=adjopp​. Memorise them against the labelled triangle, not as isolated letters.
Ratios independent of side length
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 8,
“The values of the trigonometric ratios of an angle do not vary with the lengths of the sides of the triangle, if the angle remains the same.”
What it means: this is why the ratios are well defined - proved by AA similarity. It lets you set the sides as k,2k,… (any positive k): the ratio cancels k, so the answer never depends on your choice.
Trigonometric identity
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 8,
“An equation involving trigonometric ratios of an angle is called a trigonometric identity, if it is true for all values of the angle(s) involved.”
What it means: an identity holds for every valid angle, so you prove it by algebra, not by plugging in one value. To prove one, transform the LHS until it becomes the RHS.
📖Key terms — NCERT verbatim (state word-for-word)
Reciprocal ratios
The ratios defined above are abbreviated as sin A, cos A, tan A, cosec A, sec A and cot A respectively. Note that the ratios cosec A, sec A and cot A are respectively, the reciprocals of the ratios sin A, cos A and tan A. (Ch 8, )
sin A, cos A never exceed 1
Since the hypotenuse is the longest side in a right triangle, the value of sin A or cos A is always less than 1 (or, in particular, equal to 1). (Ch 8, )
sin A is not sin times A
sin A is not the product of ‘sin’ and A. ‘sin’ separated from A has no meaning. (Ch 8, )
Must-know concepts AUTHORED FRAMING

…and where they’re tested

1
Find every ratio from one
Given one ratio, draw a right triangle and mark the two sides in that ratio as multiples of k (e.g. tanA=34​⇒ opposite =4k, adjacent =3k). Get the third side by Pythagoras, then read all six ratios straight off - k always cancels.
2-3 mark “find the other trigonometric ratios”
2
Ratios of the specific angles
The exact values at 0°,30°,45°,60°,90° come from the 45-45-90 and 30-60-90 triangles (Table 8.1). sin rises 0→1, cos falls 1→0; tan90°, cot0°, sec90°, csc0° are not defined. Learn the table cold - it is assumed in every evaluation question.
“Evaluate” expressions in specific angles (Exercise 8.2)
3
The three Pythagorean identities
sin2A+cos2A=1; 1+tan2A=sec2A (for 0°≤A<90°); 1+cot2A=csc2A (for 0°<A≤90°). All three come from Pythagoras. Mind the angle ranges - tan/sec break at 90°, cot/csc break at 0°.
MCQ simplifications like 9sec2A−9tan2A (Exercise 8.3 Q3)
4
Proving identities (LHS → RHS)
Convert everything to sin and cos, take a common denominator, and use sin2A+cos2A=1 (often via 1−sin2A=cos2A). Work on one side only until it becomes the other - never manipulate both sides at once.
3-5 mark “prove that …” (Exercise 8.3 Q4)

Problem-solving strategies AUTHORED
→
Given one ratio, want the others?Draw the triangle, set the two sides as k-multiples, get the third by Pythagoras, read off all six.
→
“Evaluate …” at specific angles?Substitute straight from Table 8.1. Do not re-derive - recall sin,cos,tan at 0/30/45/60/90 instantly.
→
“Prove that …” (identity)?Turn everything into sin and cos, take a common denominator, apply sin2A+cos2A=1. Work one side only.
→
A ratio looks like it could be >1?Check: sinA and cosA are at most 1; secA and cscA are at least 1. If not, re-check opposite vs adjacent.
→
Angle changes from A to C?Opposite and adjacent swap. Re-label before writing the ratio - this is the most common slip.
Common mistakes that cost marks

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Swapping opposite and adjacent when the angle changes (angle A vs angle C) - re-label the sides against the new angle first.
✕
Claiming sinA>1 or cosA>1 - impossible, since the hypotenuse is the longest side.
✕
Reading sinA as sin×A - ‘sin’ with no angle is meaningless.
✕
Writing 0 or ∞ for undefined ratios - tan90°, cot0°, sec90°, csc0° are ‘not defined’.
✕
Ignoring the identity's angle range - 1+cot2A=csc2A holds for 0°<A≤90°, not at A=0°.

The Pythagorean trigonometric identities

NCERT proves one identity, sin2A+cos2A=1, directly from the Pythagoras theorem, then derives the other two by dividing the same relation differently. Every identity in the chapter ultimately rests on these three (p.128-129).

1
Start from Pythagoras in △ABC, right-angled at B.
AB2+BC2=AC2(1)
2
Divide each term of (1) by AC2.
(ACAB​)2+(ACBC​)2=1⇒cos2A+sin2A=1
True for all A with 0°≤A≤90°, so it is a trigonometric identity.
3
Divide (1) by AB2 instead.
1+(ABBC​)2=(ABAC​)2⇒1+tan2A=sec2A
tanA,secA are undefined at 90°, so this holds for 0°≤A<90°.
4
Divide (1) by BC2.
(BCAB​)2+1=(BCAC​)2⇒cot2A+1=csc2A
cotA,cscA are undefined at 0°, so this holds for 0°<A≤90°.

3 competency questions from the CBSE board pattern, with the full step-marked solution for each — the marks are shown per step, as the official scheme awards them.

5 marksSection D

A man on the top of a vertical tower observes a car moving at a uniform speed coming directly towards it. If it takes 12 minutes for the angle of depression to change from 30° to 45°, how soon after this will the car reach the tower? Give your answer to nearest minutes. (Use √3 = 1.73). OR If the angle of elevation of a cloud from a point 10 metres above a lake is 30° and the angle of depression of its reflection in the lake is 60°, find the height of the cloud from the surface of the lake.

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] Let AB be the tower of height h and let the car move from D (angle of depression 30°) to C (angle of depression 45°) in 12 minutes; let the uniform speed give distance DC covered in 12 minutes.
  2. [1 mark] In ΔABC (depression 45°): tan 45° = AB/BC ⟹ BC = AB.
  3. [1 mark] In ΔABD (depression 30°): tan 30° = AB/BD ⟹ BD = √3·AB.
  4. [1 mark] DC = BD − BC = (√3 − 1)·AB, and this is covered in 12 minutes; time to cover BC = AB at the same speed = 12 × BC/DC = 12/(√3 − 1).
  5. [1 mark] Time = 12/(√3 − 1) = 6(√3 + 1) = 6(2.73) ≈ 16 minutes. [OR variant — cloud over a lake: observer 10 m above lake, horizontal distance x. tan 30° = (h − 10)/x and tan 60° = (h + 10)/x; dividing, 3 = (h + 10)/(h − 10) ⟹ 3h − 30 = h + 10 ⟹ h = 20 m.]
2 marksSection B

A flagpole casts its shadow that is 25 m long on the ground. The angle made by the tip of the flagpole and the tip of its shadow on the ground is 45°. Find the height of the flagpole.

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] tan 45° = opposite/adjacent = (height of flagpole)/25.
  2. [1 mark] 1 = (height of flagpole)/25 → height of flagpole = 25 m.
2 marksSection B

Ravi got a clinometer from his school's maths lab and started measuring angles of elevation. He saw a corporate building with the company logo painted on a wall. From a point P on the ground, 24 metres from the base of the building, the angle of elevation of the roof is 45°. The angle of elevation of C, the centre of the logo, is 30°. What is the height of the centre of the logo from the ground?

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] tan 30° = h/x → h/24 = 1/√3.
  2. [1 mark] h = 24/√3 = 8√3 = 8 × 1.73 = 13.84 m.
