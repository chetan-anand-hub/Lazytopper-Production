Surface Areas and Volumes

★

What the board actually asks: surface area and volume of a COMBINATION of solids — cone-on-hemisphere toys, capsules, cylinders with hemispherical ends, a cuboid surmounted by a half-cylinder. Break the object into the basic solids you already know (cuboid, cube, cylinder, cone, sphere, hemisphere), add only the EXPOSED areas for surface area, and simply ADD the volumes.

The Big Idea AUTHORED

Break it into shapes you already know.

Every object in this chapter is two basic solids stuck together. Nothing new to memorise — you already know the surface areas and volumes of the cuboid, cube, cylinder, cone, sphere and hemisphere from Class IX. The whole skill is: see the pieces, then combine them by two rules. For surface area, add only the faces you can still see (the join hides some area). For volume, just add the pieces up (nothing disappears).

Definitions the board rewards NCERT VERBATIM

State these in NCERT’s exact words

Surface area of a combination of solids
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 12,
“the total surface area of the new solid is the sum of the curved surface areas of each of the individual parts.”
What it means: add up only the surfaces you can still SEE after joining — for a cylinder capped by two hemispheres, that is the two curved hemispheres plus the curved cylinder; the flat circular faces vanish inside the join.
Volume of a combination of solids
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 12,
“The volume of the solid formed by joining two basic solids will actually be the sum of the volumes of the constituents, as we see in the examples below.”
What it means: unlike surface area, volume loses nothing at the join — just ADD the volume of each basic solid. This is the one place where you literally sum the pieces with no subtraction for the shared face.
Why surface areas are not simply added
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 12,
“in calculating the surface area, we have not added the surface areas of the two constituents, because some part of the surface area disappeared in the process of joining them.”
What it means: when two solids are stuck together, the faces that touch are hidden inside and no longer count. That is exactly why surface area is NOT the plain sum of the two full surface areas.
Total surface area is not additive
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 12,
“You may note that 'total surface area of the top' is not the sum of the total surface areas of the cone and hemisphere.”
What it means: the sharp warning NCERT prints after Example 1. Do not add TSA(cone) + TSA(hemisphere) — that double-counts the hidden circular base. Add only the exposed curved surfaces.

Must-know concepts AUTHORED FRAMING

…and where they’re tested

1
Break a combination into basic solids
Every object here is two basic solids joined at a shared face. Name the pieces first — e.g. a medicine capsule is a cylinder with a hemisphere on each end; a toy top is a cone surmounted by a hemisphere. Once you can see the pieces, all the formulas are ones you already know.
the setup mark on essentially every combination-solid question
2
Surface area = only the EXPOSED faces
Add the curved (and any still-visible flat) surfaces that remain after joining; drop the faces hidden inside the join. Never add the two full total surface areas — that double-counts the buried circle. When a solid is scooped out, the removed curved surface is added and the flat opening is handled carefully.
2-3 mark 'find the surface area / area to be painted' problems
3
Volume = simply ADD the constituents
Volume is the friendly rule: the volume of the joined solid is the plain sum of the pieces' volumes, because no volume is lost at the join. For a scooped/hollow solid, subtract the volume of the removed piece.
3-5 mark volume and capacity problems
4
The cone's slant height comes first
Any combination with a cone needs its curved surface area πrl, which needs the slant height l=r2+h2​. Compute l BEFORE the CSA. And the cone's height is usually the whole solid's height minus the attached hemisphere's radius, not the whole height.
any combination containing a cone (toy top, tent, rocket)
5
Capacity, and hollowed / scooped solids
When a hemisphere is scooped out of a cylinder (a glass, a bird-bath), the capacity drops by the removed hemisphere's volume, while the surface gains the hemisphere's curved area in place of a flat lid. Read whether the question wants apparent capacity or actual capacity.
capacity and 'surface area of the remaining solid' problems

Common mistakes that cost marks

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Adding the two full surface areas — TSA of a combination is NOT TSA(part 1) + TSA(part 2); the buried join circle is double-counted. Add only the exposed curved/flat areas.
✕
Wrong cone height — for a cone on a hemisphere, the cone's height is the total height minus the hemisphere's radius, not the whole height.
✕
Mixing units — combining a 1.45 m height with a 30 cm radius without converting. Bring everything to one unit first.
✕
Subtracting for volume — volumes of joined solids simply ADD; nothing disappears at the join. Only SCOOPED-out solids involve subtraction.

Why the surface area of a combination is a sum of curved areas

This is the core reasoning of the chapter, worked on NCERT's cone-on-hemisphere toy (pp. 162-164). It shows WHY you add only the exposed curved surfaces, and why the total surface area is not the plain sum of the two solids' total surface areas.

1
Whenever we meet a new solid, break it into basic solids we have already solved.
NCERT's method for a combination: reduce the new problem to smaller problems already solved (p.162).
2
Build the toy by bringing the flat face of a cone and the flat face of a hemisphere together, with equal base radii.
The two flat circular faces meet exactly and are sealed inside the join, so neither is on the outside surface (p.163).
3
Only the curved surfaces remain visible, so the surface area is their sum.
TSA of the toy=CSA of hemisphere+CSA of cone=2πr2+πrl
The exposed surface is exactly the curved hemisphere plus the curved cone (p.163).
4
Therefore the total surface area is NOT the sum of the two solids' full total surface areas.
TSA(cone) + TSA(hemisphere) would count the shared base circle twice; NCERT prints this warning after Example 1 (p.164).
5
For volume the same join hides nothing, so volumes simply add.
Vtoy​=32​πr3+31​πr2h
No volume disappears at the join, so the combined volume is the sum of the constituents (p.167).
