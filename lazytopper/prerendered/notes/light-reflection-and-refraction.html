Definitions the board rewards NCERT VERBATIM

State these in NCERT’s exact words

Laws of reflection of light
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 9,
“(i) The angle of incidence is equal to the angle of reflection, and (ii) The incident ray, the normal to the mirror at the point of incidence and the reflected ray, all lie in the same plane.”
What it means: the ray comes in and bounces off at the same angle to the normal (∠i=∠r), and the incident ray, normal and reflected ray are all flat in one plane.
Laws of refraction of light (Snell's law)
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 9,
“(i) The incident ray, the refracted ray and the normal to the interface of two transparent media at the point of incidence, all lie in the same plane. (ii) The ratio of sine of angle of incidence to the sine of angle of refraction is a constant, for the light of a given colour and for the given pair of media. This law is also known as Snell's law of refraction.”
What it means: sinrsini​ stays the same for a given colour of light and a given pair of media — that constant is the refractive index.
Absolute refractive index
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 9,
“If medium 1 is vacuum or air, then the refractive index of medium 2 is considered with respect to vacuum. This is called the absolute refractive index of the medium.”
What it means: measured against vacuum/air, nm​=Speed of light in the mediumSpeed of light in air​=vc​ (NCERT p.149). A larger n means light slows more — the medium is optically denser.
Principal focus & focal length of a spherical mirror
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 9,
“[Rays parallel to the principal axis] are all meeting/intersecting at a point on the principal axis of the mirror. This point is called the principal focus of the concave mirror. … The distance between the pole and the principal focus of a spherical mirror is called the focal length.”
What it means: parallel rays converge at F (concave) — focal length f = pole-to-F distance, and R=2f.
Magnification produced by a spherical mirror
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 9,
“Magnification produced by a spherical mirror gives the relative extent to which the image of an object is magnified with respect to the object size. It is expressed as the ratio of the height of the image to the height of the object.”
What it means: m=hh′​=−uv​. A negative m means a real, inverted image; positive means virtual, erect.
Power of a lens & the dioptre
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 9,
“The power of a lens is defined as the reciprocal of its focal length. … The SI unit of power of a lens is 'dioptre'. It is denoted by the letter D. … 1 dioptre is the power of a lens whose focal length is 1 metre. 1D = 1 m⁻¹.”
What it means: P=f(metres)1​. Convex lens → positive power, concave → negative.
📖Key terms — NCERT verbatim (state word-for-word)
Pole
The centre of the reflecting surface of a spherical mirror is a point called the pole." (Ch 9, )
Centre of curvature
…This point is called the centre of curvature of the spherical mirror." (Ch 9, )
Radius of curvature
The radius of the sphere of which the reflecting surface of a spherical mirror forms a part, is called the radius of curvature of the mirror." (Ch 9, )
Principal axis
[A straight line passing through the pole and the centre of curvature…] This line is called the principal axis." (Ch 9, )
Aperture
The diameter of the reflecting surface of spherical mirror is called its aperture." (Ch 9, )
R = 2f
…the radius of curvature is found to be equal to twice the focal length. We put this as R = 2f." (Ch 9, )
Lens
A transparent material bound by two surfaces, of which one or both surfaces are spherical, forms a lens." (Ch 9, )
Optical centre
The central point of a lens is its optical centre. … A ray of light through the optical centre of a lens passes without suffering any deviation." (p.150–151) (Ch 9, )

Common mistakes that cost marks

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Sign convention errors — the #1 lost mark. Object distance u is always negative; concave f negative, convex f positive.
✕
Mixing mirror and lens magnification — mirror m=−v/u, lens m=+v/u.
✕
Forgetting to convert to metres for power — P=1/f needs f in metres, not cm.
✕
Not stating the nature — a numerical answer needs "real/virtual, erect/inverted, magnified/diminished" for full marks.

CBSE does not require the mirror/lens formula derivation here — the formal core is the sign convention and ray rules that make every numerical and diagram correct.

Distances (along principal axis)

  • •All distances measured from the pole / optical centre.
  • −Distances against the incident light (object side) are negative.
  • +Distances along the incident light are positive.

What this means in practice

  • −Object distance u is always negative (real object).
  • −Concave mirror / concave lens focal length: negative.
  • +Convex mirror / convex lens focal length: positive.
  • ±Heights above axis +, below axis −.

3 competency questions from the CBSE board pattern, with the full step-marked solution for each — the marks are shown per step, as the official scheme awards them.

2 marksSection B

Search mirrors are mirrors that are used to look for hidden objects underneath the cars. The hidden objects can be easily spotted as the mirror provides a wider field of view. (a) What type of mirrors are generally used to make search mirrors? (b) With the help of a ray diagram describe the nature of image formed by the type of mirror identified in (a).

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] (a) Convex mirrors — they diverge light and provide a wider field of view than plane or concave mirrors.
  2. [1 mark] (b) Image formed by a convex mirror is always virtual, erect, and diminished. Ray diagram: object in front; one ray parallel to axis appears to diverge from focus behind mirror; another ray towards centre of curvature reflects back along itself. Image forms behind mirror, between pole and focus.
3 marksSection C

Absolute refractive indices of two media P and Q are 1.33 (nP) and 2.52 (nQ) respectively. The speed of light in medium P is 2 × 10^8 m/s. (a) What would be the speed of light in medium Q (vQ)? (b) If the angle of incidence for a ray of light travelling from medium P to Q is 0°, then what will be the path of light in medium Q?

Step-marked solution
  1. [1 mark] nP = c/vP; nQ = c/vQ. Ratio: nP/nQ = vQ/vP ⟹ 1.33/2.52 = vQ / (2 × 10^8).
  2. [1 mark] vQ = (1.33 × 2 × 10^8) / 2.52 = 2.66 / 2.52 × 10^8 ≈ 1.056 × 10^8 m/s.
  3. [1 mark] (b) At normal incidence (angle of incidence = 0°), light enters perpendicular to the interface and travels undeviated (no refraction).
3 marksSection C

A 4 cm tall object is placed perpendicular to the principal axis of a convex lens of focal length 20 cm. The distance of the object from the lens is 15 cm. Find the nature, position and size of the image formed.

Step-marked solution
  1. [0.5 mark] Given: ho = 4 cm, u = -15 cm, f = +20 cm. Using lens formula 1/v - 1/u = 1/f.
  2. [1 mark] 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/20 + 1/(-15) = 3/60 - 4/60 = -1/60 ⟹ v = -60 cm.
  3. [1 mark] Magnification m = v/u = -60 / -15 = +4. So hi = m × ho = 4 × 4 = +16 cm.
  4. [0.5 mark] Nature: image is VIRTUAL, ERECT and MAGNIFIED (formed on the same side as the object, 60 cm from the lens).
