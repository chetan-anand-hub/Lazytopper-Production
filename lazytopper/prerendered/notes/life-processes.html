The Big Idea AUTHORED

Four processes keep a living body from falling apart - nutrition, respiration, transportation, excretion.

Every living body must take in food and energy, break it down to power its cells, move materials to where they are needed, and clear out the waste. In Class 10 these four life processes are examined mostly through labelled diagrams and stage-by-stage explanations, so most marks reward the exact NCERT terms and correctly drawn, correctly labelled structures.

Definitions the board rewards NCERT VERBATIM

State these in NCERT’s exact words

Life processes
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“The processes which together perform this maintenance job are life processes.”
What it means: The basic functions - nutrition, respiration, transportation, excretion - that every living body runs continuously to stay organised and alive.
Nutrition
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“So there must be a process to transfer a source of energy from outside the body of the organism, which we call food, to the inside, a process we commonly call nutrition.”
What it means: Taking in food from outside to supply the body with energy and raw materials.
Autotrophic nutrition (photosynthesis)
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“Carbon and energy requirements of the autotrophic organism are fulfilled by photosynthesis. It is the process by which autotrophs take in substances from the outside and convert them into stored forms of energy.”
What it means: Green plants and some bacteria make their own food from CO2 and water using sunlight and chlorophyll.
Respiration
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“The process of acquiring oxygen from outside the body, and to use it in the process of break-down of food sources for cellular needs, is what we call respiration.”
What it means: Breaking down glucose (usually with oxygen) inside cells to release energy for life activities.
Transportation
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“This situation creates a need for a transportation system for carrying food and oxygen from one place to another in the body.”
What it means: The system (blood in animals, vascular tissue in plants) that moves food, oxygen and wastes between the parts that supply them and the parts that need them.
Excretion
📖NCERT verbatim — memorise this exact wording· Ch 5,
“The biological process involved in the removal of these harmful metabolic wastes from the body is called excretion.”
What it means: Getting rid of the poisonous nitrogenous wastes (like urea) that the body's chemical reactions produce.

Must-know concepts AUTHORED FRAMING

…and where they’re tested

1
Autotrophic vs heterotrophic nutrition
Autotrophs (green plants, some bacteria) build their own food from inorganic CO2 and water; heterotrophs (animals, fungi) take in the complex food made by others and break it down with enzymes.
3-mark: differentiate autotrophic and heterotrophic nutrition with examples.
2
Photosynthesis
In the leaf, chlorophyll in chloroplasts absorbs light; the three events are absorption of light energy, conversion to chemical energy plus splitting of water, and reduction of CO2 to carbohydrate. Raw materials: CO2 (through stomata), water (from roots) and sunlight.
necessary conditions and by-products of autotrophic nutrition; role of stomata and guard cells.
3
Human digestive system
A long alimentary canal (mouth to oesophagus to stomach to small intestine to large intestine to anus) with region-specific enzymes; the small intestine is the site of complete digestion and its villi maximise absorption.
5-mark: label the human alimentary canal and name the enzymes and their functions; role of saliva / HCl / bile.
4
Respiration - aerobic vs anaerobic
Glucose (6 carbon) first splits to pyruvate (3 carbon) in the cytoplasm; then either aerobic (in mitochondria, giving CO2 + water and more energy) or anaerobic (yeast gives ethanol + CO2; muscle gives lactic acid, causing cramps).
differences between aerobic and anaerobic respiration with organisms; site of pyruvate breakdown.
5
Human circulatory system and double circulation
The four-chambered heart keeps oxygenated and de-oxygenated blood separate; blood (plasma plus cells) is pumped through arteries, capillaries and veins and passes through the heart twice per cycle.
5-mark: describe double circulation and why it is necessary; why oxygenated and de-oxygenated blood are separated in birds and mammals.
6
Transport in plants - xylem and phloem
Xylem carries water and minerals upward from the roots (root pressure plus transpiration pull); phloem translocates soluble food from the leaves in both directions, using energy from ATP.
differences between transport in xylem and phloem; how water and how food are transported in plants.
7
Excretion in humans - the nephron
Kidneys filter nitrogenous wastes (urea, uric acid); each nephron performs filtration in the Bowman's capsule then selective re-absorption along the tubule; urine passes through ureters to the bladder and out via the urethra.
5-mark: structure and functioning of the nephron; how the amount of urine is regulated; compare alveoli and nephron.

Example Fig. 5.5 walkthrough,
“Trace how an Amoeba ingests and digests its food. [authored prompt]”
1
Ingestion.Amoeba takes in food using temporary finger-like extensions (pseudopodia) of the cell surface, which fuse over the food particle to form a food-vacuole.
1 mark
2
Digestion.Inside the food-vacuole, complex substances are broken down into simpler ones.
1 mark
3
Absorption.The simpler substances then diffuse into the cytoplasm.
4
Egestion.The remaining undigested material is moved to the surface of the cell and thrown out.
1 mark
Mark-scheme logic (authored): CBSE 3-mark - 1 mark each for pseudopodia/food-vacuole (ingestion), intracellular digestion, and egestion of undigested matter. Absorption (diffusion into the cytoplasm) is part of the process but is not a separately-credited step in the 3-mark scheme.

Problem-solving strategies AUTHORED
→
Diagram question?Draw it large, label with straight ruled lines, and use exact NCERT terms - write "oesophagus" not "food pipe", "alveoli" not "air sacs".
→
"Differences between" asked?Answer in a two-column table (xylem vs phloem, aerobic vs anaerobic, arteries vs veins) - one clear point per row scores faster than a paragraph.
→
Enzyme / organ question?Pair every organ with its secretion and function - saliva to amylase to starch, stomach to pepsin + HCl to protein, pancreas to trypsin/lipase - examiners award the enzyme name AND its substrate separately.
→
"Why is it necessary?" add-on?Board answers want the reason, not just the process - e.g. double circulation is needed to stop oxygenated and de-oxygenated blood mixing, giving an efficient O2 supply for high energy needs.
Common mistakes that cost marks

Where students lose easy marks

✕
Writing "food pipe" instead of oesophagus, or "wind pipe / air sacs" instead of trachea / alveoli - CBSE demands the exact technical term.
✕
Confusing xylem and phloem: xylem = water + minerals, roots upward (one-way); phloem = food, both directions. Reversing them loses the whole mark.
✕
Mislabelling heart chambers - putting oxygenated blood on the right, or swapping atria and ventricles. Remember: LEFT side = oxygen-rich, and ventricles have thicker walls than atria.
✕
Saying anaerobic respiration in muscles gives ethanol - in human muscle cells pyruvate becomes lactic acid (ethanol + CO2 is only in yeast/fermentation).
✕
Describing filtration only and forgetting that in the nephron useful substances (glucose, amino acids, salts, water) are RE-ABSORBED.
Formula strip
Photosynthesis (standard equation)
6CO2 + 6H2O --(sunlight, chlorophyll)--> C6H12O6 + 6O2  (standard CBSE equation - NCERT states the three events, not this balanced line)
Glucose breakdown
Glucose (6C) to Pyruvate (3C) in cytoplasm; then aerobic to CO2 + H2O + energy, or anaerobic - yeast to ethanol + CO2, muscle to lactic acid
ATP
ATP is the energy currency of the cell; breaking its terminal phosphate bond releases about 30.5 kJ/mol
Heart chambers
4 chambers - left/right atrium + left/right ventricle. Fish 2-chambered, amphibians/many reptiles 3-chambered, birds & mammals 4-chambered
Blood pressure
Systolic about 120 mm Hg, diastolic about 80 mm Hg (measured with a sphygmomanometer)
Alveolar surface
Spread out, the alveolar surface would cover about 80 m2 - maximising gas exchange
Kidney filtrate
About 180 L of initial filtrate a day, but only 1-2 L excreted - the rest is re-absorbed
Mindmap

      • Heterotrophic - animals & fungi; enzymes break down complex food
      • Human digestion - mouth to stomach to small intestine; villi absorb
      • Amoeba - pseudopodia + food-vacuole
      • Glucose to pyruvate (cytoplasm)
      • Aerobic (mitochondria) to CO2 + water + more energy
      • Anaerobic - yeast to ethanol + CO2; muscle to lactic acid
      • Energy stored as ATP
      • Human - 4-chambered heart, double circulation
      • Blood vessels - arteries, veins, capillaries
      • Plants - xylem (water, up) & phloem (food, both ways)
      • Transpiration pull + root pressure
      • Human - kidney, nephron (filtration + re-absorption)
      • Path - ureter to bladder to urethra
      • Plants - waste in vacuoles, resins/gums, falling leaves
