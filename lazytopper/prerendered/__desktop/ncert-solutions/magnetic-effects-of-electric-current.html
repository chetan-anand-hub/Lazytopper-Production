In-text Q1 · Section 12.2.42 marks
Consider a circular loop of wire lying in the plane of the table. Let the current pass through the loop clockwise. Apply the right-hand rule to find out the direction of the magnetic field inside and outside the loop.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Apply the right-hand thumb rule: curl the fingers of the right hand in the direction of current flow.
  2. Current is clockwise as seen from above the table, so when fingers curl clockwise, the thumb points downwards (into the table).
  3. Hence inside the loop the magnetic field points downwards (into the table).
  4. Field lines are closed curves, so outside the loop the field points upwards (out of the table).

Answer: Inside the loop: into the table (downwards). Outside the loop: out of the table (upwards).

In-text Q3 · Section 12.2.41 mark
Choose the correct option. The magnetic field inside a long straight solenoid-carrying current

Step-by-step solution

  1. Inside a long current-carrying solenoid, the field lines are parallel straight lines.
  2. This means the magnetic field has the same magnitude and direction at every interior point — the field is uniform.
  3. Hence option (d) is correct.

Answer: Option (d): is the same at all points.

In-text Q1 · Section 12.42 marks
Name two safety measures commonly used in electric circuits and appliances.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Safety measure 1 — Electric fuse: a low-melting-point wire in series with the live wire; melts and breaks the circuit when current exceeds the safe limit (overloading / short-circuit).
  2. Safety measure 2 — Earthing: metallic body of the appliance is connected via the green earth wire to a metal plate buried in the ground.
  3. Earthing provides a low-resistance path for any leakage current → user does not get a shock.
  4. Together, fuse + earthing protect both the appliance and the user.

Answer: Electric fuse (protects against overloading/short-circuit) and earthing (protects user from shock).

In-text Q2 · Section 12.43 marks
An electric oven of 2 kW power rating is operated in a domestic electric circuit (220 V) that has a current rating of 5 A. What result do you expect? Explain.
Step 1 Use P = V × I → I = P / V.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Use P = V × I → I = P / V.
  2. Substitute: I = 2000 W ÷ 220 V ≈ 9.09 A.
  3. Circuit's safe current rating is 5 A, but the oven draws ≈ 9.09 A, which is well above the limit.
  4. The circuit is overloaded → the fuse melts and breaks the circuit; otherwise wires overheat and may cause a fire.
  5. Hence the oven must be operated on a higher-rating (e.g., 15 A) circuit.

Answer: Oven draws ≈ 9.09 A > 5 A rating → overloading → fuse blows. Use a 15 A circuit instead.

Worked examples

Example 12.13 marks
A current through a horizontal power line flows in east to west direction. What is the direction of magnetic field at a point directly below it and at a point directly above it?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Apply the right-hand thumb rule: thumb in the direction of current (east → west, so thumb points west).
  2. Fingers curl around the wire, giving the magnetic-field direction at any point around the wire.
  3. At a point directly BELOW the wire, the curling fingers point from north to south → field is directed towards SOUTH.
  4. At a point directly ABOVE the wire, the curling fingers point from south to north → field is directed towards NORTH.
  5. Viewed from the east end the field rotates clockwise; viewed from the west end it rotates anti-clockwise.

Answer: Below the wire: towards South. Above the wire: towards North.

Exercise Q32 marks
State whether the following statements are true or false. (a) The field at the centre of a long circular coil carrying current will be parallel straight lines. (b) A wire with a green insulation is usually the live wire of an electric supply.

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Inside a long solenoid, field lines are parallel straight lines → uniform field → TRUE.
  2. (b) In standard domestic wiring colours: red = live, black = neutral, green = earth.
  3. Green wire is therefore the earth wire, not the live wire → FALSE.

Answer: (a) True. (b) False — green is the earth wire.

Exercise Q42 marks
List two methods of producing magnetic fields.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Method 1: A permanent magnet (e.g., bar magnet, horse-shoe magnet) produces a magnetic field in its surrounding region.
  2. Method 2: An electric current flowing through a conductor (straight wire / circular loop / solenoid) creates a magnetic field around it.
  3. The second method is the basis of electromagnets — a solenoid with a soft-iron core.

Answer: (1) Permanent magnet; (2) electric current flowing through a conductor (e.g., solenoid → electromagnet).

Exercise Q63 marks
Imagine that you are sitting in a chamber with your back to one wall. An electron beam, moving horizontally from back wall towards the front wall, is deflected by a strong magnetic field to your right side. What is the direction of magnetic field?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Electron motion is from back wall to front wall, so conventional current direction (opposite to electron motion) is from front to back.
  2. Force on the beam is towards the right of the observer.
  3. Apply Fleming's left-hand rule: middle finger = conventional current (front → back); thumb = force (right).
  4. The first finger (magnetic field) then points vertically DOWNWARDS.
  5. Hence the magnetic field is directed vertically downwards.

Answer: Vertically downwards.

Exercise Q82 marks
When does an electric short circuit occur?

Step-by-step solution

  1. A short circuit occurs when the live (red) and neutral (black) wires touch each other directly — bypassing any appliance load.
  2. Typical causes: damaged insulation on wires, or a fault inside an appliance.
  3. The resistance between the wires becomes very low, so the current rises abruptly (I = V/R).
  4. This very high current melts the fuse (Joule heating) and breaks the circuit — preventing fire.

Answer: When live and neutral wires touch each other directly (insulation damage or appliance fault), causing a sudden surge of current.

Exercise Q93 marks
What is the function of an earth wire? Why is it necessary to earth metallic appliances?

Step-by-step solution

  1. The earth wire (green insulation) is connected to a metal plate buried deep in the ground.
  2. It provides a low-resistance conducting path from the appliance's metal body to the earth.
  3. If the live wire ever touches the appliance's metallic body, the leakage current flows to earth (low resistance) instead of through the user.
  4. The body of the appliance is thus kept at earth potential → no shock to the user even on accidental contact.

Answer: Earth wire provides a safe low-resistance path for leakage current to flow to ground, keeping the appliance body at earth potential and preventing electric shocks.
