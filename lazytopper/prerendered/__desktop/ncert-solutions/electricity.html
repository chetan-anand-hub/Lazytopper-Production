In-text Q1 · Section 11.12 marks
What does an electric circuit mean?
Step 1 [1 mark] An electric circuit is a continuous and closed conducting path of an electric current.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] An electric circuit is a continuous and closed conducting path of an electric current.
  2. [0.5 mark] It comprises a source of potential difference (cell/battery), connecting wires, a switch (plug key) and electrical components.
  3. [0.5 mark] Current flows only when the path is closed; opening the switch breaks the circuit and stops the current.

Answer: A continuous and closed conducting path along which an electric current flows.

  4. [0.5 mark] Hence about 6.25 × 10¹⁸ electrons constitute one coulomb of charge.

In-text Q2 · Section 11.52 marks
Will current flow more easily through a thick wire or a thin wire of the same material, when connected to the same source? Why?
Step 1 [0.5 mark] For a given material and length, R = ρl/A, so R is inversely proportional to area of cross-section.

  2. [0.5 mark] Thicker wire ⇒ larger A ⇒ smaller R.
  3. [0.5 mark] By Ohm's law, I = V/R; smaller R at the same V gives larger I.

Answer: Through the thick wire — larger area of cross-section gives lower resistance and hence higher current.

In-text Q3 · Section 11.52 marks
Let the resistance of an electrical component remains constant while the potential difference across the two ends of the component decreases to half of its former value. What change will occur in the current through it?
Step 1 [0.5 mark] Ohm's law: I = V/R. Here R remains constant.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] Ohm's law: I = V/R. Here R remains constant.
  2. [0.5 mark] New potential difference V' = V/2.
  3. [0.5 mark] New current I' = V'/R = (V/2)/R = (1/2)(V/R) = I/2.
  4. [0.5 mark] Hence the current through the component reduces to half of its initial value.

Answer: Current becomes half of its former value.

Judge the equivalent resistance when the following are connected in parallel – (a) 1 Ω and 10⁶ Ω, (b) 1 Ω and 10³ Ω, and 10⁶ Ω.

In-text Q3 · Section 11.6.23 marks
What are the advantages of connecting electrical devices in parallel with the battery instead of connecting them in series?
Step 1 [1 mark] In parallel, each device gets the same (full) supply voltage and operates at its rated power.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] In parallel, each device gets the same (full) supply voltage and operates at its rated power.
  2. [0.5 mark] Each branch carries the current required by its device; devices with very different resistances/ratings can coexist.
  3. [1 mark] If one device is switched off or fails, the others in other branches continue to function (the circuit is not broken).
  4. [0.5 mark] The total resistance is lower than any single device, so more current can be supplied to drive multiple appliances.

Answer: Each device runs at full voltage and is independently controllable; failure of one doesn't break the rest.

In-text Q4 · Section 11.6.25 marks
How can three resistors of resistances 2 Ω, 3 Ω, and 6 Ω be connected to give a total resistance of (a) 4 Ω, (b) 1 Ω?

  4. [1 mark] 1/R = 1/2 + 1/3 + 1/6 = 3/6 + 2/6 + 1/6 = 6/6 = 1.
  5. [1 mark] Therefore R = 1 Ω.

Answer: (a) 2 Ω in series with parallel of (3 Ω, 6 Ω) → 4 Ω. (b) All three in parallel → 1 Ω.

In-text Q1 · Section 11.72 marks
Why does the cord of an electric heater not glow while the heating element does?
Step 1 [0.5 mark] The cord (copper, low resistivity) and the heating element (nichrome, high resistivity) carry the same current I in series.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] The cord (copper, low resistivity) and the heating element (nichrome, high resistivity) carry the same current I in series.
  2. [0.5 mark] By Joule's law H = I²Rt, the heat produced is proportional to resistance for the same I and t.
  3. [0.5 mark] The element has a high resistance, so most of the heat is dissipated there and it becomes red-hot and glows.
  4. [0.5 mark] The cord has a very low resistance, so very little heat is produced and it remains cool and does not glow.

Answer: Heating element has high resistance (alloy) → I²R heat very large, glows; cord (low-R copper) dissipates negligible heat.

In-text Q1 · Section 11.82 marks
What determines the rate at which energy is delivered by a current?
Step 1 [0.5 mark] Rate of energy delivery = electric power P.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] Rate of energy delivery = electric power P.
  2. [0.5 mark] P = V × I (work done per unit time per unit charge × charge per second).
  3. [0.5 mark] Equivalently, using Ohm's law, P = I²R = V²/R.
  4. [0.5 mark] Thus the rate is determined by the potential difference and the current (or by R for a given V).

Answer: Electric power P = VI = I²R = V²/R determines the rate.

Step 1 [1 mark] (a) Tungsten's very high melting point (~3380 °C) lets its filament become red-hot/white-hot without melting; it emits enough light when hot.
