In-text Q2 · Section 4.12 marks
What would be the electron dot structure of a molecule of sulphur which is made up of eight atoms of sulphur? (Hint — The eight atoms of sulphur are joined together in the form of a ring.)

Step-by-step solution

  1. S has 6 valence electrons. To complete octet, each S forms 2 covalent bonds.
  2. 8 S atoms link to form a closed RING via single S–S bonds.
  3. Each S has 2 lone pairs in addition to the 2 bonding pairs — total 8 e⁻ around each S.

Answer: S₈ ring with single S–S bonds; each S has 2 bonded pairs + 2 lone pairs.

In-text Q2 · Section 4.22 marks
What are the two properties of carbon which lead to the huge number of carbon compounds we see around us?

Step-by-step solution

  1. (i) Catenation: C–C bonds are very strong, allowing chains, branches, rings.
  2. (ii) Tetravalency: C forms 4 covalent bonds, can combine with many elements, giving huge structural diversity.

Answer: Catenation and tetravalency.

In-text Q3 · Section 4.22 marks
What will be the formula and electron dot structure of cyclopentane?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Cyclopentane = saturated cyclic hydrocarbon with 5 C atoms.
  2. General formula for cycloalkane: CₙH₂ₙ → C₅H₁₀.
  3. Each C in the ring has 2 H atoms. All bonds shown as electron dot pairs: every C shares 1 pair with each neighbour C and each H.

Answer: C₅H₁₀ — 5-carbon ring, each C bonded to 2 H atoms, all single bonds.

Exercise Q43 marks
Explain the nature of the covalent bond using the bond formation in CH₃Cl.

Step-by-step solution

  1. C has 4 valence e⁻ (needs 4 more for octet); each H has 1 e⁻ (needs 1 more for duplet); Cl has 7 e⁻ (needs 1 more for octet).
  2. C forms 3 single bonds with H (one e⁻ from each) and 1 single bond with Cl.
  3. Each bond = 1 shared pair (covalent bond). C ends with 8 e⁻; Cl with 8 e⁻; each H with 2 e⁻.
  4. Nature: shared-pair covalent bonds, no ion formation.

Answer: CH₃Cl: 3 C–H bonds + 1 C–Cl bond, all shared-pair covalent bonds.

Exercise Q63 marks
What is an homologous series? Explain with an example.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Homologous series = compounds with same functional group + same general formula, differing by CH₂.
  2. Example: Alkane series CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. Members CH₄, C₂H₆, C₃H₈, C₄H₁₀, C₅H₁₂.
  3. Each consecutive member differs by +CH₂ (mass 14).
  4. Properties: similar chemical behaviour; m.p./b.p. gradually increase with n.

Answer: Series of compounds with same functional group + general formula, differing by CH₂. Example: alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

Exercise Q82 marks
Why does micelle formation take place when soap is added to water? Will a micelle be formed in other solvents such as ethanol also?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Soap = ionic head + hydrocarbon tail. Water = polar; tails hate water.
  2. Tails cluster inside, heads outside → micelle.
  3. In ethanol: ethanol can dissolve both polar and non-polar groups → no incompatibility → no micelles.

Answer: Micelles form in water due to hydrophobic effect. NOT in ethanol (no incompatibility).

Exercise Q103 marks
Explain the formation of scum when hard water is treated with soap.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Hard water has Ca²⁺ and Mg²⁺ ions.
  2. Soap = sodium salt of fatty acid. The Na⁺ gets exchanged with Ca²⁺/Mg²⁺.
  3. Insoluble (RCOO)₂Ca and (RCOO)₂Mg precipitate as SCUM (white curdy solid).
  4. Result: soap is wasted; cloth doesn't get clean unless enough soap is used to first remove all Ca/Mg.

Answer: Soap + hard water → insoluble Ca/Mg salts of fatty acid = scum. Wastes soap.

Exercise Q132 marks
Which of the following hydrocarbons undergo addition reactions: C₂H₆, C₃H₈, C₃H₆, C₂H₂ and CH₄.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Addition reactions need unsaturation (double or triple bond).
  2. Saturated (alkanes): C₂H₆, C₃H₈, CH₄ → undergo substitution, NOT addition.
  3. Unsaturated: C₃H₆ (alkene), C₂H₂ (alkyne) → undergo addition reactions.

Answer: C₃H₆ and C₂H₂ undergo addition reactions; C₂H₆, C₃H₈, CH₄ do not.

Exercise Q142 marks
Give a test that can be used to differentiate between saturated and unsaturated hydrocarbons.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Bromine water test: add a few drops of orange Br₂(aq) to each sample.
  2. Unsaturated: decolourised (Br₂ adds to C=C; e.g., CH₂=CH₂ + Br₂ → CH₂Br–CH₂Br).
  3. Saturated: no decolourisation (no addition possible).
  4. Alternative: alkaline KMnO₄ (purple → colourless with unsaturated).

Answer: Bromine-water test: decolourises with unsaturated; no change with saturated.

Exercise Q155 marks
Explain the mechanism of the cleaning action of soaps.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Soap has dual nature: ionic hydrophilic head (-COO⁻Na⁺) + hydrocarbon hydrophobic tail.
  2. Tails attach to oil/grease (dirt is held on cloth by greasy film); heads face the water.
  3. Many soap molecules form a sphere — a MICELLE — with dirt enclosed at the centre and ionic heads outside.
  4. Micelles are stable in water (suspended as colloidal particles).
  5. Agitation (rubbing, machine action) dislodges the micelles from cloth fibres.
  6. Rinsing carries away the suspended micelles → cloth becomes clean.

Answer: Soap tails grab oil/dirt forming micelles; heads dissolve in water; agitation dislodges micelles; rinse takes them away.
