In-text Q1 · Section 1.12 marks
Why should a magnesium ribbon be cleaned before burning in air?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Magnesium metal in air slowly reacts with O₂ to form a thin protective layer of magnesium oxide (MgO) on its surface.
  2. [0.5 mark] This oxide layer is inert and prevents the metal beneath from coming in contact with oxygen during burning.
  3. [0.5 mark] Cleaning the ribbon with sandpaper removes the MgO layer, exposing fresh magnesium that ignites readily to give the characteristic dazzling white flame.

Answer: To remove the protective MgO layer so fresh magnesium can burn readily.

In-text Q1 · Section 1.2.23 marks
A solution of a substance 'X' is used for whitewashing. (i) Name the substance 'X' and write its formula. (ii) Write the reaction of the substance 'X' named in (i) above with water.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Quick lime (calcium oxide, CaO) is used for whitewashing because it reacts with water to form slaked lime, Ca(OH)₂.
  2. [0.5 mark] The slaked lime is then applied to walls. It slowly reacts with CO₂ in air to form CaCO₃ (a thin shiny layer) over two-three days.
  3. [0.5 mark] Reaction of CaO with water: CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq).
  4. [1 mark] This is a combination reaction and is highly exothermic (heat released, the container feels warm).

Answer: X = CaO (quick lime); CaO(s) + H₂O(l) → Ca(OH)₂(aq) + Heat

In-text Q1 · Section 1.2.52 marks
Why does the colour of copper sulphate solution change when an iron nail is dipped in it?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Iron is placed above copper in the reactivity series, so Fe can displace Cu from its salt solution.
  2. [0.5 mark] Reaction: Fe(s) + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s) — a displacement reaction.
  3. [0.5 mark] Blue CuSO₄ is consumed (colour fades). Greenish FeSO₄ forms; brown metallic copper coats the iron nail.

Answer: Fe displaces Cu from CuSO₄: Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. Blue fades; greenish FeSO₄ and brown Cu deposit appear.

In-text Q2 · Section 1.2.52 marks
Give an example of a double displacement reaction other than the one given in Activity 1.10.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] In a double displacement reaction, two compounds exchange ions to form two new compounds.
  2. [0.5 mark] Example: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq).
  3. [0.5 mark] Ag⁺ goes from AgNO₃ to AgCl; Na⁺ goes from NaCl to NaNO₃. An insoluble white precipitate of AgCl forms — this is also a precipitation reaction.

Answer: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s) + NaNO₃(aq)

Exercise Q21 mark
Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → Al₂O₃ + 2Fe The above reaction is an example of a:

Step-by-step solution

  1. Al is more reactive than Fe — Al displaces Fe from Fe₂O₃.
  2. Only one element (Al) replaces another element (Fe) from its compound — this is a displacement reaction.
  3. It is also exothermic and is famously known as the thermite reaction. Answer: (D)

Answer: displacement reaction

Exercise Q31 mark
What happens when dilute hydrochloric acid is added to iron filings? Tick the correct answer.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Iron is more reactive than hydrogen and displaces H from dilute HCl.
  2. Reaction: Fe(s) + 2HCl(aq) → FeCl₂(aq) + H₂(g).
  3. Products: iron(II) chloride and hydrogen gas (bubbles). Answer: (A)

Answer: Hydrogen gas and iron chloride are produced.

Exercise Q63 marks
Balance the following chemical equations. (a) HNO₃ + Ca(OH)₂ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + H₂O (b) NaOH + H₂SO₄ → Na₂SO₄ + H₂O (c) NaCl + AgNO₃ → AgCl + NaNO₃ (d) BaCl₂ + H₂SO₄ → BaSO₄ + HCl

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] (a) NO₃: LHS=1, RHS=2 → put 2 HNO₃. H: LHS=2(HNO₃)+2(OH)=4 → put 2 H₂O on RHS. Check: Ca 1=1, NO₃ 2=2, H 4=4, O balanced.
  2. [0.5 mark] (b) Na: LHS=1, RHS=2 → put 2 NaOH. H: LHS=2+2=4 → put 2 H₂O. SO₄ 1=1 ✓.
  3. [0.5 mark] (c) Each side: 1 Na, 1 Cl, 1 Ag, 1 NO₃ — already balanced.
  4. [1 mark] (d) Cl: LHS=2 → put 2 HCl. H: LHS=2 (H₂SO₄), RHS=2 ✓. Ba 1=1, SO₄ 1=1.

Answer: (a) 2HNO₃+Ca(OH)₂→Ca(NO₃)₂+2H₂O (b) 2NaOH+H₂SO₄→Na₂SO₄+2H₂O (c) already balanced (d) BaCl₂+H₂SO₄→BaSO₄+2HCl

Exercise Q142 marks
In the refining of silver, the recovery of silver from silver nitrate solution involved displacement by copper metal. Write down the reaction involved.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Copper lies above silver in the reactivity series → Cu can displace Ag from its salt.
  2. [0.5 mark] Cu(s) + 2AgNO₃(aq) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2Ag(s).
  3. [0.5 mark] The solution turns blue (formation of Cu(NO₃)₂); pure silver precipitates out.

Answer: Cu(s) + 2AgNO₃(aq) → Cu(NO₃)₂(aq) + 2Ag(s) — displacement reaction.

Exercise Q153 marks
What do you mean by a precipitation reaction? Explain by giving examples.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] When solutions of two ionic compounds are mixed and an insoluble salt is formed, the insoluble salt settles out as a precipitate.
  2. [0.5 mark] Example 1: BaCl₂(aq) + Na₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ (white precipitate) + 2NaCl(aq).
  3. [0.5 mark] Example 2: AgNO₃(aq) + NaCl(aq) → AgCl(s)↓ (white curdy precipitate) + NaNO₃(aq).
  4. [1 mark] Precipitation reactions are usually double-displacement reactions where one of the products is insoluble in water.

Answer: A reaction producing an insoluble solid (precipitate) from two soluble solutions. E.g. BaCl₂+Na₂SO₄→BaSO₄↓+2NaCl.

Exercise Q172 marks
A shiny brown coloured element 'X' on heating in air becomes black in colour. Name the element 'X' and the black coloured compound formed.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] A shiny brown metal that turns black on heating in air is copper.
  2. [0.5 mark] On heating: 2Cu(s) + O₂(g) → 2CuO(s). The black coating is copper(II) oxide.
  3. [0.5 mark] This is an oxidation reaction (Cu gains oxygen).

Answer: X = Copper (Cu); black compound = Copper(II) oxide (CuO).
