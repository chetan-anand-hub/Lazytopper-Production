Exercise Q21 mark
Which of the following constitute a food-chain?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Grass (producer) is eaten by the goat (herbivore), which is eaten by humans (consumer), so Grass → Goat → Human is a food chain; (a) has only producers, (c) only herbivores, and in (d) the goat does not eat fish — option (b). Answer: (B)

Answer: Grass, goat and human — option (b)

Exercise Q43 marks
What will happen if we kill all the organisms in one trophic level?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] The food chain breaks: organisms of the next higher trophic level lose their food and will starve and die (or migrate).
  2. [1 mark] Organisms of the lower trophic level are no longer eaten, so their population increases greatly (e.g. killing all frogs: snakes starve, grasshoppers multiply and damage the grass).
  3. [1 mark] Hence the ecological balance of the ecosystem is disturbed; if the producers are killed, all the consumers die.

Answer: The food chain breaks: the next higher level starves, the lower level multiplies unchecked, and the ecological balance is upset.

Exercise Q53 marks
Will the impact of removing all the organisms in a trophic level be different for different trophic levels? Can the organisms of any trophic level be removed without causing any damage to the ecosystem?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Yes, the impact differs: removing producers is the most damaging — no energy enters the ecosystem and all consumers die.
  2. [1 mark] Removing herbivores: producers grow unchecked and carnivores starve; removing top carnivores: herbivores multiply and overgraze; removing decomposers: wastes pile up and nutrients are not recycled.
  3. [1 mark] No — no trophic level can be removed without damage, because all trophic levels are interdependent and the ecological balance is disturbed.

Answer: Yes, the impact differs from level to level (worst for producers); no trophic level can be removed without damaging the ecosystem, as all levels are interdependent.

Exercise Q63 marks
What is biological magnification? Will the levels of this magnification be different at different levels of the ecosystem?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Biological magnification: progressive increase in the concentration of harmful non-biodegradable chemicals (e.g. pesticides) at successive trophic levels of a food chain.
  2. [1 mark] Yes, the levels differ: since the chemicals are not degraded, they accumulate progressively at each trophic level.
  3. [1 mark] Concentration is lowest in producers and highest at the top trophic level — maximum in human beings, who occupy the top of food chains.

Answer: Accumulation of non-biodegradable chemicals at increasing concentration along a food chain; yes — lowest in producers, highest at the top level (humans).

Exercise Q93 marks
Why is damage to the ozone layer a cause for concern? What steps are being taken to limit this damage?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] The ozone layer shields the earth from the Sun's harmful UV radiation; damage lets more UV reach the earth.
  2. [1 mark] UV radiation causes skin cancer (and eye damage) in humans and harms plants and other organisms; the damage is linked to CFCs used as refrigerants and in fire extinguishers.
  3. [1 mark] Steps: the 1987 UNEP agreement to freeze CFC production at 1986 levels, and making CFC-free refrigerators mandatory throughout the world.

Answer: Ozone depletion lets harmful UV radiation reach the earth (skin cancer, harm to organisms); CFC production was frozen at 1986 levels by the 1987 UNEP agreement and CFC-free refrigerators are now mandatory.
