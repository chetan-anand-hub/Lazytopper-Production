Multiple choice questions

Exemplar Q11 mark
Exchange of genetic material takes place in

Step-by-step solution

  1. In sexual reproduction, gametes from two parents fuse → genetic material is exchanged/combined.
  2. Vegetative, asexual, and budding involve a single parent → no genetic exchange. Answer: (C)

Answer: Sexual reproduction — option (c).

Exemplar Q31 mark
A cross between a tall plant (TT) and short pea plant (tt) resulted in progeny that were all tall plants because

Step-by-step solution

  1. TT × tt → all F1 are Tt.
  2. If all Tt are tall → T (tallness) masks t (shortness).
  3. Hence tallness is dominant. Answer: (A)

Answer: Tallness is dominant — option (a).

Exemplar Q61 mark
In human males all the chromosomes are paired perfectly except one. This/these unpaired chromosome is/are (i) large chromosome (ii) small chromosome (iii) Y-chromosome (iv) X-chromosome

Step-by-step solution

  1. Human males have XY as the sex chromosome pair.
  2. X is a normal-sized chromosome and Y is much shorter — so the pair is mismatched.
  3. Both X and Y are 'unpaired' in the sense that they are not identical partners.
  4. Option (c): (iii) and (iv). Answer: (C)

Answer: Option (c) — (iii) and (iv).

Exemplar Q71 mark
The maleness of a child is determined by

Step-by-step solution

  1. Boys are XY → presence of the Y chromosome is what makes the zygote develop as male.
  2. X comes from mother always; Y comes only from father → maleness is set by inheritance of Y. Answer: (B)

Answer: Y chromosome in zygote — option (b).

Exemplar Q81 mark
A zygote which has an X-chromosome inherited from the father will develop into a

Step-by-step solution

  1. Mother always gives X. If father also gives X → zygote XX → female.
  2. If father gives Y → zygote XY → male.
  3. So inheriting X from the father → girl. Answer: (B)

Answer: Girl — option (b).

Exemplar Q303 marks
In human beings, the statistical probability of getting either a male or female child is 50 : 50. Give a suitable explanation.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Father (XY) produces equal numbers of X-bearing and Y-bearing sperm via meiosis.
  2. Mother (XX) produces only X-bearing eggs.
  3. Fertilisation is random — any sperm has equal chance of fusing with the egg.
  4. P(X-sperm fertilises) = 50% → girl; P(Y-sperm fertilises) = 50% → boy.
  5. Hence the 50:50 statistical probability of male/female children.

Answer: Father produces 50% X- and 50% Y-sperm; random fertilisation → 50:50 boy:girl probability.

Exemplar Q342 marks
Give the pair of contrasting traits of the following characters in pea plant and mention which is dominant and recessive: (i) yellow seed (ii) round seed

Step-by-step solution

  1. Mendel selected pea characters with two contrasting forms each.
  2. (i) Yellow seed (dominant) vs Green seed (recessive) — Y vs y.
  3. (ii) Round seed (dominant) vs Wrinkled seed (recessive) — R vs r.
  4. Dominance was established by F1 showing only the dominant form.

Answer: (i) Yellow (dominant) / Green (recessive). (ii) Round (dominant) / Wrinkled (recessive).
