Exercise 9.1

Exemplar Ex 9.1 Q11 mark
If radii of two concentric circles are 4 cm and 5 cm, then the length of each chord of one circle which is tangent to the other circle is
Step 1 Let AB be a chord of the larger circle tangent to the smaller circle at P. OP ⊥ AB, OP = 4.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let AB be a chord of the larger circle tangent to the smaller circle at P. OP ⊥ AB, OP = 4.
  2. OA = 5. In right ∆OPA: AP² = 25 − 16 = 9 ⇒ AP = 3.
  3. AB = 2 × AP = 6 cm.

Answer: 6 cm — option (b).

Exemplar Ex 9.1 Q91 mark
If two tangents inclined at an angle 60° are drawn to a circle of radius 3 cm, then the length of each tangent is
Step 1 Let T be the external point. OT bisects the 60° angle, so ∠OTP = 30°.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let T be the external point. OT bisects the 60° angle, so ∠OTP = 30°.
  2. In right ∆OPT (right-angled at P): tan 30° = OP/TP ⇒ TP = OP/tan 30° = 3/(1/√3) = 3√3 cm.

Answer: 3√3 cm — option (d).

Exemplar Ex 9.1 Q41 mark
From a point P at a distance of 13 cm from the centre O of a circle of radius 5 cm, the pair of tangents PQ and PR are drawn. The area of the quadrilateral PQOR is
Step 1 PQ = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm. Similarly PR = 12 cm.

Step-by-step solution

  1. PQ = √(13² − 5²) = √144 = 12 cm. Similarly PR = 12 cm.
  2. Each of ∆PQO and ∆PRO is right-angled with legs 5 and 12.
  3. Area of one such triangle = (1/2) × 5 × 12 = 30 cm².
  4. Area(PQOR) = 2 × 30 = 60 cm².

Answer: 60 cm² — option (a).

Exemplar Ex 9.1 Q31 mark
AB is a chord of a circle and AOC is its diameter such that ∠ACB = 50°. If AT is the tangent to the circle at A, then ∠BAT equals
Step 1 ∠ABC = 90° (angle in a semicircle), so in ∆ABC: ∠BAC = 180° − 90° − 50° = 40°.

Step-by-step solution

  1. ∠ABC = 90° (angle in a semicircle), so in ∆ABC: ∠BAC = 180° − 90° − 50° = 40°.
  2. AT ⊥ AC (radius OA along AC ⇒ tangent ⊥ radius). So ∠OAT = 90°.
  3. ∠BAT = ∠OAT − ∠BAC = 90° − 40° = 50°.

Answer: 50° — option (c).

Exemplar Ex 9.1 Q61 mark
AT is a tangent to a circle with centre O such that OT = 4 cm and ∠OTA = 30°. Then AT equals

Exemplar Ex 9.1 Q81 mark
If PA and PB are tangents to the circle with centre O such that ∠APB = 50°, then ∠OAB equals
Step 1 PA = PB (equal tangents), so ∆PAB is isosceles with ∠PAB = ∠PBA = (180° − 50°)/2 = 65°.

Step-by-step solution

  1. PA = PB (equal tangents), so ∆PAB is isosceles with ∠PAB = ∠PBA = (180° − 50°)/2 = 65°.
  2. OA ⊥ PA ⇒ ∠OAP = 90°.
  3. ∠OAB = ∠OAP − ∠BAP = 90° − 65° = 25°.

Answer: 25° — option (a).

Exercise 9.2

Exemplar Ex 9.2 Q32 marks
Is the statement 'The length of the tangent from an external point P on a circle with centre O is always less than OP' true? Justify briefly.
Step 1 Let P be external, T the point of contact. Then OT ⊥ PT, so in right ∆OTP, OP is the hypotenuse.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let P be external, T the point of contact. Then OT ⊥ PT, so in right ∆OTP, OP is the hypotenuse.
  2. Hypotenuse > each leg ⇒ PT < OP.
  3. Hence the statement is TRUE.

Answer: True; PT < OP because OP is the hypotenuse.

Exemplar Ex 9.2 Q42 marks
Can the angle between two tangents drawn from an external point be 0°? Justify.
Step 1 If the angle were 0°, the two tangent rays would coincide, giving only one tangent from the external point.

Step-by-step solution

  1. If the angle were 0°, the two tangent rays would coincide, giving only one tangent from the external point.
  2. But from an external point, exactly two distinct tangents exist (NCERT Section 10.3). So the angle is strictly positive.
  3. Hence the statement is FALSE.

Answer: False; two distinct tangents make a strictly positive angle.

Exercise 9.3

Exemplar Ex 9.3 Q13 marks
Out of two concentric circles, the radius of the outer circle is 5 cm and the chord AC of length 8 cm is a tangent to the inner circle. Find the radius of the inner circle.
Step 1 Let P be the point of contact on the inner circle. OP ⊥ AC and bisects AC at P, so AP = 4 cm.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let P be the point of contact on the inner circle. OP ⊥ AC and bisects AC at P, so AP = 4 cm.
  2. In right ∆OPA: OP² = OA² − AP² = 25 − 16 = 9.
  3. OP = 3 cm — the radius of the inner circle.

Answer: Radius of inner circle = 3 cm.

Exemplar Ex 9.3 Q33 marks
If from an external point B of a circle with centre O, two tangents BC and BD are drawn such that ∠DBC = 120°, prove that BO = 2BC.
Step 1 BO bisects ∠DBC, so ∠OBC = 60°. ∠OCB = 90° (radius ⊥ tangent).

Step-by-step solution

  1. BO bisects ∠DBC, so ∠OBC = 60°. ∠OCB = 90° (radius ⊥ tangent).
  2. In right ∆OCB: cos 60° = BC/BO ⇒ BC = BO × (1/2).
  3. Hence BO = 2BC. Also BC + BD = 2BC = BO.

Answer: Proved: BO = 2BC (and BC + BD = BO).

Exercise 9.4

Exemplar Ex 9.4 Q35 marks
From an external point P, two tangents PA and PB are drawn to a circle with centre O. At a point E on the circle, the tangent intersects PA at C and PB at D. If PA = 10 cm, find the perimeter of triangle PCD.
Step 1 [1 mark] Tangents drawn from an external point are equal, so PA = PB = 10 cm.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Tangents drawn from an external point are equal, so PA = PB = 10 cm.
  2. [1 mark] From C the two tangents give CA = CE; from D they give DB = DE (equal tangents from an external point).
  3. [1 mark] Perimeter(△PCD) = PC + CD + DP = PC + (CE + ED) + DP.
  4. [1 mark] Substitute CE = CA and ED = DB: = (PC + CA) + (DP + DB) = PA + PB.
  5. [1 mark] Perimeter = 10 + 10 = 20 cm. Hence the perimeter of △PCD is 20 cm.

Answer: Perimeter of ∆PCD = 20 cm.

Exemplar Ex 9.4 Q85 marks
AB is a diameter and AC is a chord of a circle with centre O such that ∠BAC = 30°. The tangent at C intersects extended AB at a point D. Prove that BC = BD.
Step 1 ∠ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle). In ∆ABC: ∠ABC = 180° − 90° − 30° = 60°.

Step-by-step solution

  1. ∠ACB = 90° (angle in a semicircle). In ∆ABC: ∠ABC = 180° − 90° − 30° = 60°.
  2. OC ⊥ CD (tangent ⊥ radius), so ∠OCD = 90°.
  3. ∠OCB = ∠OBC (isosceles, OC = OB = radius), and ∠OBC = 60°. So ∠OCB = 60°.
  4. ∠BCD = ∠OCD − ∠OCB = 90° − 60° = 30°.
  5. Exterior angle at B in ∆BCD: ∠OBC = 60° = ∠BDC + ∠BCD ⇒ ∠BDC = 60° − 30° = 30°.
  6. Thus ∠BCD = ∠BDC = 30° ⇒ ∆BCD is isosceles ⇒ BC = BD.

Answer: Proved: BC = BD.

Exemplar Sample Question 1 (page 106)3 marks
If d₁ and d₂ (with d₂ > d₁) are the diameters of two concentric circles and c is the length of a chord of the larger circle that is tangent to the smaller circle, prove that d₂² = c² + d₁².
Step 1 Let the chord AB of the larger circle touch the smaller circle at C. Then OC ⊥ AB and OC = d₁/2.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the chord AB of the larger circle touch the smaller circle at C. Then OC ⊥ AB and OC = d₁/2.
  2. OA = d₂/2 (radius of larger). C is the midpoint of AB, so CB = c/2.
  3. In right ∆OCB: OC² + CB² = OB² ⇒ (d₁/2)² + (c/2)² = (d₂/2)².
  4. Multiply by 4: d₁² + c² = d₂². Hence d₂² = c² + d₁².

Answer: Proved: d₂² = c² + d₁².
