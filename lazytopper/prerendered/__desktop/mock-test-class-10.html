What a board-pattern mock test is

A mock test is a full practice paper that follows the board paper: the same sections, the same kinds of questions and the same marks for each. You attempt it in one sitting, write every answer in full, and only then check your marks. It shows you how the whole paper feels, not just one chapter.

The CBSE 2026-27 pattern for Class 10 sets about 50% of the paper as competency-based questions, about 20% as MCQs and about 30% as short and long answers. A good mock follows that mix, so you meet case studies and application questions as often as you will in the exam.

Online mock or printed mock?

The online full mock is the quickest way to start: pick a subject and begin. The printed Mock Pack suits you if you want the exact feel of writing on paper, with a pen, in one sitting, away from the screen. You can use both: an online mock to find the weak chapters, and printed papers to build stamina closer to the boards.

How to use a mock test well

  1. Sit the whole paper in one go, without notes, and write every step the way you would in the exam.
  2. Mark it step by step against the marking scheme, not just on the final answers.
  3. Sort each lost mark into a cause: a silly slip when copying, a calculation error, a concept you have not understood, or presentation only, such as a missing unit or label.
  4. Fix the concept mistakes first, then retake that chapter’s questions before the next mock.

Step 2 is where most marks are found. Board answers earn marks for the formula, the working, the final answer and the unit separately; read how it works on the CBSE Class 10 marking scheme page and in the guide to how CBSE examiners mark answers.
