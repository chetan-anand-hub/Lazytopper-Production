Multiple choice questions

Exemplar Q11 mark
Which of the following is not a physical change?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Physical changes alter only the form/state of matter; chemical composition is preserved (e.g. boiling, melting, dissolving).
  2. Combustion of LPG involves burning hydrocarbons in oxygen to form CO₂ and H₂O — composition is changed and new substances are formed.
  3. Hence combustion is a chemical change, not physical. Option (d). Answer: (D)

Answer: Combustion of LPG (option d)

Exemplar Q41 mark
Which of the following are exothermic processes? (i) Reaction of water with quick lime (ii) Dilution of an acid (iii) Evaporation of water (iv) Sublimation of camphor (crystals)

Step-by-step solution

  1. Quick lime + water (CaO + H₂O → Ca(OH)₂) releases large amount of heat → exothermic (i) ✓.
  2. Acid dilution releases heat (concentrated acid into water → exothermic) → (ii) ✓.
  3. Evaporation absorbs heat from surroundings (cooling effect) → endothermic; (iii) ✗.
  4. Sublimation absorbs heat to convert solid→gas → endothermic; (iv) ✗.
  5. Answer: (a) (i) and (ii).

Answer: (i) and (ii) — option (a)

Exemplar Q51 mark
Three beakers labelled as A, B and C each containing 25 mL of water were taken. A small amount of NaOH, anhydrous CuSO₄ and NaCl were added to the beakers A, B and C respectively. It was observed that there was an increase in the temperature of the solutions contained in beakers A and B, whereas in case of beaker C, the temperature of the solution falls. Which one of the following statement(s) is(are) correct? (i) In beakers A and B, exothermic process has occurred (ii) In beakers A and B, endothermic process has occurred (iii) In beaker C, exothermic process has occurred (iv) In beaker C, endothermic process has occurred

Step-by-step solution

  1. Beakers A (NaOH) and B (anhydrous CuSO₄) showed temperature INCREASE → heat released → exothermic. So (i) ✓.
  2. Beaker C (NaCl) showed temperature DROP → heat absorbed from solution → endothermic. So (iv) ✓.
  3. Statements (ii) and (iii) describe the opposite, which contradicts the observation. False.
  4. Correct: (i) and (iv) → option (c). Answer: (C)

Answer: (i) and (iv) — option (c)

Exemplar Q101 mark
Barium chloride on reacting with ammonium sulphate forms barium sulphate and ammonium chloride. Which of the following correctly represents the type of the reaction involved? (i) Displacement reaction (ii) Precipitation reaction (iii) Combination reaction (iv) Double displacement reaction

Step-by-step solution

  1. Equation: BaCl₂(aq) + (NH₄)₂SO₄(aq) → BaSO₄(s)↓ + 2NH₄Cl(aq).
  2. Two compounds exchange their ions (Ba²⁺ with NH₄⁺) → double displacement reaction (iv) ✓.
  3. An insoluble BaSO₄ precipitate is formed → also a precipitation reaction (ii) ✓.
  4. Not displacement (no single element swap); not combination (multiple products).
  5. Both (ii) and (iv) are correct. Answer: (d).

Answer: (ii) and (iv) — option (d)

Exemplar Q131 mark
In the double displacement reaction between aqueous potassium iodide and aqueous lead nitrate, a yellow precipitate of lead iodide is formed. While performing the activity if lead nitrate is not available, which of the following can be used in place of lead nitrate?

Step-by-step solution

  1. We need a soluble Pb²⁺ salt so Pb²⁺ ions are available in solution to react with I⁻ from KI.
  2. (a) Lead sulphate is INSOLUBLE — no free Pb²⁺ in solution. Cannot be used.
  3. (b) Lead acetate is soluble and provides Pb²⁺ ✓. Pb(CH₃COO)₂ + 2KI → PbI₂↓ + 2CH₃COOK.
  4. (c) Ammonium nitrate has no Pb. (d) Potassium sulphate has no Pb.
  5. Answer: (b) Lead acetate.

Answer: Lead acetate — option (b)

Exemplar Q161 mark
Which one of the following processes involve chemical reactions?

Step-by-step solution

  1. (a) Compressing O₂ — only changes pressure, no new substance → physical.
  2. (b) Liquefying air — only state change → physical.
  3. (c) Petrol left open — evaporation (physical change).
  4. (d) Heating Cu in air forms CuO (black copper oxide) — new substance is produced → chemical reaction ✓.
  5. Answer: (d).

Answer: Heating copper wire in air — option (d)

Exemplar Q20(a)3 marks
Write the balanced chemical equation for the following reaction and identify the type of reaction: Thermite reaction — iron(III) oxide reacts with aluminium and gives molten iron and aluminium oxide.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] Write skeletal equation: Fe₂O₃ + Al → Fe + Al₂O₃.
  2. [0.5 mark] Balance Fe: LHS=2 → RHS needs 2 Fe.
  3. [0.5 mark] Balance Al: RHS Al₂O₃ has 2 Al → LHS needs 2 Al.
  4. [0.5 mark] Balance O: LHS=3, RHS=3 ✓.
  5. [0.5 mark] Final: Fe₂O₃(s) + 2Al(s) → 2Fe(l) + Al₂O₃(s).
  6. [0.5 mark] Type: Al is more reactive than Fe → it displaces Fe from Fe₂O₃ → displacement reaction. Heat released is intense enough to keep Fe molten → highly exothermic.

Answer: Fe₂O₃ + 2Al → 2Fe + Al₂O₃; displacement (exothermic)

Exemplar Q263 marks
A solution of potassium chloride when mixed with silver nitrate solution, an insoluble white substance is formed. Write the chemical reaction involved and also mention the type of the chemical reaction.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Two solutions mix; ions exchange — K⁺ pairs with NO₃⁻, Ag⁺ pairs with Cl⁻.
  2. [0.5 mark] Equation: KCl(aq) + AgNO₃(aq) → AgCl(s)↓ + KNO₃(aq).
  3. [0.5 mark] AgCl is insoluble in water → forms a white curdy precipitate.
  4. [1 mark] Type: double displacement reaction; also a precipitation reaction.

Answer: KCl + AgNO₃ → AgCl↓ + KNO₃ — double displacement / precipitation.

Exemplar Q273 marks
Ferrous sulphate decomposes with the evolution of a gas having a characteristic odour of burning sulphur. Write the chemical reaction involved and identify the type of reaction.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Heating FeSO₄ crystals breaks them into ferric oxide and sulphur oxides.
  2. [0.5 mark] Balance: 2 FeSO₄ → 1 Fe₂O₃ (Fe 2=2), 1 SO₂ + 1 SO₃ (S 2=2, O: LHS=8, RHS=3+2+3=8 ✓).
  3. [0.5 mark] Final balanced equation: 2FeSO₄(s) --Δ--> Fe₂O₃(s) + SO₂(g) + SO₃(g).
  4. [1 mark] Single reactant → multiple products = decomposition; heat-driven → thermal decomposition.

Answer: 2FeSO₄ --Heat--> Fe₂O₃ + SO₂ + SO₃; thermal decomposition.

Exemplar Q373 marks
Zinc liberates hydrogen gas when reacted with dilute hydrochloric acid, whereas copper does not. Explain why?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] The reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au.
  2. [1 mark] Zn lies above H — Zn is more reactive than hydrogen, so it can replace H⁺ from HCl: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂(g)↑.
  3. [1 mark] Cu lies below H — Cu is less reactive than hydrogen, so Cu cannot displace H from HCl. No reaction occurs between Cu and dilute HCl at ordinary temperatures.

Answer: Zn is above H in reactivity series (can displace H from HCl); Cu is below H (cannot).
