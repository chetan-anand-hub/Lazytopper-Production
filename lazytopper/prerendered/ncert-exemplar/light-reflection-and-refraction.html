Multiple choice questions

Exemplar Q11 mark
Which of the following can make a parallel beam of light when light from a point source is incident on it?

Step-by-step solution

  1. A point source placed at the focus of a concave mirror gives a reflected parallel beam.
  2. A point source placed at the focus of a convex lens gives a refracted parallel beam.
  3. Convex mirror and concave lens are diverging — they cannot give a parallel beam from a point source.

Answer: Option (a)

Exemplar Q21 mark
A 10 mm long awl pin is placed vertically in front of a concave mirror. A 5 mm long image of the awl pin is formed at 30 cm in front of the mirror. The focal length of this mirror is
Step 1 Image is real and inverted (formed in front of mirror) → magnification m = –h'/h = –5/10 = –1/2.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Image is real and inverted (formed in front of mirror) → magnification m = –h'/h = –5/10 = –1/2.
  2. Also m = –v/u ⇒ –1/2 = –(–30)/u ⇒ u = –60 cm.
  3. Apply mirror formula: 1/f = 1/v + 1/u = 1/(–30) + 1/(–60) = –2/60 – 1/60 = –3/60 = –1/20.
  4. Therefore f = –20 cm (concave mirror, focal length is negative by sign convention). Answer: (B)

Answer: f = –20 cm — option (b)

Exemplar Q81 mark
Which of the following statements is true?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Power P = 1/f (with f in metres).
  2. For f = +0.25 m (convex lens, positive focal length): P = 1/0.25 = +4 D.
  3. Convex lens has POSITIVE power → +4 D matches option (a).
  4. Concave lens would have negative focal length and negative power.

Answer: Option (a)

Exemplar Q91 mark
Magnification produced by a rear view mirror fitted in vehicles

Step-by-step solution

  1. Rear-view mirrors are CONVEX mirrors.
  2. A convex mirror always forms a virtual, erect, DIMINISHED image.
  3. Therefore magnification m < 1 always. Answer: (A)

Answer: Less than one — option (a)

Exemplar Q101 mark
Rays from Sun converge at a point 15 cm in front of a concave mirror. Where should an object be placed so that size of its image is equal to the size of the object?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Rays from the Sun are effectively parallel; they converge at the principal focus of the concave mirror.
  2. Hence focal length f = 15 cm; radius of curvature R = 2f = 30 cm.
  3. For image size = object size, the object must be placed at the centre of curvature C.
  4. Therefore object should be at 30 cm in front of the mirror. Answer: (B)

Answer: 30 cm in front of the mirror — option (b)

Exemplar Q121 mark
In torches, search lights and headlights of vehicles the bulb is placed

Step-by-step solution

  1. The reflector used in torches/headlights is a concave mirror.
  2. When the bulb (point source) is placed at the focus of a concave mirror, reflected rays emerge as a parallel beam.
  3. A parallel beam provides intense, long-range illumination.
  4. Hence the bulb is placed VERY NEAR the focus of the reflector. Answer: (B)

Answer: Very near to the focus — option (b)

Exemplar Q181 mark
A child is standing in front of a magic mirror. She finds the image of her head bigger, the middle portion of her body of the same size and that of the legs smaller. The following is the order of combinations for the magic mirror from the top.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Head appears BIGGER → that part must be a CONCAVE mirror (which can magnify when object is close).
  2. Middle portion appears SAME size → that part must be a PLANE mirror.
  3. Legs appear SMALLER → that part must be a CONVEX mirror (always diminishes).
  4. From top to bottom: concave, plane, convex — option (c). Answer: (C)

Answer: Concave, plane and convex — option (c)

Exemplar Q191 mark
In which of the following, the image of an object placed at infinity will be highly diminished and point sized?

Step-by-step solution

  1. When the object is at infinity, parallel rays come in.
  2. Concave mirror and convex lens form a real, highly diminished, point-sized image at their focus.
  3. Convex mirror and concave lens form a virtual, highly diminished, point-sized image at their focus (behind the mirror / on the same side as the object respectively).
  4. Hence ALL four optical elements give a highly diminished point-sized image at the focus for an object at infinity. Answer: (D)

Answer: All four — option (d)

Exemplar Q203 marks
Identify the device used as a spherical mirror or lens in following cases, when the image formed is virtual and erect in each case. (a) Object is placed between device and its focus, image formed is enlarged and behind it. (b) Object is placed between the focus and device, image formed is enlarged and on the same side as that of the object. (c) Object is placed between infinity and device, image formed is diminished and between focus and optical centre on the same side as that of the object. (d) Object is placed between infinity and device, image formed is diminished and between pole and focus, behind it.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] (a) Image is virtual, erect, enlarged and BEHIND the device → concave mirror (object between P and F).
  2. [0.5 mark] (b) Image is virtual, erect, enlarged and ON SAME SIDE as object → convex lens (object between optical centre and focus).
  3. [0.5 mark] (c) Image is virtual, erect, diminished, between F and optical centre, SAME SIDE as object → concave lens.
  4. [1 mark] (d) Image is virtual, erect, diminished, between P and F, BEHIND the device → convex mirror.

Answer: (a) Concave mirror, (b) Convex lens, (c) Concave lens, (d) Convex mirror.

Exemplar Q222 marks
A pencil when dipped in water in a glass tumbler appears to be bent at the interface of air and water. Will the pencil appear to be bent to the same extent, if instead of water we use liquids like kerosene or turpentine. Support your answer with reason.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] The apparent bending of a pencil at the air–liquid interface is due to refraction of light from the immersed part as it crosses into air.
  2. [0.5 mark] The extent of bending is governed by the refractive index of the liquid relative to air.
  3. [0.5 mark] Refractive indices: water ≈ 1.33, kerosene ≈ 1.44, turpentine ≈ 1.47.
  4. [0.5 mark] Higher refractive index → greater refraction → pencil appears MORE bent. So bending in kerosene / turpentine is NOT the same as in water.

Answer: No, pencil bends more in kerosene/turpentine than in water because they are optically denser.

Exemplar Q272 marks
How are power and focal length of a lens related? You are provided with two lenses of focal length 20 cm and 40 cm respectively. Which lens will you use to obtain more convergent light?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] Relation: power of a lens, P = 1 / f, where f is in metres. SI unit: dioptre (D).
  2. [0.5 mark] For f = 20 cm = 0.20 m → P = 1/0.20 = +5 D.
  3. [0.5 mark] For f = 40 cm = 0.40 m → P = 1/0.40 = +2.5 D.
  4. [0.5 mark] The 20 cm lens has higher power, hence it converges parallel rays more strongly. Use the 20 cm lens for more convergent light.

Answer: P = 1/f. Use the 20 cm lens (P = +5 D) for more convergent light.

Exemplar Q305 marks
Draw ray diagrams showing the image formation by a concave mirror when an object is placed (a) between pole and focus of the mirror (b) between focus and centre of curvature of the mirror (c) at centre of curvature of the mirror (d) a little beyond centre of curvature of the mirror (e) at infinity

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] (a) Object between P and F: rays diverge after reflection; trace them backwards to get a VIRTUAL, erect, enlarged image behind the mirror.
  2. [1 mark] (b) Object between F and C: image is REAL, inverted, enlarged, formed BEYOND C.
  3. [1 mark] (c) Object at C: image is REAL, inverted, of the SAME size, formed at C itself.
  4. [1 mark] (d) Object a little beyond C: image is REAL, inverted, DIMINISHED, formed between F and C.
  5. [1 mark] (e) Object at infinity: parallel rays converge at F → image is REAL, inverted, highly diminished and point-sized at the focus.

Answer: Five ray diagrams as above showing image properties for each object position.

Exemplar Q335 marks
Draw ray diagrams showing the image formation by a concave lens when an object is placed (a) at the focus of the lens (b) between focus and twice the focal length of the lens (c) beyond twice the focal length of the lens

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] A concave lens is a diverging lens; for any real object it always forms a VIRTUAL, erect, DIMINISHED image on the same side as the object, between the optical centre and the focus.
  2. [1 mark] (a) Object at F: image is virtual, erect, diminished, between O and F (closer to lens than F).
  3. [1 mark] (b) Object between F and 2F: image is virtual, erect, diminished, between O and F (somewhat closer to lens).
  4. [1 mark] (c) Object beyond 2F: image is virtual, erect, very diminished, very close to F on the same side as the object.
  5. [1 mark] Construction: draw a ray parallel to the principal axis — refracts as if coming from F on the object side; draw a ray through optical centre — passes undeviated. Their backward extensions meet to give the virtual image.

Answer: All three give virtual, erect, diminished images on the same side as the object.

Exemplar Q345 marks
Draw ray diagrams showing the image formation by a convex mirror when an object is placed (a) at infinity (b) at finite distance from the mirror

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] A convex mirror is a diverging mirror; the focus F and centre of curvature C are BEHIND the reflecting surface.
  2. [1 mark] (a) Object at infinity: a parallel beam from infinity, after reflection, appears to diverge from the focus F behind the mirror → image is virtual, erect, highly diminished (point-sized) at F.
  3. [1 mark] (b) Object at a finite distance in front: take a ray parallel to the axis (reflects as if diverging from F behind the mirror) and a ray directed towards C (retraces its path). Their backward extensions meet behind the mirror.
  4. [1 mark] Image is virtual, erect and DIMINISHED, formed between the pole P and the focus F behind the mirror.
  5. [1 mark] This wide field of view and erect diminished image is why convex mirrors are used as rear-view mirrors in vehicles.

Answer: (a) Virtual, erect, point-sized at F. (b) Virtual, erect, diminished between P and F behind the mirror.

Exemplar Q355 marks
The image of a candle flame formed by a lens is obtained on a screen placed on the other side of the lens. If the image is three times the size of the flame and the distance between lens and image is 80 cm, at what distance should the candle be placed from the lens? What is the nature of the image at a distance of 80 cm and the lens?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] The image is formed on a screen on the other side of the lens → image is REAL and INVERTED; lens is CONVEX.
  2. [1 mark] Magnification m = v / u = –3 (real-inverted image is 3× larger; sign convention gives m negative for real images).
  3. [1 mark] Given v = +80 cm (image on the other side of the lens, in the direction of light).
  4. [1 mark] From m = v / u: –3 = 80 / u ⇒ u = –80/3 cm ≈ –26.67 cm. So the candle is placed about 26.67 cm in front of the lens.
  5. [1 mark] Nature of image: real, inverted, magnified (3×). Lens used is a convex lens of focal length given by 1/f = 1/v – 1/u = 1/80 – 1/(–80/3) = 1/80 + 3/80 = 4/80 ⇒ f = 20 cm.

Answer: u ≈ 26.67 cm in front of the lens; image is real, inverted, magnified; convex lens of f = 20 cm.

Exemplar Q375 marks
Define power of a lens. What is its unit? One student uses a lens of focal length 50 cm and another of –50 cm. What is the nature of the lens and its power used by each of them?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Definition: Power of a lens is the reciprocal of its focal length measured in metres, P = 1/f. Physically it measures the degree of convergence or divergence of light by the lens.
  2. [1 mark] SI unit: dioptre (D); 1 dioptre = 1 m⁻¹.
  3. [1 mark] Convention: convex (converging) lens has positive focal length and positive power; concave (diverging) lens has negative focal length and negative power.
  4. [1 mark] Student 1: f = +50 cm = +0.5 m → lens is CONVEX; P = 1/0.5 = +2 D.
  5. [1 mark] Student 2: f = –50 cm = –0.5 m → lens is CONCAVE; P = 1/(–0.5) = –2 D.

Answer: Convex lens, +2 D and Concave lens, –2 D.
