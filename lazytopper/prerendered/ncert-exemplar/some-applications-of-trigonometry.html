Exercise 8.2

Exemplar Ch8 Ex 8.2 Q72 marks
State true or false with justification: 'If the length of the shadow of a tower is increasing, then the angle of elevation of the Sun is also increasing.'
Step 1 Let the tower have height h and shadow length s. Then tan(angle of elevation) = h/s.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the tower have height h and shadow length s. Then tan(angle of elevation) = h/s.
  2. If s increases (with h fixed), h/s decreases, so tan(elevation) decreases, so the angle decreases.
  3. Hence the statement is FALSE — as the shadow lengthens, the angle of elevation of the Sun DECREASES (think of early morning vs noon).

Answer: False — longer shadow ⇒ smaller angle of elevation (since tan θ = h/s decreases).

Exemplar Ch8 Ex 8.2 Q112 marks
State true or false with justification: 'The angle of elevation of the top of a tower is 30°. If the height of the tower is doubled, the angle of elevation will also double.'
Step 1 If original height is h and distance is d, then tan 30° = h/d ⇒ d = h√3.

Step-by-step solution

  1. If original height is h and distance is d, then tan 30° = h/d ⇒ d = h√3.
  2. After doubling height: new tan(angle) = (2h)/d = 2h/(h√3) = 2/√3.
  3. tan 60° = √3 ≈ 1.732, while 2/√3 ≈ 1.155 ≠ √3.
  4. So the new angle is arctan(2/√3) ≈ 49.1°, not 60°.
  5. Hence the statement is FALSE — doubling the height does NOT double the angle.

Answer: False — doubling h gives tan(new) = 2/√3, not √3 (60°). The angle does not double.

Exemplar Ch8 Ex 8.2 Q123 marks
State true or false with justification: 'If both the height of a tower and the distance of the observation point from its foot are increased by 10%, then the angle of elevation of its top remains unchanged.'
Step 1 Originally: tan θ = h/d.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Originally: tan θ = h/d.
  2. After 10% increase in both: new tan = (1.1 h)/(1.1 d) = h/d = tan θ.
  3. So the new angle equals the old angle.
  4. Hence the statement is TRUE.

Answer: True — proportional scaling of both legs leaves the ratio (and hence the angle) unchanged.

Exercise 8.3

Exemplar Ch8 Ex 8.3 Q82 marks
Find the angle of elevation of the Sun when the shadow of a pole h metres high is √3 h metres long.
Step 1 Let the angle of elevation be θ. tan θ = height/shadow = h/(√3 h) = 1/√3.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the angle of elevation be θ. tan θ = height/shadow = h/(√3 h) = 1/√3.
  2. tan θ = 1/√3 ⇒ θ = 30°.

Answer: 30°.

Exemplar Ch8 Ex 8.3 Q143 marks
An observer 1.5 m tall is 20.5 m away from a tower 22 m high. Determine the angle of elevation of the top of the tower from the eye of the observer.
Step 1 Effective height above the eye level = 22 − 1.5 = 20.5 m.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Effective height above the eye level = 22 − 1.5 = 20.5 m.
  2. Horizontal distance = 20.5 m.
  3. tan(angle) = 20.5/20.5 = 1.
  4. Angle = 45°.

Answer: Angle of elevation = 45°.

Exercise 8.4

Exemplar Ch8 Ex 8.4 Q35 marks
The angle of elevation of the top of a tower from a certain point is 30°. If the observer moves 20 metres towards the tower, the angle of elevation increases by 15°. Find the height of the tower.
Step 1 Let the tower be AB with height h, B the foot. Let the first point be P, the second be Q, with QB nearer.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the tower be AB with height h, B the foot. Let the first point be P, the second be Q, with QB nearer.
  2. At P: tan 30° = h / PB ⇒ PB = h/tan 30° = h√3.
  3. At Q (after moving 20 m closer): angle is 30° + 15° = 45°. tan 45° = h/QB ⇒ QB = h.
  4. PB − QB = 20 ⇒ h√3 − h = 20 ⇒ h(√3 − 1) = 20.
  5. h = 20/(√3 − 1) = 20(√3 + 1)/[(√3 − 1)(√3 + 1)] = 20(√3 + 1)/(3 − 1) = 20(√3 + 1)/2 = 10(√3 + 1).
  6. Numerically ≈ 10 × 2.732 ≈ 27.32 m.

Answer: Height of the tower = 10(√3 + 1) m ≈ 27.32 m.

Exemplar Ch8 Ex 8.4 Q75 marks
The shadow of a tower standing on level ground is 50 m longer when the Sun's elevation is 30° than when it is 60°. Find the height of the tower.
Step 1 Let tower height = h, shadow at 60° = x. Then at 30°, shadow = x + 50.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let tower height = h, shadow at 60° = x. Then at 30°, shadow = x + 50.
  2. At 60°: tan 60° = h/x ⇒ √3 = h/x ⇒ h = x√3. …(1)
  3. At 30°: tan 30° = h/(x + 50) ⇒ 1/√3 = h/(x + 50) ⇒ h = (x + 50)/√3. …(2)
  4. Equate: x√3 = (x + 50)/√3 ⇒ 3x = x + 50 ⇒ 2x = 50 ⇒ x = 25.
  5. h = 25√3 m ≈ 43.3 m.

Answer: Height of the tower = 25√3 m ≈ 43.3 m.

Exemplar Ch8 Ex 8.4 Q135 marks
The angle of elevation of the top of a tower 30 m high from the foot of another tower in the same plane is 60°, and the angle of elevation of the top of the second tower from the foot of the first is 30°. Find the distance between the two towers and the height of the second tower.
Step 1 [1 mark] Diagram: two towers on the same horizontal plane — Tower 1 of height 30 m, Tower 2 of height h, distance between their feet = d.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Diagram: two towers on the same horizontal plane — Tower 1 of height 30 m, Tower 2 of height h, distance between their feet = d.
  2. [1 mark] From the foot of Tower 2, the elevation of the top of Tower 1 is 60°: tan 60° = 30/d ⇒ √3 = 30/d.
  3. [1 mark] So d = 30/√3 = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m.
  4. [1 mark] From the foot of Tower 1, the elevation of the top of Tower 2 is 30°: tan 30° = h/d ⇒ 1/√3 = h/(10√3).
  5. [1 mark] h = 10√3/√3 = 10 m. Hence the distance between the towers is 10√3 m and the height of the second tower is 10 m.

Answer: Distance between towers = 10√3 m ≈ 17.32 m; height of second tower = 10 m.

Exemplar Ch8 Ex 8.4 Q165 marks
The angle of elevation of the top of a vertical tower from a point on the ground is 60°. From another point 10 m vertically above the first, its angle of elevation is 45°. Find the height of the tower.
Step 1 Let the tower be AB with height h, foot at B. Let P be the ground point and Q be the point 10 m vertically above P.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the tower be AB with height h, foot at B. Let P be the ground point and Q be the point 10 m vertically above P.
  2. Let horizontal distance from P (and from Q, since Q is directly above P) to B = d.
  3. From P: tan 60° = h/d ⇒ √3 = h/d ⇒ d = h/√3. …(1)
  4. From Q: the height of the tower as seen from Q is (h − 10) and horizontal distance is still d. tan 45° = (h − 10)/d ⇒ 1 = (h − 10)/d ⇒ d = h − 10. …(2)
  5. Equate (1) and (2): h/√3 = h − 10 ⇒ h = √3(h − 10) ⇒ h = h√3 − 10√3 ⇒ h(√3 − 1) = 10√3 ⇒ h = 10√3/(√3 − 1).
  6. Rationalise: h = 10√3(√3 + 1)/[(√3 − 1)(√3 + 1)] = 10√3(√3 + 1)/2 = 5√3(√3 + 1) = 5(3 + √3) = 15 + 5√3.
  7. Numerically ≈ 15 + 8.66 ≈ 23.66 m.

Answer: Height of the tower = (15 + 5√3) m ≈ 23.66 m.
