Exemplar Q21 mark
The ability of metals to be drawn into thin wire is known as

Step-by-step solution

  1. Ductility = drawn into thin wires.
  2. Malleability = beaten into sheets. So drawn into wire = ductility. Answer: (A)

Answer: Ductility — option (a)

Exemplar Q71 mark
Generally metals react with acids to give salt and hydrogen gas. Which of the following acids does not give hydrogen gas on reacting with metals (except Mn and Mg)?

Exemplar Q121 mark
Metals are refined by using different methods. Which of the following metals are refined by electrolytic refining? (i) Au (ii) Cu (iii) Na (iv) K

Step-by-step solution

  1. Electrolytic refining is used for less reactive metals like Cu, Au, Ag, Zn.
  2. Na and K are very reactive — they cannot be refined by electrolysis of aqueous solutions (would react with water). They are obtained by electrolysis of molten chlorides directly. So (i) Au and (ii) Cu are refined electrolytically. Answer: (A)

Answer: (i) and (ii) — option (a)

Exemplar Q301 mark
The electronic configurations of three elements X, Y and Z are X — 2,8; Y — 2,8,7 and Z — 2,8,2. Which of the following is correct?

Step-by-step solution

  1. X: 2,8 → 8 valence e⁻ → noble gas (Ne, inert).
  2. Y: 2,8,7 → 7 valence e⁻ → non-metal (one short of octet, gains electron).
  3. Z: 2,8,2 → 2 valence e⁻ → metal (loses electrons easily).
  4. So Y is non-metal and Z is metal — option (d). Answer: (D)

Answer: (d) — Y non-metal, Z metal

Exemplar Q383 marks
During extraction of metals, electrolytic refining is used to obtain pure metals. (a) Which material will be used as anode and cathode for refining of silver metal by this process? (b) Suggest a suitable electrolyte also. (c) In this electrolytic cell, where do we get pure silver after passing electric current?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] (a) Anode = impure Ag (to be refined); Cathode = pure Ag strip.
  2. [1 mark] (b) Electrolyte must be a soluble Ag salt → AgNO₃ solution (with a little HNO₃).
  3. [1 mark] (c) During electrolysis, Ag dissolves from anode → Ag⁺ moves through electrolyte → deposits as pure Ag on cathode. Impurities settle as anode mud.

Answer: Anode: impure Ag; Cathode: pure Ag; Electrolyte: AgNO₃ solution; Pure Ag deposits on cathode.

Exemplar Q403 marks
Generally, when metals are treated with mineral acids, hydrogen gas is liberated but when metals (except Mn and Mg), treated with HNO₃, hydrogen is not liberated, why?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Most acids (HCl, H₂SO₄ dil): metal + acid → salt + H₂↑.
  2. [0.5 mark] HNO₃ acts as an oxidising agent: H₂ is oxidised back to H₂O.
  3. [0.5 mark] Instead of H₂, products like NO (with dilute HNO₃) or NO₂ (with conc HNO₃) are formed.
  4. [1 mark] Mn and Mg are exceptions: with very dilute HNO₃, they release H₂ because the acid is too dilute to oxidise it fully.

Answer: HNO₃ is oxidising — re-oxidises H₂ to H₂O. Only very reactive Mn and Mg can give H₂ with very dilute HNO₃.

Exemplar Q423 marks
When a metal X is treated with cold water, it gives a basic salt Y with molecular formula XOH (Molecular mass = 40) and liberates a gas Z which easily catches fire. Identify X, Y and Z and also write the reaction involved.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] XOH = X + 17 (OH). Given mass = 40, so X = 40 − 17 = 23. X is Na (atomic mass 23).
  2. [1 mark] Y = NaOH. Z = H₂ gas (catches fire — H₂ burns explosively).
  3. [1 mark] Reaction: 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂↑. So much heat is released that H₂ catches fire.

Answer: X = Na, Y = NaOH, Z = H₂. 2Na + 2H₂O → 2NaOH + H₂.

Exemplar Q443 marks
The following reaction takes place when aluminium powder is heated with MnO₂: 3MnO₂(s) + 4Al(s) → 3Mn(l) + 2Al₂O₃(l) + Heat (a) Is aluminium getting reduced? (b) Is MnO₂ getting oxidised?

Step-by-step solution

  2. [0.5 mark] (a) Al is OXIDISED (not reduced) → Answer: No.
  3. [0.5 mark] (b) MnO₂ is REDUCED (not oxidised) → Answer: No.
  4. [1 mark] Al acts as reducing agent; MnO₂ is the oxidising agent.

Answer: (a) No, Al is oxidised. (b) No, MnO₂ is reduced.

Exemplar Q473 marks
A metal that exists as a liquid at room temperature is obtained by heating its sulphide in the presence of air. Identify the metal and its ore and give the reaction involved.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Mercury is the only liquid metal at room temperature.
  2. [0.5 mark] Mercury's main ore is cinnabar (HgS).
  3. [0.5 mark] Roasting: 2HgS + 3O₂ → 2HgO + 2SO₂.
  4. [1 mark] Further heating: 2HgO → 2Hg + O₂ (Hg has low thermal stability of oxide). So Hg is obtained simply by heating its sulphide in air.

Answer: Metal = Hg; Ore = HgS (cinnabar). 2HgS+3O₂→2HgO+2SO₂; 2HgO→2Hg+O₂.

Exemplar Q583 marks
A solution of CuSO₄ was kept in an iron pot. After few days the iron pot was found to have a number of holes in it. Explain the reason in terms of reactivity. Write the equation of the reaction involved.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Reactivity series check: Fe is above Cu.
  2. [0.5 mark] Fe + CuSO₄(aq) → FeSO₄(aq) + Cu(s) — displacement reaction.
  3. [0.5 mark] Iron atoms from the pot's walls go into solution as Fe²⁺; copper plates out.
  4. [1 mark] Continued reaction over days corrodes the iron, leaving holes.

Answer: Fe is above Cu in reactivity. Fe + CuSO₄ → FeSO₄ + Cu. Iron dissolves → holes in pot.

Exemplar Q635 marks
Of the three metals X, Y and Z. X reacts with cold water, Y with hot water and Z with steam only. Identify X, Y and Z and also arrange them in order of increasing reactivity.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] More reactive metals react with cold water; less reactive ones need hot water; even less reactive need steam.
  2. [1 mark] X reacts with cold water — most reactive of the three. E.g., Na or Ca.
  3. [0.5 mark] Y reacts with hot water — moderate reactivity. E.g., Mg.
  4. [0.5 mark] Z reacts with steam only — least reactive of the three. E.g., Fe or Al.
  5. [1 mark] Reactivity order (increasing): Z < Y < X. So Fe < Mg < Na.
  6. [1 mark] (General reactivity series: K > Na > Ca > Mg > Al > Zn > Fe > Pb > H > Cu > Hg > Ag > Au.)

Answer: X = Na (or Ca); Y = Mg; Z = Fe (or Al). Increasing reactivity: Z < Y < X.
