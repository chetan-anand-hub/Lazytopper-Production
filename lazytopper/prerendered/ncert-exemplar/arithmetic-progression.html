Exemplar Ex 5.2 Q1(ii)2 marks
Is 0, 2, 0, 2, … an AP? Justify your answer.
Step 1 a₂ − a₁ = 2 − 0 = 2.

Step-by-step solution

  1. a₂ − a₁ = 2 − 0 = 2.
  2. a₃ − a₂ = 0 − 2 = −2.
  3. Since a₂ − a₁ ≠ a₃ − a₂, the differences are not constant, so it is NOT an AP.

Answer: Not an AP — successive differences differ.

  1. Let the three terms be a − d, a, a + d. Sum: 3a = 33 ⇒ a = 11.
  2. (a − d)(a + d) = a + 29 ⇒ a² − d² = a + 29 ⇒ 121 − d² = 40 ⇒ d² = 81 ⇒ d = ±9.

Answer: AP: 2, 11, 20, … or 20, 11, 2, ….

The sum of four consecutive numbers in an AP is 32 and the ratio of the product of the first and last to the product of the two middle terms is 7 : 15. Find the numbers.
Step 1 Let the four numbers be a − 3d, a − d, a + d, a + 3d.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the four numbers be a − 3d, a − d, a + d, a + 3d.
  2. Sum: 4a = 32 ⇒ a = 8.
  3. Ratio: (a² − 9d²) / (a² − d²) = 7/15 ⇒ 15(64 − 9d²) = 7(64 − d²).
  4. 960 − 135d² = 448 − 7d² ⇒ 512 = 128d² ⇒ d² = 4 ⇒ d = ±2.
  5. Taking d = 2: numbers are 2, 6, 10, 14.

Answer: Numbers are 2, 6, 10, 14 (or in reverse).
