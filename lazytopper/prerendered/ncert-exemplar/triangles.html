Exercise 6.1

Exemplar Ex 6.1 Q31 mark
If △ABC ~ △EDF and △ABC is not similar to △DEF, then which of the following is NOT true?
Step 1 △ABC ~ △EDF means A↔E, B↔D, C↔F. So AB/ED = BC/DF = CA/FE.

Step-by-step solution

  1. △ABC ~ △EDF means A↔E, B↔D, C↔F. So AB/ED = BC/DF = CA/FE.
  2. From AB/ED = BC/DF: AB · DF = ED · BC ⇒ option (d) BC · DE = AB · FD is TRUE.
  3. From BC/DF = CA/FE: BC · FE = DF · CA ⇒ option (a) BC · EF = AC · FD is TRUE.
  4. From AB/ED = CA/FE: AB · FE = ED · CA ⇒ option (b) AB · EF = AC · DE is TRUE.
  5. Option (c) BC · DE = AB · EF would require a different correspondence and is NOT a consequence of △ABC ~ △EDF.

Answer: Option (c) BC · DE = AB · EF is NOT true.

Exemplar Ex 6.1 Q41 mark
If in two triangles ABC and PQR, AB/QR = BC/PR = CA/PQ, then:
Step 1 AB/QR = BC/PR = CA/PQ means the matched ratios are AB↔QR, BC↔PR, CA↔PQ.

Step-by-step solution

  1. AB/QR = BC/PR = CA/PQ means the matched ratios are AB↔QR, BC↔PR, CA↔PQ.
  2. So vertex A is between AB and CA → matches the vertex between QR and PQ → that is Q.
  3. Vertex B is between AB and BC → matches the vertex between QR and PR → R.
  4. Vertex C is between BC and CA → matches the vertex between PR and PQ → P.
  5. Correspondence: A↔Q, B↔R, C↔P ⇒ △ABC ~ △QRP, equivalently △PQR ~ △CAB.

Answer: △PQR ~ △CAB — option (a).

Exemplar Ex 6.1 Q51 mark
Two line segments AC and BD intersect at the point P such that PA = 6 cm, PB = 3 cm, PC = 2.5 cm, PD = 5 cm, ∠APB = 50° and ∠CDP = 30°. Then ∠PBA equals:
Step 1 Check ratios: PA/PD = 6/5 and PB/PC = 3/2.5 = 6/5. Equal ratios.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Check ratios: PA/PD = 6/5 and PB/PC = 3/2.5 = 6/5. Equal ratios.
  2. Included angle: ∠APB = ∠CPD (vertically opposite) = 50°.
  3. By SAS similarity, △APB ~ △DPC.
  4. Hence ∠PBA = ∠PCD. In △PCD, ∠PCD = 180° − ∠CPD − ∠CDP = 180° − 50° − 30° = 100°.
  5. So ∠PBA = 100°.

Answer: ∠PBA = 100° — option (d).

Exemplar Ex 6.1 Q71 mark
In two triangles ABC and DEF, ∠B = ∠E, ∠F = ∠C and AB = 3 DE. Then the two triangles are:
Step 1 ∠B = ∠E and ∠C = ∠F ⇒ by AA similarity, △ABC ~ △DEF.

Step-by-step solution

  1. ∠B = ∠E and ∠C = ∠F ⇒ by AA similarity, △ABC ~ △DEF.
  2. AB = 3 DE ⇒ scale factor is 3:1, so the triangles are NOT the same size.
  3. Hence similar but not congruent.

Answer: Similar but not congruent — option (b).

Exemplar Ex 6.1 Q101 mark
If in triangles ABC and DEF, AB/DE = BC/FD, then they will be similar when:
Step 1 For SAS similarity, the equal angle must be the angle INCLUDED between the proportional sides.

Step-by-step solution

  1. For SAS similarity, the equal angle must be the angle INCLUDED between the proportional sides.
  2. Sides AB and BC meet at vertex B in △ABC.
  3. Sides DE and FD meet at vertex D in △DEF (DE shares D; FD shares D).
  4. Hence the angles included between the given proportional sides are ∠B (in △ABC) and ∠D (in △DEF).
  5. So similarity requires ∠B = ∠D.

Answer: ∠B = ∠D — option (c).

Exercise 6.2

Exemplar Ex 6.2 Q22 marks
It is given that △DEF ~ △RPQ. Is it true to say that ∠D = ∠R and ∠F = ∠P? Why?
Step 1 Correspondence from △DEF ~ △RPQ: D↔R, E↔P, F↔Q.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Correspondence from △DEF ~ △RPQ: D↔R, E↔P, F↔Q.
  2. Therefore ∠D = ∠R ✓.
  3. But ∠F corresponds to ∠Q, not ∠P. So ∠F = ∠P is FALSE; the correct equality is ∠F = ∠Q.

Answer: ∠D = ∠R is true; ∠F = ∠P is false (correct match is ∠F = ∠Q).

Exemplar Ex 6.2 Q32 marks
A and B are on sides PQ and PR of △PQR with PQ = 12.5 cm, PA = 5 cm, BR = 6 cm and PB = 4 cm. Is AB ∥ QR? Give reasons.
Step 1 AQ = PQ − PA = 12.5 − 5 = 7.5 cm.

Step-by-step solution

  1. AQ = PQ − PA = 12.5 − 5 = 7.5 cm.
  2. PA/AQ = 5/7.5 = 2/3.
  3. PB/BR = 4/6 = 2/3.
  4. Ratios equal ⇒ by converse of BPT, AB ∥ QR.

Answer: Yes, AB ∥ QR.

Exemplar Ex 6.2 Q62 marks
Is the following statement true? Why? 'Two quadrilaterals are similar if their corresponding angles are equal.'
Step 1 Definition: two polygons of the same number of sides are similar iff BOTH (i) corresponding angles are equal AND (ii) corresponding sides are proportional.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Definition: two polygons of the same number of sides are similar iff BOTH (i) corresponding angles are equal AND (ii) corresponding sides are proportional.
  2. Counter-example: a 2×2 square and a 2×5 rectangle — both have all angles 90°, but sides give ratios 2:2 = 1 and 2:5 = 0.4, not equal.
  3. Therefore equal angles alone do not guarantee similarity for quadrilaterals.

Answer: False — equal angles are necessary but not sufficient (counter-example: square vs rectangle).

Exemplar Ex 6.2 Q82 marks
If in two right triangles one of the acute angles of one triangle is equal to an acute angle of the other triangle, can the two triangles be similar? Why?
Step 1 Both triangles have a right angle (90°) — first pair of equal angles.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Both triangles have a right angle (90°) — first pair of equal angles.
  2. Given: one acute angle of one = one acute angle of the other — second pair of equal angles.
  3. Two pairs of equal angles ⇒ AA similarity ⇒ triangles are similar.

Answer: Yes — by AA similarity (right angle + one equal acute angle).

Exercise 6.3

Exemplar Ex 6.3 Q73 marks
If △ABC ~ △DEF with AB = 4 cm, DE = 6 cm, EF = 9 cm and FD = 12 cm, find the perimeter of △ABC.
Step 1 Similarity scale factor k = AB/DE = 4/6 = 2/3.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Similarity scale factor k = AB/DE = 4/6 = 2/3.
  2. BC corresponds to EF ⇒ BC = (2/3) · 9 = 6 cm.
  3. CA corresponds to FD ⇒ CA = (2/3) · 12 = 8 cm.
  4. Perimeter of △ABC = 4 + 6 + 8 = 18 cm.

Answer: Perimeter of △ABC = 18 cm.

Exemplar Ex 6.3 Q113 marks
In △PQR, N is a point on PR such that QN ⊥ PR. If PN · NR = QN², prove that ∠PQR = 90°.
Step 1 Given: QN ⊥ PR; PN · NR = QN².

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: QN ⊥ PR; PN · NR = QN².
  2. Rewrite: PN/QN = QN/NR. With ∠PNQ = ∠QNR = 90°, by SAS similarity, △PNQ ~ △QNR.
  3. From similarity, ∠NPQ = ∠NQR (corresponding angles).
  4. In right △PNQ, ∠NPQ + ∠PQN = 90°.
  5. Substitute ∠NPQ = ∠NQR: ∠NQR + ∠PQN = 90°.
  6. But ∠NQR + ∠PQN = ∠PQR (the full angle at Q split by QN).
  7. Therefore ∠PQR = 90°. Q.E.D.

Answer: Proved: ∠PQR = 90° via SAS similarity of △PNQ and △QNR.

Exemplar Ex 6.3 Q142 marks
A 15 m high tower casts a shadow 24 m long at a certain time, and at the same time a telephone pole casts a shadow 16 m long. Find the height of the telephone pole.
Step 1 At the same time, sun's rays make equal angles with the ground. Both tower and pole are vertical.

Step-by-step solution

  1. At the same time, sun's rays make equal angles with the ground. Both tower and pole are vertical.
  2. By AA similarity, the two right triangles are similar.
  3. 15/24 = h/16 ⇒ h = (15 × 16)/24 = 240/24 = 10 m.

Answer: Height of the telephone pole = 10 m.

Exercise 6.4

Exemplar Ex 6.4 Q25 marks
△ABC ~ △EDF with AB = 5 cm, AC = 7 cm, DF = 15 cm and DE = 12 cm. Find the lengths of the remaining sides of the triangles.
Step 1 Correspondence from △ABC ~ △EDF: A↔E, B↔D, C↔F.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Correspondence from △ABC ~ △EDF: A↔E, B↔D, C↔F.
  2. Side ratios: AB/ED = BC/DF = CA/FE.
  3. AB/ED = 5/12. From BC/DF = 5/12: BC = 15 × 5/12 = 75/12 = 25/4 cm = 6.25 cm.
  4. From CA/FE = 5/12: 7/FE = 5/12 ⇒ FE = 7 · 12/5 = 84/5 cm = 16.8 cm.
  5. Remaining sides: BC = 25/4 cm; EF = 84/5 cm.

Answer: BC = 25/4 cm (6.25 cm); EF = 84/5 cm (16.8 cm).

Exemplar Ex 6.4 Q3 (Theorem 6.1)5 marks
Prove that if a line is drawn parallel to one side of a triangle to intersect the other two sides, then the two sides are divided in the same ratio. (Basic Proportionality Theorem.)
Step 1 Given: △ABC; DE ∥ BC with D ∈ AB, E ∈ AC.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: △ABC; DE ∥ BC with D ∈ AB, E ∈ AC.
  2. To prove: AD/DB = AE/EC.
  3. Construction: join BE, CD; draw perpendiculars DM ⊥ AC and EN ⊥ AB.
  4. Compute ratios of areas: ar(△ADE)/ar(△BDE) = ((1/2)·AD·EN)/((1/2)·DB·EN) = AD/DB. (*)
  5. Similarly ar(△ADE)/ar(△DEC) = ((1/2)·AE·DM)/((1/2)·EC·DM) = AE/EC. (**)
  6. △BDE and △DEC share base DE and lie between the parallels DE and BC ⇒ they have equal areas: ar(△BDE) = ar(△DEC).
  7. Therefore (*) and (**) give AD/DB = AE/EC. Q.E.D.

Answer: Proved: AD/DB = AE/EC (Basic Proportionality Theorem).
