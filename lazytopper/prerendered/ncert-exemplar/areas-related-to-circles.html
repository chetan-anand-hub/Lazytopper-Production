Is the area of the circle inscribed in a square of side a cm equal to πa² cm²? Justify briefly.
Step 1 The inscribed circle has diameter = a, so radius = a/2.

Step-by-step solution

  1. The inscribed circle has diameter = a, so radius = a/2.
  2. Area = π(a/2)² = πa²/4, not πa².
  3. Hence the statement is FALSE.

Exemplar Ex 11.2 Q92 marks
If two sectors of different circles have equal arc lengths, must they have equal areas? Justify briefly.
Step 1 Area of sector = (1/2) × r × arc length (using A = (1/2)rl).

Step-by-step solution

  1. Area of sector = (1/2) × r × arc length (using A = (1/2)rl).
  2. If arc lengths are equal but radii differ, the areas will differ (larger radius ⇒ larger area).
  3. Hence the statement is FALSE.

Answer: False; equal arc lengths do not force equal sector areas.

Exemplar Ex 11.2 Q42 marks
Is it true to say that area of a segment of a circle is less than the area of its corresponding sector? Why?
Step 1 [1 mark] For a minor segment, area = area of sector − area of the triangle, which is indeed less than the sector.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] For a minor segment, area = area of sector − area of the triangle, which is indeed less than the sector.
  2. [1 mark] But for a major segment the triangle's area is added to the major sector, so the major segment exceeds its sector; hence the statement is not true in general (only for a minor segment).

Answer: Not always — it is true only for a minor segment, not a major one.

Exemplar Ex 11.2 Q62 marks
In covering a distance s metres, a circular wheel of radius r metres makes s/(2πr) revolutions. Is this statement true? Why?
Step 1 [1 mark] In one revolution a wheel covers a distance equal to its circumference, 2πr metres.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] In one revolution a wheel covers a distance equal to its circumference, 2πr metres.
  2. [1 mark] So the number of revolutions to cover s metres is s ÷ (2πr) = s/(2πr) — the statement is True.

Answer: True — distance per revolution is 2πr, so revolutions = s/(2πr).

The numerical value of the area of a circle is greater than the numerical value of its circumference. Is this statement true? Why?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Area = πr² and circumference = 2πr; πr² > 2πr ⇔ r > 2.
  2. [1 mark] So it holds only when r > 2 (for r < 2 the circumference is numerically greater, for r = 2 they are equal) — the statement is not always true; it depends on the radius.

Answer: Not always — it is true only when the radius r > 2; it depends on r.

Exemplar Sample Question 1 (Ex 11.4)5 marks
A chord of a circle of radius 10 cm subtends an angle of 90° at the centre. Find the area of the corresponding major segment. (Use π = 3.14.)
Step 1 [1 mark] Setup: chord AB subtends 90° at centre O, radius r = 10 cm, so the major sector angle = 360° − 90° = 270°.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Setup: chord AB subtends 90° at centre O, radius r = 10 cm, so the major sector angle = 360° − 90° = 270°.
  2. [1 mark] Area of major sector = (270/360) × πr² = (3/4) × π × 100.
  3. [1 mark] = (3/4) × 3.14 × 100 = 235.5 cm².
  4. [1 mark] Triangle OAB (right-angled at O): area = (1/2) × 10 × 10 = 50 cm².
  5. [1 mark] Area of major segment = area of major sector + area of triangle = 235.5 + 50 = 285.5 cm².

Answer: Major segment = 285.5 cm².

Exemplar Sample Question 2 (Ex 11.4)5 marks
With vertices A, B and C of a right triangle ABC (right-angled at B) as centres, arcs of radius 5 cm each are drawn. If AB = 14 cm, BC = 48 cm and CA = 50 cm, find the area of the shaded region inside the triangle but outside the three sectors.
Step 1 [1 mark] Verification: 14² + 48² = 196 + 2304 = 2500 = 50², so ΔABC is right-angled at B.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Verification: 14² + 48² = 196 + 2304 = 2500 = 50², so ΔABC is right-angled at B.
  2. [1 mark] Area of ΔABC = (1/2) × AB × BC = (1/2) × 14 × 48 = 336 cm².
  3. [1 mark] Each sector contributes (∠/360) × π × 5², and the total angle = ∠A + ∠B + ∠C = 180° (angle sum of a triangle).
  4. [1 mark] Total sector area = (180/360) × π × 25 = (1/2) × π × 25 = 12.5 × 3.14 = 39.25 cm².
  5. [1 mark] Shaded area = area of triangle − total sector area = 336 − 39.25 = 296.75 cm².

Answer: Shaded area = 296.75 cm².
