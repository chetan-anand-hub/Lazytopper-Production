Exercise 12.1

Exemplar Ex 12.1 Q141 mark
A medicine-capsule is in the shape of a cylinder of diameter 0.5 cm with two hemispheres stuck to each of its ends. The length of the entire capsule is 2 cm. The capacity of the capsule is
Step 1 Radius r = 0.25 cm; cylinder length h = 2 − 2(0.25) = 1.5 cm.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Radius r = 0.25 cm; cylinder length h = 2 − 2(0.25) = 1.5 cm.
  2. V = πr²h + (4/3)πr³ = π(0.0625)(1.5) + (4/3)π(0.015625) = π[0.09375 + 0.02083].
  3. = π × 0.11458 ≈ 3.14 × 0.11458 ≈ 0.36 cm³.

Answer: 0.36 cm³ — option (A).

Exemplar Ex 12.1 Q151 mark
If two solid hemispheres of same base radius r are joined together along their bases, then the curved surface area of this new solid is
Step 1 Two hemispheres joined along their bases form a complete sphere.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Two hemispheres joined along their bases form a complete sphere.
  2. Surface area of a sphere = 4πr².
  3. (The two flat circles are joined and are no longer exposed.)

Answer: 4πr² — option (A).

Exemplar Ex 12.1 Q161 mark
A right circular cylinder of radius r cm and height h cm (h > 2r) just encloses a sphere of diameter
Step 1 The sphere must fit inside the cylinder, so its diameter is limited by the cylinder's diameter.

Step-by-step solution

  1. The sphere must fit inside the cylinder, so its diameter is limited by the cylinder's diameter.
  2. Cylinder diameter = 2r. The condition h > 2r ensures the sphere also fits in height.
  3. Hence the largest enclosed sphere has diameter 2r.

Answer: 2r cm — option (B).

Exercise 12.2

Exemplar Ex 12.2 Q12 marks
State whether true or false and justify: 'Two identical solid hemispheres of equal base radius r cm are stuck together along their bases. The total surface area of the combination is 6πr².'
Step 1 Joining two hemispheres along their flat bases gives a sphere of radius r.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Joining two hemispheres along their flat bases gives a sphere of radius r.
  2. Surface area of a sphere = 4πr² (NOT 6πr²).
  3. So the statement is FALSE.

Answer: False — the combined solid is a sphere, whose surface area is 4πr², not 6πr².

Exemplar Ex 12.2 Q22 marks
State whether true or false and justify: 'A solid cylinder of radius r and height h is placed over another cylinder of the same height and radius. The total surface area of the shape so formed is 4πrh + 4πr².'
Step 1 Stacking the two cylinders gives a single cylinder of radius r and total height 2h.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Stacking the two cylinders gives a single cylinder of radius r and total height 2h.
  2. TSA = 2πr(2h) + 2πr² = 4πrh + 2πr² (top + bottom circles only).
  3. The given expression has 4πr² (two extra circles) — that's wrong.
  4. Hence FALSE.

Answer: False — TSA is 4πrh + 2πr², not 4πrh + 4πr².

Exemplar Ex 12.2 Q42 marks
Write 'True' or 'False' and justify: A solid ball is exactly fitted inside the cubical box of side a. The volume of the ball is (4/3)a³.
Step 1 [1 mark] A ball fitting exactly inside a cube of side a has diameter a, hence radius a/2.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] A ball fitting exactly inside a cube of side a has diameter a, hence radius a/2.
  2. [1 mark] Its volume = (4/3)π(a/2)³ = πa³/6, not (4/3)a³ — so the statement is False.

Answer: False — the volume of the ball is πa³/6.

Exercise 12.3

Exemplar Ex 12.3 Q53 marks
Two identical cubes each of volume 64 cm³ are joined together end to end. What is the surface area of the resulting cuboid?
Step 1 Edge of each cube = ∛64 = 4 cm.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Edge of each cube = ∛64 = 4 cm.
  2. Resulting cuboid: l × b × h = 8 × 4 × 4 cm.
  3. TSA = 2(lb + bh + hl) = 2(32 + 16 + 32) = 2 × 80 = 160 cm².

Answer: 160 cm²

Exemplar Ex 12.3 Q63 marks
From a solid cube of side 7 cm, a conical cavity of height 7 cm and radius 3 cm is hollowed out. Find the volume of the remaining solid. (Use π = 22/7)
Step 1 V(cube) = 7³ = 343 cm³.

Step-by-step solution

  1. V(cube) = 7³ = 343 cm³.
  2. V(cone) = (1/3)πr²h = (1/3)(22/7)(9)(7) = (22 × 9)/3 = 66 cm³.
  3. V(remaining) = 343 − 66 = 277 cm³.

Answer: 277 cm³

Exemplar Ex 12.3 Q73 marks
Two cones with the same base radius 8 cm and height 15 cm are joined together along their bases. Find the surface area of the shape so formed. (Use π = 22/7)
Step 1 Joining two cones along their bases hides both circular faces; only the two slant surfaces remain.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Joining two cones along their bases hides both circular faces; only the two slant surfaces remain.
  2. Slant height l = √(r² + h²) = √(8² + 15²) = √(64 + 225) = √289 = 17 cm.
  3. SA = 2 × CSA(cone) = 2 × πrl = 2 × (22/7)(8)(17) = 2 × 2992/7 = 5984/7 ≈ 854.86 cm².

Answer: ≈ 854.86 cm² (i.e., 5984/7 cm²).

Exercise 12.4

Exemplar Ex 12.4 Q145 marks
A rocket is in the form of a right circular cylinder closed at the lower end and surmounted by a cone with the same radius as that of the cylinder. The diameter and height of the cylinder are 6 cm and 12 cm, respectively. If the slant height of the conical portion is 5 cm, find the total surface area and volume of the rocket. (Use π = 3.14)
Step 1 Radius r = 3 cm; cylinder height h₁ = 12 cm; cone slant l = 5 cm → cone height h₂ = √(l² − r²) = √(25 − 9) = 4 cm.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Radius r = 3 cm; cylinder height h₁ = 12 cm; cone slant l = 5 cm → cone height h₂ = √(l² − r²) = √(25 − 9) = 4 cm.
  2. TSA = CSA(cylinder) + area(bottom circle of cylinder) + CSA(cone) = 2πrh₁ + πr² + πrl.
  3. = 2(3.14)(3)(12) + (3.14)(9) + (3.14)(3)(5) = 226.08 + 28.26 + 47.10 = 301.44 cm².
  4. V = V(cylinder) + V(cone) = πr²h₁ + (1/3)πr²h₂ = (3.14)(9)(12) + (1/3)(3.14)(9)(4).
  5. = 339.12 + 37.68 = 376.80 cm³.

Answer: TSA = 301.44 cm²; V = 376.80 cm³.

Exemplar Ex 12.4 Q155 marks
A building is in the form of a cylinder surmounted by a hemispherical vaulted dome and contains 41 19/21 m³ of air. If the internal diameter of dome is equal to its total height above the floor, find the height of the building.
Step 1 [1 mark] Let the dome radius be r; internal diameter 2r equals the total height H, so H = 2r.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Let the dome radius be r; internal diameter 2r equals the total height H, so H = 2r.
  2. [1 mark] Cylinder height = H − r = 2r − r = r, with radius r.
  3. [1 mark] Volume = πr²(r) + (2/3)πr³ = (5/3)πr³ = 41 19/21 = 880/21.
  4. [1 mark] (5/3)(22/7)r³ = 880/21 ⇒ (110/21)r³ = 880/21 ⇒ r³ = 8 ⇒ r = 2.
  5. [1 mark] Height of building H = 2r = 4 m.

Answer: Height of the building = 4 m.
