Exemplar Q11 mark
What happens when a solution of an acid is mixed with a solution of a base in a test tube? (i) The temperature of the solution increases (ii) The temperature of the solution decreases (iii) The temperature of the solution remains the same (iv) Salt formation takes place

Step-by-step solution

  1. Acid + Base → Salt + Water (neutralisation) — this reaction is exothermic, so temperature INCREASES (i) ✓ and salt IS FORMED (iv) ✓.
  2. Temperature does not decrease (rules out ii) nor stay same (rules out iii).
  3. Answer: (d) (i) and (iv).

Answer: (i) and (iv) — option (d)

Exemplar Q21 mark
An aqueous solution turns red litmus solution blue. Excess addition of which of the following solution would reverse the change?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Red→blue means the solution is BASIC. To reverse, we need an ACID.
  2. Of the options, only HCl is a strong acid. Baking powder, lime, NH₄OH are all basic.
  3. Excess HCl turns the litmus back to red. Answer: (D)

Answer: Hydrochloric acid — option (d)

Exemplar Q71 mark
A sample of soil is mixed with water and allowed to settle. The clear supernatant solution turns the pH paper yellowish-orange. Which of the following would change the colour of this pH paper to greenish-blue?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Yellowish-orange on pH paper indicates acidic soil (pH ~4-5).
  2. To turn the paper greenish-blue (pH ~8-9, alkaline), we need to add a basic substance.
  3. Lemon juice and vinegar are acidic; common salt is neutral. Only an antacid (mild base) would shift toward alkaline. Answer: (D)

Answer: An antacid — option (d)

Exemplar Q91 mark
If a few drops of a concentrated acid accidentally spills over the hand of a student, what should be done?

Step-by-step solution

  1. First: dilute the acid by washing immediately with plenty of water (reduces the H⁺ concentration on skin).
  2. Then: apply paste of mild base NaHCO₃ to neutralise residual acid. Strong base (like NaOH) is corrosive too — don't use it on skin. Answer: (B)

Answer: Wash with water then apply NaHCO₃ paste — option (b)

Exemplar Q101 mark
Sodium hydrogencarbonate when added to acetic acid evolves a gas. Which of the following statements are true about the gas evolved? (i) It turns lime water milky (ii) It extinguishes a burning splinter (iii) It dissolves in a solution of sodium hydroxide (iv) It has a pungent odour

Step-by-step solution

  1. NaHCO₃ + CH₃COOH → CH₃COONa + H₂O + CO₂↑. Gas is CO₂.
  2. (i) CO₂ turns lime water milky ✓. (ii) CO₂ extinguishes flames ✓. (iii) CO₂ + 2NaOH → Na₂CO₃ + H₂O — dissolves in NaOH ✓. (iv) CO₂ is odourless ✗.
  3. Correct: (i), (ii) and (iii). Answer: (b).

Answer: (i), (ii) and (iii) — option (b)

Exemplar Q121 mark
One of the constituents of baking powder is sodium hydrogencarbonate, the other constituent is

Step-by-step solution

  1. Baking powder = NaHCO₃ + tartaric acid (a mild edible acid).
  2. When mixed with water, the acid reacts with NaHCO₃ to release CO₂, making the cake rise.
  3. Other acids listed are mineral acids (HCl, H₂SO₄) — too strong, would taste bad / be unsafe. Answer: (B)

Answer: Tartaric acid — option (b)

Exemplar Q141 mark
Which of the following statements is correct about an aqueous solution of an acid and of a base? (i) Higher the pH, stronger the acid (ii) Higher the pH, weaker the acid (iii) Lower the pH, stronger the base (iv) Lower the pH, weaker the base

Step-by-step solution

  1. Acidic strength: lower pH = stronger acid. So higher pH means WEAKER acid → (ii) ✓.
  2. Basic strength: higher pH = stronger base. So lower pH means WEAKER base → (iv) ✓.
  3. (i) and (iii) are wrong (reversed logic). Answer: (D)

Answer: (ii) and (iv) — option (d)

Exemplar Q161 mark
Which of the following phenomena occur, when a small amount of acid is added to water? (i) Ionisation (ii) Neutralisation (iii) Dilution (iv) Salt formation

Step-by-step solution

  1. When acid is added to water: (i) Acid ionises in water releasing H⁺ ions ✓. (iii) The acid gets diluted (volume increases, [H⁺] decreases per unit volume) ✓.
  2. No base present → no (ii) neutralisation; no (iv) salt formation. Answer: (B)

Answer: (i) and (iii) — option (b)

Exemplar Q171 mark
Which one of the following can be used as an acid-base indicator by a visually impaired student?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Visually impaired students cannot detect colour changes; they need an OLFACTORY (smell-based) indicator.
  2. Vanilla essence has a strong smell — its smell disappears in basic solutions. So it can be used by detecting whether smell remains (acidic) or vanishes (basic).
  3. Litmus, turmeric, petunia leaves are colour-based — not suitable. Answer: (C)

Answer: Vanilla essence — option (c)

Exemplar Q291 mark
Which of the following are present in a dilute aqueous solution of hydrochloric acid?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Dilute HCl(aq) ionises completely (strong acid): HCl + H₂O → H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻.
  2. Main species: H₃O⁺ and Cl⁻ ions. Answer: (A)

Answer: H₃O⁺ + Cl⁻ — option (a)

Exemplar Q301 mark
Identify the correct representation of reaction occurring during chlor-alkali process:

Step-by-step solution

  1. Chlor-alkali: electrolysis of aqueous NaCl (brine).
  2. Reactants: NaCl(aq) and H₂O(l). Products: NaOH(aq) (stays in solution), Cl₂(g) (at anode), H₂(g) (at cathode).
  3. Option (d) has all correct state symbols. Answer: (D)

Answer: 2NaCl(aq)+2H₂O(l)→2NaOH(aq)+Cl₂(g)+H₂(g) — option (d)

Exemplar Q353 marks
What happens when nitric acid is added to egg shell?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Egg shell is mainly calcium carbonate (CaCO₃).
  2. [0.5 mark] Acid + carbonate → salt + water + CO₂.
  3. [0.5 mark] Reaction: CaCO₃(s) + 2HNO₃(aq) → Ca(NO₃)₂(aq) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)↑.
  4. [1 mark] Observation: brisk effervescence, shell dissolves, gas turns lime water milky.

Answer: CaCO₃ + 2HNO₃ → Ca(NO₃)₂ + H₂O + CO₂↑. Egg shell dissolves with effervescence.
