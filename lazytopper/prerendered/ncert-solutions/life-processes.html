In-text Q1 · Section 5.13 marks
Why is diffusion insufficient to meet the oxygen requirements of multi-cellular organisms like humans?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Diffusion rate is fast over micrometres but very slow over centimetres.
  2. [0.5 mark] Multicellular organisms have cells located deep inside; surface area-to-volume ratio is low.
  3. [0.5 mark] Diffusion alone cannot supply oxygen rapidly enough to internal cells — they would suffocate.
  4. [1 mark] Hence dedicated respiratory + circulatory systems are needed for transport.

Answer: Diffusion is too slow for deep-seated cells in large multicellular organisms — specialised systems needed.

In-text Q2 · Section 5.43 marks
Why is it necessary to separate oxygenated and deoxygenated blood in mammals and birds?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Mammals and birds are warm-blooded (endothermic) — need constant high O₂ supply to maintain body temperature.
  2. [0.5 mark] 4-chambered heart keeps O₂-rich (left) and O₂-poor (right) blood separate.
  3. [0.5 mark] Pure O₂-rich blood reaches tissues → maximum efficiency of cellular respiration.
  4. [1 mark] Cold-blooded animals (fish, reptiles) have less efficient circulation as they don't need constant temperature.

Answer: Warm-blooded animals need high energy → separation ensures efficient O₂ supply.

In-text Q3 · Section 5.42 marks
What are the components of the transport system in highly organised plants?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Xylem: water + minerals upward (root → leaf).
  2. [1 mark] Phloem: food (sugars) from leaves → all parts (translocation, bidirectional).

Answer: Xylem (water/minerals from roots) and phloem (food from leaves).

In-text Q5 · Section 5.42 marks
How is food transported in plants?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] Food (sucrose) made in leaves is loaded into phloem sieve tubes (active transport, requires ATP).
  2. [0.5 mark] Sucrose loading raises osmotic pressure → water enters from xylem.
  3. [0.5 mark] Increased pressure pushes phloem contents to areas of low pressure (sinks: storage tissues, roots, fruits).
  4. [0.5 mark] Process is bidirectional, called translocation.

Answer: Food transported as sucrose in phloem via translocation (active, energy-using, bidirectional).

Exercise Q53 marks
How are fats digested in our bodies? Where does this process take place?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Fats enter small intestine from stomach.
  2. [0.5 mark] Bile (from liver) emulsifies fats into smaller droplets — increases surface area.
  3. [0.5 mark] Pancreatic lipase + intestinal lipase break emulsified fats → fatty acids + glycerol.
  4. [1 mark] Fatty acids and glycerol absorbed through villi into lymph and bloodstream.

Answer: Fats are digested in SMALL INTESTINE. Bile emulsifies + lipase breaks them to fatty acids + glycerol.

Exercise Q93 marks
How are the alveoli designed to maximise the exchange of gases?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Large number → huge total surface area (~80 m²).
  2. [0.5 mark] Thin walls (one cell thick) → short diffusion distance.
  3. [0.5 mark] Rich blood supply (capillary network surrounding each alveolus).
  4. [1 mark] Moist inner surface allows gas dissolution before diffusion.

Answer: Many alveoli + thin walls + rich capillary network + moist surface = maximal gas exchange.

Exercise Q115 marks
Describe double circulation of blood in human beings. Why is it necessary?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Double circulation = blood passes through heart twice per cycle.
  2. [1 mark] Pulmonary circuit: right ventricle → pulmonary artery → lungs → pulmonary vein → left atrium. (Picks up O₂.)
  3. [0.5 mark] Systemic circuit: left ventricle → aorta → all body parts → vena cava → right atrium. (Delivers O₂, picks up CO₂.)
  4. [0.5 mark] 4-chambered heart keeps the two circuits separate.
  5. [1 mark] Necessity: ensures pure O₂-rich blood is delivered to body; high metabolic efficiency for warm-blooded animals.
  6. [1 mark] Without separation, mixed blood would have lower O₂ content → less efficient → can't maintain constant temperature.

Answer: Two circuits: pulmonary (heart-lungs) and systemic (heart-body). 4-chambered heart keeps O₂-rich/O₂-poor separate. Necessary for warm-blooded efficiency.
