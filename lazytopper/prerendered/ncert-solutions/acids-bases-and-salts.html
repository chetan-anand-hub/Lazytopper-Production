In-text Q2 · Section 2.12 marks
Which gas is usually liberated when an acid reacts with a metal? Illustrate with an example. How will you test for the presence of this gas?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Acid + active metal → salt + hydrogen gas. The metal must be above hydrogen in the reactivity series.
  2. [0.5 mark] Example: Zn(s) + dilute H₂SO₄(aq) → ZnSO₄(aq) + H₂(g)↑.
  3. [0.5 mark] Test: bring a lighted matchstick / burning splinter to the mouth of the test tube. A 'pop' sound confirms H₂ (the gas burns explosively in air to form water).

Answer: H₂ gas. Example Zn+H₂SO₄→ZnSO₄+H₂. Test: pop sound with burning splinter.

In-text Q4 · Section 2.2.12 marks
While diluting an acid, why is it recommended that the acid should be added to water and not water to the acid?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Mixing concentrated acid with water releases a large amount of heat (highly exothermic).
  2. [0.5 mark] If water is poured into the acid, the heat heats up the small volume of water rapidly — may cause splashing of acid and glass breakage.
  3. [0.5 mark] Adding acid slowly to water spreads the heat across a large water mass, keeping temperature rise manageable — safe.

Answer: Dilution is exothermic. Adding water to acid causes splashing/burns; adding acid to water is safe.

In-text Q6 · Section 2.2.12 marks
How is the concentration of hydroxide ions (OH⁻) affected when excess base is dissolved in a solution of sodium hydroxide?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] NaOH(s) + water → Na⁺(aq) + OH⁻(aq). Each formula unit of dissolved NaOH contributes one OH⁻.
  2. [0.5 mark] Adding more NaOH means more OH⁻ ions per unit volume → [OH⁻] increases.
  3. [0.5 mark] The pH of the solution increases (becomes more strongly basic).

Answer: [OH⁻] increases as more base dissolves.

In-text Q2 · Section 2.32 marks
What effect does the concentration of H⁺(aq) ions have on the nature of the solution?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Acidity is determined by the H⁺(aq) ion concentration.
  2. [0.5 mark] High [H⁺] → low pH → strongly acidic solution.
  3. [0.5 mark] Low [H⁺] → high pH → basic/alkaline solution.

Answer: Higher [H⁺] → more acidic; lower [H⁺] → more basic.

In-text Q3 · Section 2.32 marks
Do basic solutions also have H⁺(aq) ions? If yes, then why are these basic?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Water ionises slightly, so every aqueous solution contains both H⁺(aq) and OH⁻(aq) ions.
  2. [0.5 mark] In a basic solution, [OH⁻] > [H⁺]; the H⁺ ion concentration is small but not zero.
  3. [0.5 mark] Since OH⁻ dominates, the solution is basic.

Answer: Yes, but [OH⁻] > [H⁺] in basic solutions, hence basic in nature.

In-text Q4 · Section 2.32 marks
Under what soil condition do you think a farmer would treat the soil of his fields with quick lime (calcium oxide) or slaked lime (calcium hydroxide) or chalk (calcium carbonate)?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Quick lime (CaO), slaked lime (Ca(OH)₂), and chalk (CaCO₃) are all basic materials.
  2. [0.5 mark] If soil is acidic (low pH), crops cannot grow well. Adding these basic substances neutralises soil acidity.
  3. [0.5 mark] After treatment, pH rises to the optimum range required for healthy crop growth.

Answer: When soil is acidic — basic substances neutralise the acidity.

In-text Q4 · Section 2.43 marks
What will happen if a solution of sodium hydrogencarbonate is heated? Give the equation of the reaction involved.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Sodium hydrogencarbonate (NaHCO₃) is a baking soda; it is thermally unstable.
  2. [0.5 mark] On heating it undergoes decomposition: 2NaHCO₃(s) --Δ--> Na₂CO₃(s) + H₂O(l) + CO₂(g)↑.
  3. [0.5 mark] Balance check: Na 2=2, H 2=2, C 2=2, O 6=3+1+2=6 ✓.
  4. [1 mark] This reaction is used in baking — CO₂ released makes the bread/cake rise.

Answer: 2NaHCO₃ --Heat--> Na₂CO₃ + H₂O + CO₂↑.

In-text Q5 · Section 2.42 marks
Write an equation to show the reaction between Plaster of Paris and water.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Plaster of Paris has formula CaSO₄·½H₂O (calcium sulphate hemihydrate).
  2. [0.5 mark] When mixed with water, it absorbs additional water molecules to form gypsum (CaSO₄·2H₂O).
  3. [0.5 mark] Equation: CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O. The set product is hard.

Answer: CaSO₄·½H₂O + 1½H₂O → CaSO₄·2H₂O (gypsum).

A solution turns red litmus blue, its pH is likely to be

Step-by-step solution

  1. Red litmus → blue indicates a BASIC (alkaline) solution.
  2. Basic solutions have pH > 7. Among 1, 4, 5, 10 — only 10 is > 7. Answer: (D)

Exercise Q21 mark
A solution reacts with crushed egg-shells to give a gas that turns lime-water milky. The solution contains

Step-by-step solution

  1. Egg-shells are mainly CaCO₃ (calcium carbonate).
  2. An acid + carbonate → CO₂ gas, which turns lime water milky.
  3. Among the options, only HCl is an acid; the others are neutral salts.
  4. CaCO₃ + 2HCl → CaCl₂ + H₂O + CO₂. Answer: (B)

Answer: HCl — option (b)

Exercise Q41 mark
Which one of the following types of medicines is used for treating indigestion?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Indigestion is caused by excess HCl in the stomach.
  2. Antacid is a mild base (e.g., milk of magnesia Mg(OH)₂) that neutralises the excess acid → relieves indigestion.
  3. Antibiotic = kills bacteria; Analgesic = painkiller; Antiseptic = prevents infection. Answer: (C)

Answer: Antacid — option (c)

Exercise Q93 marks
Five solutions A, B, C, D and E when tested with universal indicator showed pH as 4, 1, 11, 7 and 9, respectively. Which solution is (a) neutral? (b) strongly alkaline? (c) strongly acidic? (d) weakly acidic? (e) weakly alkaline? Arrange the pH in increasing order of hydrogen-ion concentration.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] pH 7 = neutral → D.
  2. [0.5 mark] pH > 10 = strongly alkaline → C (pH 11).
  3. [0.5 mark] pH < 2 = strongly acidic → B (pH 1).
  4. [0.5 mark] pH between 4–6 = weakly acidic → A (pH 4); pH between 8–10 = weakly alkaline → E (pH 9).
  5. [0.5 mark] Higher pH = lower [H⁺]. So increasing [H⁺] is from highest pH to lowest pH: C < E < D < A < B.

Answer: (a) D (b) C (c) B (d) A (e) E. Increasing [H⁺]: C<E<D<A<B.

Exercise Q102 marks
Equal lengths of magnesium ribbons are taken in test tubes A and B. Hydrochloric acid (HCl) is added to test tube A, while acetic acid (CH₃COOH) is added to test tube B. Amount and concentration taken for both the acids are same. In which test tube will the fizzing occur more vigorously and why?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] HCl is a STRONG acid — completely ionises in water → high [H⁺].
  2. [0.5 mark] CH₃COOH is a WEAK acid — only partially ionises → much lower [H⁺].
  3. [0.5 mark] Rate of H₂ release ∝ [H⁺], so HCl gives more vigorous fizzing.

Answer: Test tube A (HCl) — HCl is a strong acid with higher [H⁺] → reacts faster, more fizz.

Exercise Q142 marks
What is a neutralisation reaction? Give two examples.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] General form: acid + base → salt + water. The H⁺ from acid combines with OH⁻ from base to form H₂O.
  2. [0.5 mark] Example 1: HCl(aq) + NaOH(aq) → NaCl(aq) + H₂O(l).
  3. [0.5 mark] Example 2: H₂SO₄(aq) + 2KOH(aq) → K₂SO₄(aq) + 2H₂O(l).

Answer: Acid + base → salt + water. Examples: HCl+NaOH→NaCl+H₂O; H₂SO₄+2KOH→K₂SO₄+2H₂O.

Exercise Q152 marks
Give two important uses of washing soda and baking soda.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] Washing soda use 1: Cleaning agent in laundries; removes permanent hardness of water.
  2. [0.5 mark] Washing soda use 2: Manufacture of glass, soap, paper; sodium compounds production.
  3. [0.5 mark] Baking soda use 1: Component of baking powder (with tartaric acid) — releases CO₂ which makes baked goods fluffy.
  4. [0.5 mark] Baking soda use 2: Mild antacid for indigestion; also used in fire extinguishers.

Answer: Washing soda: laundry cleaning + manufacture of glass/soap. Baking soda: baking + antacid.
