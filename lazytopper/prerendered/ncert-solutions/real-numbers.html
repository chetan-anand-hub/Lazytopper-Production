Exercise 1.1

NCERT Ex 1.1 Q1(ii)1 mark
The prime factorisation of 156 is
Step 1 Divide 156 by 2: 156 ÷ 2 = 78.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Divide 156 by 2: 156 ÷ 2 = 78.
  2. 78 ÷ 2 = 39. 39 ÷ 3 = 13. 13 is prime.
  3. So 156 = 2 × 2 × 3 × 13 = 2² × 3 × 13.

Answer: 2² × 3 × 13 — option (a).

NCERT Ex 1.1 Q41 mark
If HCF(306, 657) = 9, then LCM(306, 657) is
Step 1 For two positive integers a and b: HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b.

Step-by-step solution

  1. For two positive integers a and b: HCF(a, b) × LCM(a, b) = a × b.
  2. LCM(306, 657) = (306 × 657) / HCF(306, 657) = 201042 / 9.
  3. 201042 / 9 = 22338.

Answer: 22338 — option (a).

NCERT Ex 1.1 Q2(i)1 mark
The LCM and HCF of 26 and 91 are respectively
Step 1 26 = 2 × 13. 91 = 7 × 13.

Step-by-step solution

  1. 26 = 2 × 13. 91 = 7 × 13.
  2. HCF = product of smallest powers of common primes = 13.
  3. LCM = product of greatest powers of all primes = 2 × 7 × 13 = 182.
  4. Check: HCF × LCM = 13 × 182 = 2366 = 26 × 91. ✓

Answer: LCM = 182, HCF = 13 — option (a).

NCERT Ex 1.1 Q1(iv)2 marks
Express 5005 as a product of its prime factors.
Step 1 5005 is divisible by 5 (ends in 5): 5005 ÷ 5 = 1001.

Step-by-step solution

  1. 5005 is divisible by 5 (ends in 5): 5005 ÷ 5 = 1001.
  2. 1001 ÷ 7 = 143.
  3. 143 ÷ 11 = 13. 13 is prime.
  4. So 5005 = 5 × 7 × 11 × 13.

Answer: 5005 = 5 × 7 × 11 × 13.

NCERT Ex 1.1 Q2(ii)2 marks
Find the HCF and LCM of 510 and 92 by the prime factorisation method.
Step 1 510 = 2 × 255 = 2 × 3 × 85 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 17.

Step-by-step solution

  1. 510 = 2 × 255 = 2 × 3 × 85 = 2 × 3 × 5 × 17.
  2. 92 = 2 × 46 = 2 × 2 × 23 = 2² × 23.
  3. HCF = product of smallest powers of common primes = 2¹ = 2.
  4. LCM = 2² × 3 × 5 × 17 × 23 = 4 × 5865 = 23460.

NCERT Ex 1.1 Q62 marks
Explain why 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 is a composite number.
Step 1 Factor 13 from both terms: 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 = 13 × (7 × 11 + 1).

Step-by-step solution

  1. Factor 13 from both terms: 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 = 13 × (7 × 11 + 1).
  2. Compute 7 × 11 + 1 = 77 + 1 = 78.
  3. So the number = 13 × 78. Both 13 > 1 and 78 > 1.
  4. A number with more than two positive factors is composite. Hence 7 × 11 × 13 + 13 is composite.

Answer: 13 × 78 = 1014, composite (has factors 13 and 78 besides 1 and itself).

NCERT Ex 1.1 Q3(ii)3 marks
Find the LCM and HCF of 17, 23 and 29 by applying the prime factorisation method.
Step 1 Prime factorisations: 17 = 17; 23 = 23; 29 = 29. (All three are themselves prime.)

Step-by-step solution

  1. Prime factorisations: 17 = 17; 23 = 23; 29 = 29. (All three are themselves prime.)
  2. There is no prime common to all three → HCF = 1.
  3. LCM = product of all distinct primes (each to the power 1) = 17 × 23 × 29.
  4. 17 × 23 = 391; 391 × 29 = 11339.
  5. Hence HCF = 1 and LCM = 11339.

Answer: HCF = 1; LCM = 11339.

NCERT Ex 1.1 Q73 marks
There is a circular path around a sports field. Sonia takes 18 minutes to drive one round of the field, while Ravi takes 12 minutes for the same. Suppose they both start at the same point and at the same time, and go in the same direction. After how many minutes will they meet again at the starting point?
Step 1 They both meet at the start when each has completed an integer number of rounds — i.e. after a time that is a multiple of both 18 and 12.

Step-by-step solution

  1. They both meet at the start when each has completed an integer number of rounds — i.e. after a time that is a multiple of both 18 and 12.
  2. The smallest such time is LCM(18, 12).
  3. Prime factorisations: 18 = 2 × 3²; 12 = 2² × 3.
  4. LCM = 2² × 3² = 4 × 9 = 36.
  5. Hence they meet at the starting point after 36 minutes.

Answer: 36 minutes.

Exercise 1.2

NCERT Ex 1.2 Q15 marks
Prove that √5 is irrational.
Step 1 Assume √5 is rational. Write √5 = a/b with gcd(a, b) = 1, b ≠ 0.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Assume √5 is rational. Write √5 = a/b with gcd(a, b) = 1, b ≠ 0.
  2. Squaring: 5 = a²/b² ⇒ a² = 5b² ⇒ 5 | a².
  3. Since 5 is prime, 5 | a² ⇒ 5 | a. Write a = 5c for some integer c.
  4. Substitute: (5c)² = 5b² ⇒ 25c² = 5b² ⇒ b² = 5c². Hence 5 | b² ⇒ 5 | b.
  5. Now 5 is a common factor of a and b, contradicting gcd(a, b) = 1. Hence √5 is irrational.

Answer: √5 is irrational.

NCERT Ex 1.2 Q25 marks
Prove that 3 + 2√5 is irrational, given that √5 is irrational.
Step 1 Suppose, for contradiction, that 3 + 2√5 is rational. Then 3 + 2√5 = a/b with a, b integers, b ≠ 0.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Suppose, for contradiction, that 3 + 2√5 is rational. Then 3 + 2√5 = a/b with a, b integers, b ≠ 0.
  2. Isolate √5: 2√5 = a/b − 3 = (a − 3b)/b.
  3. Divide by 2: √5 = (a − 3b)/(2b).
  4. RHS is a ratio of integers (with non-zero denominator) → rational.
  5. So √5 would be rational, contradicting the fact that √5 is irrational. Hence 3 + 2√5 is irrational.

Answer: 3 + 2√5 is irrational.

NCERT Example 43 marks
Find the HCF and LCM of 6, 72 and 120 using the prime factorisation method.
Step 1 6 = 2 × 3.

Step-by-step solution

  1. 6 = 2 × 3.
  2. 72 = 8 × 9 = 2³ × 3².
  3. 120 = 8 × 15 = 2³ × 3 × 5.
  4. HCF = smallest powers of common primes = 2¹ × 3¹ = 6.
  5. LCM = greatest powers of all primes that appear = 2³ × 3² × 5 = 8 × 9 × 5 = 360.

Answer: HCF = 6; LCM = 360.

NCERT Example 63 marks
Show that 5 − √3 is irrational, given that √3 is irrational.
Step 1 Assume 5 − √3 is rational. Then 5 − √3 = a/b with integers a, b and b ≠ 0.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Assume 5 − √3 is rational. Then 5 − √3 = a/b with integers a, b and b ≠ 0.
  2. Rearrange: √3 = 5 − a/b = (5b − a)/b.
  3. RHS is a ratio of two integers with non-zero denominator → rational.
  4. So √3 would be rational, contradicting the given fact that √3 is irrational.
  5. Hence our assumption is wrong. Therefore 5 − √3 is irrational.

Answer: 5 − √3 is irrational (by contradiction, using √3 irrational).

NCERT Example 55 marks
Prove that √3 is irrational.
Step 1 Suppose √3 is rational. Then √3 = a/b with gcd(a, b) = 1 and b ≠ 0.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Suppose √3 is rational. Then √3 = a/b with gcd(a, b) = 1 and b ≠ 0.
  2. Squaring: 3 = a²/b² ⇒ a² = 3b² ⇒ 3 | a².
  3. By the prime-divides-square theorem, 3 | a. Write a = 3c.
  4. Substitute: 9c² = 3b² ⇒ b² = 3c². So 3 | b², hence 3 | b.
  5. Now 3 | a and 3 | b, contradicting gcd(a, b) = 1. Hence √3 is irrational.

Answer: √3 is irrational.

A sweet seller has 420 kaju barfis and 130 badam barfis. She wants to stack them in such a way that each stack has the same number, and they take up the least area of the tray. (i) What number of barfis should be placed in each stack? (ii) How many stacks of kaju barfis and how many stacks of badam barfis will be formed?
Step 1 To minimise tray area, make each stack as TALL as possible while keeping a uniform stack size that divides both 420 and 130 evenly.

Step-by-step solution

  1. To minimise tray area, make each stack as TALL as possible while keeping a uniform stack size that divides both 420 and 130 evenly.
  2. Largest such number is HCF(420, 130). Factorise: 420 = 2² × 3 × 5 × 7, 130 = 2 × 5 × 13.
  3. HCF = product of smallest powers of common primes = 2 × 5 = 10.
  4. So each stack contains 10 barfis. Kaju stacks = 420/10 = 42. Badam stacks = 130/10 = 13.
  5. Total stacks on the tray = 42 + 13 = 55, each of height 10.

Answer: (i) 10 barfis per stack; (ii) 42 kaju stacks and 13 badam stacks.
