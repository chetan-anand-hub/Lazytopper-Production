In-text questions

In-text Q2 · Section 4.22 marks
What are the two properties of carbon which lead to the huge number of carbon compounds we see around us?

Step-by-step solution

  1. (i) Catenation: C–C bonds are very strong, allowing chains, branches, rings.
  2. (ii) Tetravalency: C forms 4 covalent bonds, can combine with many elements, giving huge structural diversity.

Answer: Catenation and tetravalency.

Exercise Q11 mark
Ethane, with the molecular formula C₂H₆ has

Step-by-step solution

  1. Ethane = H₃C–CH₃. Bonds: 6 C–H bonds + 1 C–C bond = 7 covalent bonds.
  2. Total = 7. Answer: (B)

Answer: 7 — option (b)

Exercise Q31 mark
While cooking, if the bottom of the vessel is getting blackened on the outside, it means that

Step-by-step solution

  1. Black deposit = soot/carbon = INCOMPLETE combustion.
  2. Complete combustion gives only CO₂ and H₂O (no soot). So the fuel is not burning completely (insufficient O₂). Answer: (B)

Answer: Fuel is not burning completely — option (b)

Exercise Q63 marks
What is an homologous series? Explain with an example.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Homologous series = compounds with same functional group + same general formula, differing by CH₂.
  2. Example: Alkane series CₙH₂ₙ₊₂. Members CH₄, C₂H₆, C₃H₈, C₄H₁₀, C₅H₁₂.
  3. Each consecutive member differs by +CH₂ (mass 14).
  4. Properties: similar chemical behaviour; m.p./b.p. gradually increase with n.

Answer: Series of compounds with same functional group + general formula, differing by CH₂. Example: alkanes CₙH₂ₙ₊₂.

Exercise Q155 marks
Explain the mechanism of the cleaning action of soaps.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Soap has dual nature: ionic hydrophilic head (-COO⁻Na⁺) + hydrocarbon hydrophobic tail.
  2. Tails attach to oil/grease (dirt is held on cloth by greasy film); heads face the water.
  3. Many soap molecules form a sphere — a MICELLE — with dirt enclosed at the centre and ionic heads outside.
  4. Micelles are stable in water (suspended as colloidal particles).
  5. Agitation (rubbing, machine action) dislodges the micelles from cloth fibres.
  6. Rinsing carries away the suspended micelles → cloth becomes clean.

Answer: Soap tails grab oil/dirt forming micelles; heads dissolve in water; agitation dislodges micelles; rinse takes them away.
