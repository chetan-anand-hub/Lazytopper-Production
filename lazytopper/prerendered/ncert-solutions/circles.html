Exercise 10.1

NCERT Ex 10.1 Q31 mark
A tangent PQ at a point P of a circle of radius 5 cm meets a line through the centre O at a point Q so that OQ = 12 cm. Length PQ is
Step 1 By Theorem 10.1, OP ⊥ PQ at the point of contact.

Step-by-step solution

  1. By Theorem 10.1, OP ⊥ PQ at the point of contact.
  2. In right ∆OPQ, by Pythagoras: PQ² = OQ² − OP² = 12² − 5² = 144 − 25 = 119.
  3. PQ = √119 cm.

Answer: √119 cm — option (d).

NCERT Ex 10.1 Q22 marks
Fill in: (i) A tangent to a circle intersects it in ___ point(s). (ii) A line intersecting a circle in two points is called a ___. (iii) A circle can have ___ parallel tangents at the most. (iv) The common point of a tangent and the circle is called the ___.
Step 1 (i) Exactly one point — by definition of a tangent.

Step-by-step solution

  1. (i) Exactly one point — by definition of a tangent.
  2. (ii) A secant.
  3. (iii) Two parallel tangents at the most — one on each side of the centre.
  4. (iv) The point of contact.

Answer: (i) one; (ii) secant; (iii) two; (iv) point of contact.

Exercise 10.2

NCERT Ex 10.2 Q11 mark
From a point Q, the length of the tangent to a circle is 24 cm and the distance of Q from the centre is 25 cm. The radius of the circle is
Step 1 Let P be the point of contact and O the centre. OP ⊥ PQ.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let P be the point of contact and O the centre. OP ⊥ PQ.
  2. OP² = OQ² − PQ² = 25² − 24² = 625 − 576 = 49.
  3. OP = 7 cm.

Answer: 7 cm — option (a).

NCERT Ex 10.2 Q31 mark
Tangents PA and PB from a point P to a circle with centre O are inclined to each other at an angle of 80°. Then ∠POA equals
Step 1 By Theorem 10.2, PA = PB, and ∆OPA ≅ ∆OPB. So ∠OPA = ∠OPB = 40°.

Step-by-step solution

  1. By Theorem 10.2, PA = PB, and ∆OPA ≅ ∆OPB. So ∠OPA = ∠OPB = 40°.
  2. ∠OAP = 90° (radius ⊥ tangent).
  3. In ∆OPA: ∠POA = 180° − 90° − 40° = 50°.

Answer: 50° — option (a).

NCERT Ex 10.2 Q62 marks
The length of a tangent from a point A at distance 5 cm from the centre of the circle is 4 cm. Find the radius of the circle.
Step 1 Let r be the radius and P the point of contact. Then OP ⊥ AP and OA = 5, AP = 4.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let r be the radius and P the point of contact. Then OP ⊥ AP and OA = 5, AP = 4.
  2. In right ∆OPA: r² = OA² − AP² = 25 − 16 = 9.
  3. r = 3 cm.

Answer: Radius = 3 cm.

NCERT Ex 10.2 Q115 marks
Prove that the parallelogram circumscribing a circle is a rhombus.
Step 1 Let ABCD be a parallelogram circumscribing a circle, touching AB, BC, CD, DA at P, Q, R, S.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let ABCD be a parallelogram circumscribing a circle, touching AB, BC, CD, DA at P, Q, R, S.
  2. By equal tangents: AP = AS, BP = BQ, CQ = CR, DR = DS.
  3. AB + CD = (AP + PB) + (CR + RD) = (AS + SD) + (BQ + QC) = AD + BC.
  4. Since ABCD is a parallelogram, AB = CD and AD = BC. So 2AB = 2AD ⇒ AB = AD.
  5. Hence all sides AB = BC = CD = DA, i.e., ABCD is a rhombus.

Answer: Proved: a parallelogram that circumscribes a circle is a rhombus.
