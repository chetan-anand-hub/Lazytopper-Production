Which of the following CANNOT be the probability of an event?
Step 1 A probability must satisfy 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.

Step-by-step solution

  1. A probability must satisfy 0 ≤ P(E) ≤ 1.
  2. −1.5 is negative, so it lies outside this range and cannot be a probability.

Answer: −1.5 — option (b).

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q51 mark
If P(E) = 0.05, then P(not E) equals
Step 1 P(E) + P(not E) = 1.

Step-by-step solution

  2. P(not E) = 1 − 0.05 = 0.95.

Answer: 0.95 — option (c).

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q13(iii)1 mark
A die is thrown once. The probability of getting an odd number is
Step 1 Total outcomes = 6 (faces 1–6).

Step-by-step solution

  1. Total outcomes = 6 (faces 1–6).
  2. Favourable odd outcomes: 1, 3, 5 → 3 outcomes.
  3. P(odd) = 3/6 = 1/2.

Answer: 1/2 — option (c).

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q62 marks
A bag contains only lemon-flavoured candies. Malini takes out one candy without looking. Find the probability that she takes out (i) an orange-flavoured candy, (ii) a lemon-flavoured candy.
Step 1 (i) There are no orange-flavoured candies in the bag, so favourable outcomes = 0. P(orange) = 0 (impossible event).

Step-by-step solution

  1. (i) There are no orange-flavoured candies in the bag, so favourable outcomes = 0. P(orange) = 0 (impossible event).
  2. (ii) Every candy in the bag is lemon-flavoured, so P(lemon) = 1 (sure event).

Answer: (i) 0; (ii) 1.

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q82 marks
A bag contains 3 red balls and 5 black balls. A ball is drawn at random. Find the probability that the ball drawn is (i) red, (ii) not red.
Step 1 Total balls = 3 + 5 = 8.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Total balls = 3 + 5 = 8.
  2. (i) P(red) = 3/8.
  3. (ii) P(not red) = 1 − 3/8 = 5/8 (or directly 5 black/8 total).

Answer: (i) 3/8; (ii) 5/8.

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q32 marks
Why is tossing a coin considered a fair way of deciding which team should get the ball at the beginning of a football game?
Step 1 A coin is symmetrical and unbiased; tossing it at random gives either head or tail with equal probability 1/2.

Step-by-step solution

  1. A coin is symmetrical and unbiased; tossing it at random gives either head or tail with equal probability 1/2.
  2. Since each team is equally likely to win the toss, no team has a built-in advantage.
  3. Hence the coin toss is a fair (impartial) way to assign the ball.

Answer: Because the two outcomes (head, tail) are equally likely, giving both teams an equal chance.

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q93 marks
A box contains 5 red marbles, 8 white marbles and 4 green marbles. One marble is drawn at random. What is the probability that the marble drawn is (i) red, (ii) white, (iii) not green?
Step 1 Total marbles = 5 + 8 + 4 = 17.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Total marbles = 5 + 8 + 4 = 17.
  2. (i) P(red) = 5/17.
  3. (ii) P(white) = 8/17.
  4. (iii) P(not green) = 1 − 4/17 = 13/17.

Answer: (i) 5/17; (ii) 8/17; (iii) 13/17.

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q103 marks
A piggy bank contains hundred 50p coins, fifty ₹1 coins, twenty ₹2 coins and ten ₹5 coins. One coin falls out at random. Find the probability that the coin (i) is a 50p coin, (ii) is not a ₹5 coin.
Step 1 Total coins = 100 + 50 + 20 + 10 = 180.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Total coins = 100 + 50 + 20 + 10 = 180.
  2. (i) P(50p) = 100/180 = 5/9.
  3. (ii) Number of coins that are not ₹5 = 180 − 10 = 170. P(not ₹5) = 170/180 = 17/18.

Answer: (i) 5/9; (ii) 17/18.

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q14(i,ii,iii)3 marks
One card is drawn from a well-shuffled deck of 52 cards. Find the probability of getting (i) a king of red colour, (ii) a face card, (iii) a red face card.
Step 1 (i) Red kings: king of hearts and king of diamonds → 2 favourable. P = 2/52 = 1/26.

Step-by-step solution

  1. (i) Red kings: king of hearts and king of diamonds → 2 favourable. P = 2/52 = 1/26.
  2. (ii) Face cards = J, Q, K of each suit × 4 suits = 12. P(face) = 12/52 = 3/13.
  3. (iii) Red face cards: J, Q, K of hearts + J, Q, K of diamonds = 6. P = 6/52 = 3/26.

Answer: (i) 1/26; (ii) 3/13; (iii) 3/26.

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q183 marks
A box contains 90 discs numbered 1 to 90. If one disc is drawn at random, find the probability that it bears (i) a two-digit number, (ii) a perfect square, (iii) a number divisible by 5.
Step 1 Total outcomes = 90.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Total outcomes = 90.
  2. (i) Two-digit numbers from 10 to 90 → 81 outcomes. P = 81/90 = 9/10.
  3. (ii) Perfect squares ≤ 90: 1, 4, 9, 16, 25, 36, 49, 64, 81 → 9 outcomes. P = 9/90 = 1/10.
  4. (iii) Multiples of 5 from 5 to 90: 5, 10, …, 90. Count = 90/5 = 18. P = 18/90 = 1/5.

Answer: (i) 9/10; (ii) 1/10; (iii) 1/5.

NCERT Ex 14.1 Q163 marks
12 defective pens are accidentally mixed with 132 good ones. One pen is taken out at random. Find the probability that the pen taken out is a good one.
Step 1 Total pens = 12 + 132 = 144.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Total pens = 12 + 132 = 144.
  2. Favourable (good) outcomes = 132.
  3. P(good) = 132/144 = 11/12.

Answer: P(good pen) = 11/12.

NCERT Example 13(i)1 mark
Two dice (one blue and one grey) are thrown simultaneously. The probability that the sum of the two numbers appearing on top is 8 equals
Step 1 Total outcomes = 6 × 6 = 36.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Total outcomes = 6 × 6 = 36.
  2. Favourable pairs (sum 8): (2,6), (3,5), (4,4), (5,3), (6,2) → 5 outcomes.
  3. P(sum = 8) = 5/36.

Answer: 5/36 — option (a).

Step 1 Sample space: {HH, HT, TH, TT} — 4 outcomes.

Step-by-step solution

CBSE board pattern · Assertion-Reason1 mark
Assertion (A): If the probability of an event is 2/3, then the probability of its complement is 1/3. Reason (R): For any event E, P(E) + P(not E) = 1.
Step 1 By R: P(not E) = 1 − 2/3 = 1/3. So A is true.

Step-by-step solution

  1. By R: P(not E) = 1 − 2/3 = 1/3. So A is true.
  2. R is the standard complementary-events identity — true.
  3. R is exactly the reason A holds.

Answer: Option (A).
