In-text Q1 · Section 9.22 marks
Define the principal focus of a concave mirror.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] A concave mirror is a converging mirror.
  2. [0.5 mark] Rays parallel to the principal axis fall on the mirror.
  3. [0.5 mark] After reflection, all these rays meet at a single point on the principal axis — this point is the principal focus (F).
  4. [0.5 mark] The distance from the pole to this point is the focal length (f).

Answer: Principal focus = point on principal axis where parallel rays converge after reflection from a concave mirror.

In-text Q1 · after Example 9.22 marks
Find the focal length of a convex mirror whose radius of curvature is 32 cm.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] For a spherical mirror, f = R / 2.
  2. [0.5 mark] Given R = 32 cm.
  3. [1 mark] f = 32/2 = 16 cm. By sign convention, for a convex mirror f is positive: f = +16 cm.

Answer: Focal length = +16 cm.

  4. [0.5 mark] The principal focus lies midway between the pole and the centre of curvature.

In-text Q3 · Section 9.22 marks
Name a mirror that can give an erect and enlarged image of an object.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] Plane mirror: erect, same size (not enlarged).
  2. [0.5 mark] Convex mirror: erect but always diminished.
  3. [0.5 mark] Concave mirror: when object is BETWEEN P and F → image is virtual, erect and enlarged (behind the mirror).
  4. [0.5 mark] Hence concave mirror is the answer (used as a shaving mirror).

Answer: Concave mirror (object placed between P and F).

In-text Q4 · Section 9.23 marks
Why do we prefer a convex mirror as a rear-view mirror in vehicles?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] A convex mirror always forms a virtual, erect and diminished image — so the driver instantly recognises the orientation of vehicles behind.
  2. [0.5 mark] Image is formed between pole and focus → smaller than object → entire road area is visible.
  3. [1 mark] Convex curvature gives a WIDER field of view than a plane mirror of the same size.
  4. [0.5 mark] Therefore convex mirrors are used as side/rear-view (wing) mirrors for safe driving.

Answer: Always gives an erect, diminished image and provides a much wider field of view than a plane mirror.

In-text Q2 · Section 9.33 marks
Light enters from air to glass having refractive index 1.50. What is the speed of light in the glass? The speed of light in vacuum is 3 × 108 m s−1.
Step 1 [1 mark] Refractive index n = c / v, where c = speed of light in vacuum and v = speed in medium.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Refractive index n = c / v, where c = speed of light in vacuum and v = speed in medium.
  2. [0.5 mark] Given n_glass = 1.50, c = 3 × 108 m s−1.
  3. [0.5 mark] Rearranging: v = c / n = (3 × 108) / 1.50.
  4. [1 mark] v = 2 × 108 m s−1. Light travels slower in glass than in vacuum.

Answer: v = 2 × 108 m s−1.

In-text Q5 · Section 9.32 marks
The refractive index of diamond is 2.42. What is the meaning of this statement?

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] Refractive index n = (speed of light in vacuum) / (speed of light in the medium).
  2. [0.5 mark] n_diamond = 2.42 means c / v_diamond = 2.42.
  3. [0.5 mark] So v_diamond = c / 2.42 = (3 × 108) / 2.42 ≈ 1.24 × 108 m s−1.
  4. [0.5 mark] Light travels about 2.42 times slower in diamond than in vacuum — diamond is optically very dense.

Answer: Light travels 2.42 times slower in diamond than in vacuum/air.

In-text Q1 · Section 9.3.82 marks
Define 1 dioptre of power of a lens.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] Power of a lens is defined as the reciprocal of its focal length: P = 1/f (f in metres).
  2. [0.5 mark] SI unit of power is the dioptre (D), where 1 D = 1 m−1.
  3. [1 mark] Therefore, 1 dioptre is the power of a lens whose focal length is 1 metre.

Answer: 1 D = power of a lens of focal length 1 m. (1 D = 1 m−1).

In-text Q2 · Section 9.3.85 marks
A convex lens forms a real and inverted image of a needle at a distance of 50 cm from it. Where is the needle placed in front of the convex lens if the image is equal to the size of the object? Also, find the power of the lens.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] For a convex lens, when image is real, inverted and SAME size as object, the object is at 2F and the image is also at 2F on the other side.
  2. [1 mark] Given v = +50 cm and image same size ⇒ u = −50 cm; both at 2F.
  3. [1 mark] Therefore 2f = 50 cm ⇒ f = +25 cm = +0.25 m.
  4. [1 mark] Power P = 1/f (in m) = 1/0.25 = +4.0 D (positive ⇒ convex lens).
  5. [1 mark] Hence needle is placed 50 cm in front of the lens, and the lens has power +4 D.

Answer: u = −50 cm; f = +25 cm; P = +4 D.

In-text Q3 · Section 9.3.82 marks
Find the power of a concave lens of focal length 2 m.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] For a concave lens, focal length is negative: f = −2 m.
  2. [1 mark] Power P = 1/f (in metres) = 1/(−2) = −0.5 D.
  3. [0.5 mark] The negative sign of power confirms it is a diverging (concave) lens.

Answer: P = −0.5 D.

Example 9.15 marks
A convex mirror used for rear-view on an automobile has a radius of curvature of 3.00 m. If a bus is located at 5.00 m from this mirror, find the position, nature and size of the image.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Given: R = +3.00 m (convex), u = −5.00 m. Focal length f = R/2 = +1.50 m.
  2. [1 mark] Mirror formula: 1/v + 1/u = 1/f ⇒ 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/1.50 − 1/(−5.00) = 1/1.50 + 1/5.00.
  3. [1 mark] 1/v = (5.00 + 1.50)/7.50 = 6.50/7.50 ⇒ v = +7.50/6.50 = +1.15 m (image behind mirror).
  4. [1 mark] Magnification m = −v/u = −(1.15)/(−5.00) = +0.23 → image is virtual, erect and diminished.
  5. [1 mark] Therefore: image is formed 1.15 m behind the mirror, virtual, erect, and 0.23 times the size of the bus.

Answer: v = +1.15 m (behind mirror); m = +0.23; image virtual, erect, diminished.

Example 9.25 marks
An object, 4.0 cm in size, is placed at 25.0 cm in front of a concave mirror of focal length 15.0 cm. At what distance from the mirror should a screen be placed in order to obtain a sharp image? Find the nature and the size of the image.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Given: h = +4.0 cm, u = −25.0 cm, f = −15.0 cm (concave).
  2. [1 mark] 1/v + 1/u = 1/f ⇒ 1/v = 1/f − 1/u = 1/(−15.0) − 1/(−25.0) = −1/15.0 + 1/25.0.
  3. [1 mark] 1/v = (−5 + 3)/75 = −2/75 ⇒ v = −37.5 cm. Screen at 37.5 cm in FRONT of the mirror (real image).
  4. [1 mark] Magnification m = −v/u = −(−37.5)/(−25.0) = −1.5 → image inverted, enlarged 1.5×.
  5. [1 mark] Image height h' = m × h = −1.5 × 4.0 = −6.0 cm (inverted). Image: real, inverted, 6.0 cm tall.

Answer: v = −37.5 cm (screen 37.5 cm in front); h' = −6.0 cm; real, inverted, enlarged.

Example 9.35 marks
A concave lens has focal length of 15 cm. At what distance should the object from the lens be placed so that it forms an image at 10 cm from the lens? Also, find the magnification produced by the lens.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Concave lens always forms a virtual, erect image on the same side as the object. So v = −10 cm, f = −15 cm.
  2. [1 mark] Lens formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f ⇒ 1/u = 1/v − 1/f = 1/(−10) − 1/(−15) = −1/10 + 1/15.
  3. [1 mark] 1/u = (−3 + 2)/30 = −1/30 ⇒ u = −30 cm. Object at 30 cm in front of the lens.
  4. [1 mark] Magnification m = v/u = (−10)/(−30) = +1/3 ≈ +0.33.
  5. [1 mark] Image is virtual, erect, one-third the size of the object.

Answer: u = −30 cm; m = +0.33; image virtual, erect, diminished.

Example 9.45 marks
A 2.0 cm tall object is placed perpendicular to the principal axis of a convex lens of focal length 10 cm. The distance of the object from the lens is 15 cm. Find the nature, position and size of the image. Also find its magnification.
Step 1 [1 mark] Given: h = +2.0 cm, f = +10 cm (convex), u = −15 cm.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Given: h = +2.0 cm, f = +10 cm (convex), u = −15 cm.
  2. [1 mark] 1/v − 1/u = 1/f ⇒ 1/v = 1/u + 1/f = 1/(−15) + 1/10.
  3. [1 mark] 1/v = (−2 + 3)/30 = 1/30 ⇒ v = +30 cm (image on other side of lens, real).
  4. [1 mark] Magnification m = v/u = 30/(−15) = −2. Image height h' = m × h = −2 × 2.0 = −4.0 cm.
  5. [1 mark] Image: real, inverted, 4.0 cm tall, formed 30 cm on the opposite side of the lens, two times enlarged.

Answer: v = +30 cm; m = −2; h' = −4.0 cm; real, inverted, enlarged.

Exercise Q11 mark
Which one of the following materials cannot be used to make a lens?

Step-by-step solution

  1. A lens must be made of a TRANSPARENT material so that light can refract through it.
  2. Water, glass and plastic are transparent. Clay is opaque — it does not transmit light.
  3. Therefore clay cannot be used to make a lens. Answer: (D)

Answer: (d) Clay (opaque material).

Exercise Q21 mark
The image formed by a concave mirror is observed to be virtual, erect and larger than the object. Where should be the position of the object?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Concave mirror: only ONE position gives a virtual, erect AND enlarged image.
  2. When the object is BETWEEN the pole P and the focus F (object distance < f).
  3. Image is formed behind the mirror — virtual, erect, magnified (used in shaving mirrors).
  4. All other positions give real, inverted images. Answer: (D)

Answer: (d) Between the pole and the principal focus.

Exercise Q31 mark
Where should an object be placed in front of a convex lens to get a real image of the size of the object?

Step-by-step solution

  1. For a convex lens: object at 2F₁ → image at 2F₂, same size, real, inverted.
  2. At F → image at infinity. At infinity → image at F. Between F and O → virtual, enlarged.
  3. Hence the object should be placed at 2F (twice the focal length) for same-size real image. Answer: (B)

Answer: (b) At twice the focal length (2F).

Exercise Q41 mark
A spherical mirror and a thin spherical lens have each a focal length of −15 cm. The mirror and the lens are likely to be

Step-by-step solution

  1. By New Cartesian Sign Convention for mirrors: f is NEGATIVE for a concave mirror, positive for convex.
  2. For lenses: f is NEGATIVE for a concave (diverging) lens, positive for a convex lens.
  3. f = −15 cm for the mirror ⇒ concave mirror.
  4. f = −15 cm for the lens ⇒ concave lens.
  5. Hence both are concave. Answer: (A)

Answer: (a) Both concave.

Exercise Q51 mark
No matter how far you stand from a mirror, your image appears erect. The mirror is likely to be

Step-by-step solution

  1. Plane mirror: always gives an erect, virtual image of the same size.
  2. Convex mirror: always gives an erect, virtual, diminished image — irrespective of object distance.
  3. Concave mirror: gives an erect image ONLY when object is between P and F — at larger distances the image becomes inverted.
  4. Hence the mirror is either plane or convex. Answer: (D)

Answer: (d) Either plane or convex.

Exercise Q61 mark
Which of the following lenses would you prefer to use while reading small letters found in a dictionary?

Step-by-step solution

  1. A magnifying glass must be a CONVEX lens — concave lenses always give diminished images.
  2. The shorter the focal length, the greater the magnification (power P = 1/f).
  3. Between f = 50 cm and f = 5 cm convex lenses, the 5 cm one magnifies more.
  4. Hence a convex lens of focal length 5 cm is best for reading small letters. Answer: (C)

Answer: (c) Convex lens of focal length 5 cm.

Exercise Q105 marks
An object 5 cm in length is held 25 cm away from a converging lens of focal length 10 cm. Draw the ray diagram and find the position, size and the nature of the image formed.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Given: h = +5 cm, u = −25 cm, f = +10 cm (convex).
  2. [1 mark] Lens formula: 1/v − 1/u = 1/f ⇒ 1/v = 1/f + 1/u = 1/10 + 1/(−25) = 1/10 − 1/25.
  3. [1 mark] 1/v = (5 − 2)/50 = 3/50 ⇒ v = 50/3 ≈ +16.67 cm. Image is on the opposite side, real.
  4. [1 mark] Magnification m = v/u = (50/3)/(−25) = −2/3. Image height h' = m × h = (−2/3) × 5 = −3.33 cm.
  5. [1 mark] Image: real, inverted, diminished, 3.33 cm tall, formed 16.67 cm from the lens on the opposite side. Ray diagram: the object (u = 25 cm > 2f = 20 cm) lies beyond 2F₁, so the image is formed between F₂ and 2F₂ — diminished, real and inverted, matching the calculation.

Answer: v ≈ +16.67 cm; h' ≈ −3.33 cm; real, inverted, diminished.
