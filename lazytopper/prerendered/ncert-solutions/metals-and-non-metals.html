In-text Q1 · Section 3.12 marks
Give an example of a metal which (i) is a liquid at room temperature. (ii) can be easily cut with a knife. (iii) is the best conductor of heat. (iv) is a poor conductor of heat.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [0.5 mark] (i) Mercury is the only metal that is liquid at room temperature.
  2. [0.5 mark] (ii) Alkali metals like sodium and potassium are very soft and can be cut with a knife.
  3. [0.5 mark] (iii) Silver is the best conductor of heat (and electricity); copper comes second.
  4. [0.5 mark] (iv) Lead and mercury are poor conductors of heat among metals.

Answer: (i) Hg (ii) Na/K (iii) Ag (iv) Pb/Hg

Exercise Q11 mark
Which of the following pairs will give displacement reactions? (a) NaCl solution and copper metal (b) MgCl₂ solution and aluminium metal (c) FeSO₄ solution and silver metal (d) AgNO₃ solution and copper metal.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Displacement requires the metal to be ABOVE the metal in the salt in reactivity series.
  2. (a) Cu cannot displace Na (Na is far more reactive). No reaction.
  3. (b) Al cannot displace Mg (Mg is more reactive than Al). No reaction.
  4. (c) Ag cannot displace Fe (Fe is more reactive). No reaction.
  5. (d) Cu CAN displace Ag (Cu above Ag in reactivity). Cu + 2AgNO₃ → Cu(NO₃)₂ + 2Ag. ✓ Answer: (D)

Answer: AgNO₃ + Cu (option d)

Exercise Q31 mark
An element reacts with oxygen to give a compound with a high melting point. This compound is also soluble in water. The element is likely to be

Step-by-step solution

  1. High melting point + soluble in water → ionic oxide of a metal (basic oxide soluble in water → forms alkali).
  2. Calcium: CaO has high m.p. (~2570°C) and dissolves in water to form Ca(OH)₂ — soluble. ✓
  3. Carbon (CO₂) is a gas; Silicon (SiO₂) has high m.p. but is INSOLUBLE in water; Iron (Fe₂O₃) is insoluble. Answer: (A)

Answer: Calcium — option (a)

Exercise Q62 marks
What are amphoteric oxides? Give two examples of amphoteric oxides.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Amphoteric oxide = metal oxide that reacts with both acids and bases.
  2. [0.5 mark] Example 1: Al₂O₃ + 6HCl → 2AlCl₃ + 3H₂O (acts as base); Al₂O₃ + 2NaOH → 2NaAlO₂ + H₂O (acts as acid).
  3. [0.5 mark] Example 2: ZnO + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂O; ZnO + 2NaOH → Na₂ZnO₂ + H₂O.

Answer: Oxides reacting with both acids and bases. Examples: Al₂O₃ and ZnO.

Exercise Q72 marks
Name two metals which will displace hydrogen from dilute acids, and two metals which will not.

Step-by-step solution

  1. [1 mark] Metals above H in reactivity series can displace H from dilute acids.
  2. [0.5 mark] Above H: K, Na, Ca, Mg, Al, Zn, Fe, Pb. Examples: Zn + 2HCl → ZnCl₂ + H₂. So Zn, Fe will displace.
  3. [0.5 mark] Below H: Cu, Hg, Ag, Au. These cannot displace H. So Cu, Ag will not react with dilute HCl/H₂SO₄.

Answer: Displace H: Zn and Fe. Do not displace: Cu and Ag.
