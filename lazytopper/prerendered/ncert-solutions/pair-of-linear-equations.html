Exercise 3.1

NCERT Ex 3.1 Q2(i)1 mark
On comparing the ratios a₁/a₂, b₁/b₂ and c₁/c₂, the pair of linear equations 5x − 4y + 8 = 0 and 7x + 6y − 9 = 0 represents lines that are:
Step 1 For the two equations a₁ = 5, b₁ = −4, c₁ = 8 and a₂ = 7, b₂ = 6, c₂ = −9.

Step-by-step solution

  1. For the two equations a₁ = 5, b₁ = −4, c₁ = 8 and a₂ = 7, b₂ = 6, c₂ = −9.
  2. Compute a₁/a₂ = 5/7 and b₁/b₂ = −4/6 = −2/3.
  3. Since 5/7 ≠ −2/3, i.e., a₁/a₂ ≠ b₁/b₂, the lines are intersecting (unique solution).
  4. Hence the pair represents intersecting lines.

Answer: Intersecting at a point — option (a).

NCERT Ex 3.1 Q2(ii)1 mark
The pair of linear equations 9x + 3y + 12 = 0 and 18x + 6y + 24 = 0 is:
Step 1 a₁/a₂ = 9/18 = 1/2.

Step-by-step solution

  1. a₁/a₂ = 9/18 = 1/2.
  2. b₁/b₂ = 3/6 = 1/2.
  3. c₁/c₂ = 12/24 = 1/2.
  4. Since a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ = c₁/c₂, the lines are coincident → infinitely many solutions (consistent, dependent).

Answer: Coincident lines with infinitely many solutions — option (c).

NCERT Ex 3.1 Q2(iii)1 mark
Graphically, the pair of equations 6x − 3y + 10 = 0 and 2x − y + 9 = 0 represents two lines which are:
Step 1 a₁/a₂ = 6/2 = 3.

Step-by-step solution

  1. a₁/a₂ = 6/2 = 3.
  2. b₁/b₂ = −3/−1 = 3.
  3. c₁/c₂ = 10/9.
  4. a₁/a₂ = b₁/b₂ ≠ c₁/c₂ → parallel lines.
  5. Hence the pair represents parallel (non-intersecting) lines.

Answer: Parallel — option (d).

NCERT Ex 3.1 Q1(i)3 marks
10 students of Class X took part in a Mathematics quiz. If the number of girls is 4 more than the number of boys, find the number of boys and girls who took part in the quiz.
Step 1 Let number of boys = x and number of girls = y.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let number of boys = x and number of girls = y.
  2. From the total: x + y = 10.
  3. From the relation: y − x = 4 (girls are 4 more than boys).
  4. Adding the two equations: 2y = 14 → y = 7; then x = 10 − 7 = 3.
  5. Hence 3 boys and 7 girls participated.

Answer: 3 boys and 7 girls.

NCERT Ex 3.1 Q1(ii)3 marks
5 pencils and 7 pens together cost ₹50, whereas 7 pencils and 5 pens together cost ₹46. Find the cost of one pencil and that of one pen.
Step 1 Let cost of a pencil = ₹x and cost of a pen = ₹y.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let cost of a pencil = ₹x and cost of a pen = ₹y.
  2. Equations: 5x + 7y = 50 …(1) and 7x + 5y = 46 …(2).
  3. Add (1) and (2): 12x + 12y = 96 → x + y = 8.
  4. Subtract (1) from (2): 2x − 2y = −4 → y − x = 2.
  5. Solve: y = 5, x = 3. Hence each pencil costs ₹3 and each pen ₹5.

Answer: Pencil = ₹3, Pen = ₹5.

NCERT Ex 3.1 Q53 marks
Half the perimeter of a rectangular garden, whose length is 4 m more than its width, is 36 m. Find the dimensions of the garden.
Step 1 Let length = x m and width = y m.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let length = x m and width = y m.
  2. Half-perimeter = (length + width) = x + y = 36 …(1).
  3. Length is 4 m more than width: x = y + 4, i.e., x − y = 4 …(2).
  4. Add (1) and (2): 2x = 40 → x = 20 m.
  5. From (1): y = 36 − 20 = 16 m. Dimensions: 20 m × 16 m.

Answer: Length = 20 m, Width = 16 m.

Exercise 3.2

NCERT Ex 3.2 Q1(i)2 marks
Solve by the substitution method: x + y = 14 and x − y = 4.
Step 1 From equation 1: x = 14 − y.

Step-by-step solution

  1. From equation 1: x = 14 − y.
  2. Substitute into x − y = 4: (14 − y) − y = 4 → 14 − 2y = 4.
  3. Solve: 2y = 10 → y = 5.
  4. Back-substitute: x = 14 − 5 = 9.

Answer: x = 9, y = 5.

NCERT Ex 3.2 Q1(iii)2 marks
Solve by the substitution method and state the nature of the solution: 3x − y = 3 and 9x − 3y = 9.
Step 1 From 3x − y = 3: y = 3x − 3.

Step-by-step solution

  1. From 3x − y = 3: y = 3x − 3.
  2. Substitute into 9x − 3y = 9: 9x − 3(3x − 3) = 9.
  3. Simplify: 9x − 9x + 9 = 9 → 9 = 9, a true statement with no variable.
  4. Hence the pair has infinitely many solutions (the two equations represent the same line).

Answer: Infinitely many solutions; the two equations are equivalent.

NCERT Ex 3.2 Q3(i)3 marks
The difference between two numbers is 26 and one number is three times the other. Find them.
Step 1 Let the smaller number = x and the larger number = y.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the smaller number = x and the larger number = y.
  2. Equations: y − x = 26 …(1) and y = 3x …(2).
  3. Substitute (2) into (1): 3x − x = 26 → 2x = 26 → x = 13.
  4. Then y = 3(13) = 39.
  5. Hence the numbers are 13 and 39.

Answer: The numbers are 13 and 39.

NCERT Ex 3.2 Q3(ii)3 marks
The larger of two supplementary angles exceeds the smaller by 18°. Find them.
Step 1 Let smaller angle = x° and larger = y°.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let smaller angle = x° and larger = y°.
  2. Supplementary: x + y = 180 …(1).
  3. Larger exceeds smaller by 18°: y − x = 18 …(2).
  4. Add (1) and (2): 2y = 198 → y = 99°.
  5. Substitute back: x = 180 − 99 = 81°. So the angles are 81° and 99°.

Answer: 81° and 99°.

NCERT Ex 3.2 Q3(v)3 marks
A fraction becomes 9/11 if 2 is added to both the numerator and the denominator. If 3 is added to both the numerator and the denominator it becomes 5/6. Find the fraction.
Step 1 Let the fraction = x/y.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the fraction = x/y.
  2. First condition: (x + 2)/(y + 2) = 9/11 → 11(x + 2) = 9(y + 2) → 11x − 9y = −4 …(1).
  3. Second condition: (x + 3)/(y + 3) = 5/6 → 6(x + 3) = 5(y + 3) → 6x − 5y = −3 …(2).
  4. Multiply (1) by 5 and (2) by 9: 55x − 45y = −20 and 54x − 45y = −27. Subtract: x = 7.
  5. Substitute in (1): 11(7) − 9y = −4 → 9y = 81 → y = 9. Fraction = 7/9.

Answer: The fraction is 7/9.

NCERT Ex 3.2 Q3(vi)3 marks
Five years hence, the age of Jacob will be three times that of his son. Five years ago, Jacob's age was seven times that of his son. What are their present ages?
Step 1 Let Jacob's present age = x years and son's present age = y years.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let Jacob's present age = x years and son's present age = y years.
  2. Five years hence: x + 5 = 3(y + 5) → x − 3y = 10 …(1).
  3. Five years ago: x − 5 = 7(y − 5) → x − 7y = −30 …(2).
  4. Subtract (2) from (1): 4y = 40 → y = 10.
  5. Substitute in (1): x = 10 + 30 = 40. Jacob is 40 and his son is 10.

Answer: Jacob = 40 years, Son = 10 years.

Exercise 3.3

NCERT Ex 3.3 Q1(i)2 marks
Solve by the elimination method: x + y = 5 and 2x − 3y = 4.
Step 1 Eq1: x + y = 5; Eq2: 2x − 3y = 4.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Eq1: x + y = 5; Eq2: 2x − 3y = 4.
  2. Multiply Eq1 by 3: 3x + 3y = 15.
  3. Add to Eq2: (3x + 2x) + (3y − 3y) = 15 + 4 → 5x = 19 → x = 19/5.
  4. Back-substitute in Eq1: y = 5 − 19/5 = 6/5.

Answer: x = 19/5, y = 6/5.

NCERT Ex 3.3 Q2(ii)5 marks
Form the pair of linear equations and solve by the elimination method: Five years ago, Nuri was thrice as old as Sonu. Ten years later, Nuri will be twice as old as Sonu. How old are Nuri and Sonu?
Step 1 Let Nuri's present age = x and Sonu's present age = y.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let Nuri's present age = x and Sonu's present age = y.
  2. Five years ago: x − 5 = 3(y − 5) → x − 3y = −10 …(1).
  3. Ten years later: x + 10 = 2(y + 10) → x − 2y = 10 …(2).
  4. Subtract (1) from (2): (x − 2y) − (x − 3y) = 10 − (−10) → y = 20.
  5. Substitute in (2): x = 10 + 2(20) = 50.
  6. Hence Nuri is 50 years old and Sonu is 20 years old. (Check: 5 years ago, 45 = 3 × 15 ✓; 10 years later, 60 = 2 × 30 ✓.)

Answer: Nuri = 50 years, Sonu = 20 years.

NCERT Example 85 marks
The ratio of incomes of two persons is 9 : 7 and the ratio of their expenditures is 4 : 3. If each of them manages to save ₹2000 per month, find their monthly incomes.
Step 1 Let monthly incomes = ₹9x and ₹7x (ratio 9 : 7).

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let monthly incomes = ₹9x and ₹7x (ratio 9 : 7).
  2. Let monthly expenditures = ₹4y and ₹3y (ratio 4 : 3).
  3. Savings = income − expenditure = ₹2000 for each: 9x − 4y = 2000 …(1); 7x − 3y = 2000 …(2).
  4. Multiply (1) by 3 and (2) by 4 to align y-coefficients: 27x − 12y = 6000; 28x − 12y = 8000.
  5. Subtract: x = 2000. Incomes = 9(2000) = ₹18,000 and 7(2000) = ₹14,000. (Verify: y = 4000 → expenditures ₹16,000 and ₹12,000 → savings ₹2000 each.)

Answer: Monthly incomes: ₹18,000 and ₹14,000.

NCERT Example 105 marks
The sum of a two-digit number and the number obtained by reversing the digits is 66. If the digits of the number differ by 2, find the number. How many such numbers are there?
Step 1 Let the tens digit = x and units digit = y. Number = 10x + y; reversed = 10y + x.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the tens digit = x and units digit = y. Number = 10x + y; reversed = 10y + x.
  2. Sum condition: (10x + y) + (10y + x) = 66 → 11(x + y) = 66 → x + y = 6 …(1).
  3. Digit difference condition: x − y = 2 …(2a) OR y − x = 2 …(2b).
  4. Case 1: (1) and (2a) → 2x = 8 → x = 4, y = 2 → number = 42.
  5. Case 2: (1) and (2b) → 2y = 8 → y = 4, x = 2 → number = 24. Hence two numbers exist: 42 and 24.

Answer: Two numbers — 42 and 24 (both satisfy the conditions).

CBSE board pattern · Case-based4 marks
Akhila went to a village fair with ₹20. She wanted to enjoy rides on the Giant Wheel and play Hoopla. The number of times she played Hoopla is half the number of rides she had on the Giant Wheel. Each Giant-Wheel ride costs ₹3 and a game of Hoopla costs ₹4, and she spent the entire ₹20. Let x = number of Giant-Wheel rides and y = number of times she played Hoopla. (i) Form a pair of linear equations representing the situation. (ii) Solve graphically (describe lines and intersection). (iii) How many rides and games did she have? (iv) Verify algebraically.
Step 1 Translate to equations: y = x/2 ⇒ x − 2y = 0 …(1); total spend 3x + 4y = 20 …(2).

Step-by-step solution

  1. Translate to equations: y = x/2 ⇒ x − 2y = 0 …(1); total spend 3x + 4y = 20 …(2).
  2. Graphical: Eq (1) passes through (0, 0), (2, 1), (4, 2); Eq (2) passes through (0, 5), (4, 2). Lines meet at (4, 2).
  3. Algebraic check: from (1), x = 2y. Substitute in (2): 3(2y) + 4y = 20 → 10y = 20 → y = 2; x = 4.
  4. Conclusion: 4 rides on the Giant Wheel and 2 games of Hoopla.
  5. Verification: 4 − 2(2) = 0 ✓; 3(4) + 4(2) = 20 ✓.

Answer: Akhila had 4 Giant-Wheel rides and 2 games of Hoopla.

CBSE board pattern · Case-based4 marks
The taxi fare in a city consists of a fixed charge together with a charge proportional to the distance covered. For a distance of 10 km, the charge paid is ₹105 and for a journey of 15 km, the charge paid is ₹155. Let the fixed charge be ₹x and the per-km charge be ₹y. (i) Form the pair of linear equations. (ii) Find the fixed charge and the per-km rate. (iii) How much does a person have to pay for travelling 25 km?
Step 1 Let fixed charge = ₹x and per-km charge = ₹y.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let fixed charge = ₹x and per-km charge = ₹y.
  2. 10 km journey: x + 10y = 105 …(1). 15 km journey: x + 15y = 155 …(2).
  3. Subtract (1) from (2): 5y = 50 → y = 10. Then from (1): x = 105 − 100 = 5.
  4. So fixed charge = ₹5 and per-km charge = ₹10.
  5. Fare for 25 km = x + 25y = 5 + 250 = ₹255.

Answer: Fixed charge ₹5, per-km charge ₹10; fare for 25 km = ₹255.

CBSE board pattern · Case-based4 marks
A lending library has a fixed charge for the first three days and an additional charge for each day thereafter. Saritha paid ₹27 for a book kept for 7 days, while Susy paid ₹21 for a book kept for 5 days. Let the fixed charge be ₹x and the additional per-day charge (after day 3) be ₹y. (i) Form the pair of equations. (ii) Find x and y. (iii) How much would a person pay for a book kept for 10 days?
Step 1 Let fixed charge for the first 3 days = ₹x; additional per-day charge = ₹y.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let fixed charge for the first 3 days = ₹x; additional per-day charge = ₹y.
  2. Saritha (7 days): x + (7 − 3)y = 27 → x + 4y = 27 …(1).
  3. Susy (5 days): x + (5 − 3)y = 21 → x + 2y = 21 …(2).
  4. Subtract (2) from (1): 2y = 6 → y = 3. From (2): x = 21 − 6 = 15.
  5. For 10 days → 7 extra days: charge = 15 + 7(3) = ₹36.

Answer: Fixed ₹15, per-extra-day ₹3; charge for 10 days = ₹36.
