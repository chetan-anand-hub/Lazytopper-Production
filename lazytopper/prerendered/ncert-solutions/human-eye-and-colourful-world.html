In-text questions

In-text Q1 · Section 10.22 marks
What is meant by power of accommodation of the eye?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Accommodation = ability of the eye lens to change its focal length.
  2. Ciliary muscles modify the curvature of the lens.
  3. Distant objects → muscles relaxed → lens thin → focal length large.
  4. Near objects → muscles contract → lens thick → focal length small.
  5. This keeps the image on the retina for objects at varying distances.

Answer: Power of accommodation = ability of the eye lens to adjust its focal length using ciliary muscles to focus images of objects at different distances on the retina.

In-text Q2 · Section 10.22 marks
A person with a myopic eye cannot see objects beyond 1.2 m distinctly. What should be the type of the corrective lens used to restore proper vision?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Far point of the myopic eye = 1.2 m (less than infinity).
  2. Myopia is corrected by a CONCAVE (diverging) lens.
  3. The lens forms a virtual image of a distant object at the eye's far point.
  4. Required focal length f = -1.2 m (negative sign for concave lens).

Answer: A concave (diverging) lens of focal length -1.2 m (power = -0.83 D) is required.

In-text Q3 · Section 10.22 marks
What is the far point and near point of the human eye with normal vision?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Near point = least distance of distinct vision.
  2. For a normal young adult, near point = 25 cm.
  3. Far point = farthest distance at which an eye can see clearly.
  4. For a normal eye, far point = infinity.

Answer: Near point = 25 cm; Far point = infinity (for normal human eye).

In-text Q4 · Section 10.23 marks
A student has difficulty reading the blackboard while sitting in the last row. What could be the defect the child is suffering from? How can it be corrected?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Symptoms: cannot see distant blackboard but can see nearby objects → far point < infinity.
  2. This refractive defect is called MYOPIA (near-sightedness).
  3. Cause: image of distant object forms IN FRONT of the retina.
  4. Correction: use spectacles with a CONCAVE (diverging) lens of suitable power.
  5. The lens forms a virtual image at the eye's far point, which the eye can then focus on the retina.

Answer: Defect = Myopia (near-sightedness). Corrected by a concave lens of suitable focal length.

Exercise Q53 marks
A person needs a lens of power -5.5 dioptres for correcting his distant vision. For correcting his near vision he needs a lens of power +1.5 dioptre. What is the focal length of the lens required for correcting (i) distant vision, and (ii) near vision?
Step 1 Use the formula: Power P = 1/f (focal length in metres).

Step-by-step solution

  1. Use the formula: Power P = 1/f (focal length in metres).
  2. (i) Distant vision: P₁ = -5.5 D → f₁ = 1/(-5.5) m = -0.1818 m ≈ -18.2 cm (concave lens corrects myopia).
  3. (ii) Near vision: P₂ = +1.5 D → f₂ = 1/1.5 m = 0.667 m ≈ +66.7 cm (convex lens corrects hypermetropia).
  4. Negative focal length → concave (diverging); positive → convex (converging).

Answer: (i) f₁ = -18.18 cm (concave). (ii) f₂ = +66.67 cm (convex).

Exercise Q63 marks
The far point of a myopic person is 80 cm in front of the eye. What is the nature and power of the lens required to correct the problem?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Far point of the myopic eye = 80 cm; correction requires a concave lens.
  2. The lens must form an image of an object at infinity (u = -∞) at the far point (v = -80 cm).
  3. Apply lens formula: 1/v - 1/u = 1/f → 1/(-80) - 0 = 1/f → f = -80 cm = -0.8 m.
  4. Power P = 1/f (m) = 1/(-0.8) = -1.25 D.
  5. Hence, a concave lens of power -1.25 D corrects the defect.

Answer: Nature: concave (diverging) lens; Power = -1.25 D (focal length = -0.8 m or -80 cm).

Exercise Q82 marks
Why is a normal eye not able to see clearly the objects placed closer than 25 cm?

Step-by-step solution

  1. The eye lens's focal length is adjusted by ciliary muscles.
  2. Ciliary muscles can contract only up to a maximum limit → focal length has a minimum value.
  3. For objects nearer than 25 cm, the required focal length is below this minimum → image cannot form on retina.
  4. Hence the object appears blurred and the eye feels strained.

Answer: Because the eye lens's focal length cannot be reduced below the limit set by maximum ciliary muscle contraction, so objects closer than 25 cm cannot be focussed on the retina.

Exercise Q103 marks
Why do stars twinkle?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Starlight enters the atmosphere and undergoes refraction.
  2. The atmosphere has layers of varying refractive index (changing with temperature/density).
  3. As physical conditions are not stationary, the apparent position of the star fluctuates.
  4. Stars are point sources, so tiny variations cause noticeable changes in light reaching the eye.
  5. The amount of light entering the eye keeps fluctuating → star appears to twinkle.

Answer: Atmospheric refraction by layers of varying refractive index makes the apparent position and brightness of point-like distant stars fluctuate → twinkling.

Exercise Q113 marks
Explain why the planets do not twinkle.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Stars are far away → appear as point sources, so atmospheric refraction causes visible twinkling.
  2. Planets are much closer → appear as extended sources (not points).
  3. A planet can be considered as many point sources side by side.
  4. Twinkling from each point averages out over the whole disc → net variation in light = 0.
  5. Hence planets shine steadily without twinkling.

Answer: Planets are extended sources (collection of many point sources); twinkling of individual points averages out, so the net brightness is steady.

Exercise Q122 marks
Why does the sky appear dark instead of blue to an astronaut?

Step-by-step solution

  1. Blue sky on earth is caused by scattering of sunlight by air molecules and fine particles.
  2. Scattering is more effective at shorter (blue) wavelengths.
  3. An astronaut in space (or at very high altitude) is above/outside the dense atmosphere.
  4. No atmosphere → no scattering of sunlight → no blue light reaches the eye → sky appears dark.

Answer: Above the atmosphere there are no particles to scatter sunlight, so no blue light reaches the eye and the sky appears dark.
