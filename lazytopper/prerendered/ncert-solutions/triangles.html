Exercise 6.1

NCERT Ex 6.1 Q1(i)1 mark
Fill in the blank: All circles are ______.
Step 1 All circles have the same shape (a closed curve at constant distance from a centre).

Step-by-step solution

  1. All circles have the same shape (a closed curve at constant distance from a centre).
  2. Two circles need not have the same radius, so they are not always congruent.
  3. Same shape but possibly different size → similar.

Answer: similar — option (b).

NCERT Ex 6.1 Q1(iii)1 mark
Fill in the blank: All ______ triangles are similar.
Step 1 Every equilateral triangle has all three angles equal to 60°.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Every equilateral triangle has all three angles equal to 60°.
  2. So any two equilateral triangles are equiangular → corresponding angles equal.
  3. By AAA similarity, all equilateral triangles are similar (regardless of side length).
  4. Isosceles triangles can have different vertex angles, so they are not always similar.

Answer: equilateral — option (b).

NCERT Ex 6.1 Q1(iv)2 marks
State the two conditions under which two polygons of the same number of sides are similar.
Step 1 Condition (i): all pairs of corresponding angles must be equal.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Condition (i): all pairs of corresponding angles must be equal.
  2. Condition (ii): all pairs of corresponding sides must be in the same ratio.
  3. Both conditions are necessary — a square vs rectangle satisfies (i) only and fails to be similar.
  4. A square vs rhombus satisfies (ii) only and fails to be similar.

Answer: Corresponding angles equal AND corresponding sides in the same ratio.

Exercise 6.2

NCERT Ex 6.2 Q1(i)2 marks
In △ABC, DE ∥ BC. If AD = 1.5 cm, DB = 3 cm and AE = 1 cm, find EC.
Step 1 DE ∥ BC, so by BPT (Theorem 6.1): AD/DB = AE/EC.

Step-by-step solution

  1. DE ∥ BC, so by BPT (Theorem 6.1): AD/DB = AE/EC.
  2. AD = 1.5, DB = 3, AE = 1; let EC = x.
  3. 1.5/3 = 1/x ⇒ x = 3/1.5 = 2.
  4. Therefore EC = 2 cm.

Answer: EC = 2 cm.

NCERT Ex 6.2 Q2(i)3 marks
E and F are points on the sides PQ and PR respectively of △PQR. For the case PE = 3.9 cm, EQ = 3 cm, PF = 3.6 cm and FR = 2.4 cm, state whether EF ∥ QR.
Step 1 Compute PE/EQ = 3.9/3 = 1.3.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Compute PE/EQ = 3.9/3 = 1.3.
  2. Compute PF/FR = 3.6/2.4 = 1.5.
  3. Compare: 1.3 ≠ 1.5, so PE/EQ ≠ PF/FR.
  4. By converse of BPT, EF ∥ QR would require equal ratios.
  5. Therefore EF is NOT parallel to QR.

Answer: EF is not parallel to QR (ratios 1.3 and 1.5 are unequal).

NCERT Ex 6.2 Q7 (Theorem 6.1 application)5 marks
Using BPT, prove that a line drawn through the mid-point of one side of a triangle parallel to another side bisects the third side. (Mid-point Theorem)
Step 1 Given: △ABC; D is the mid-point of AB; DE ∥ BC; E lies on AC.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: △ABC; D is the mid-point of AB; DE ∥ BC; E lies on AC.
  2. To prove: AE = EC (E is the mid-point of AC).
  3. Proof: DE ∥ BC ⇒ by BPT, AD/DB = AE/EC.
  4. D is the mid-point of AB ⇒ AD = DB ⇒ AD/DB = 1.
  5. Therefore AE/EC = 1 ⇒ AE = EC.
  6. Conclusion: E is the mid-point of AC, so the line through D parallel to BC bisects AC.

Answer: Proved: a line through the mid-point of one side parallel to another side bisects the third side.

Exercise 6.3

NCERT Ex 6.3 Q153 marks
A vertical pole of length 6 m casts a shadow 4 m long on the ground, and at the same time a tower casts a shadow 28 m long. Find the height of the tower.
Step 1 Let the tower's height = h m. Both the pole and the tower are vertical, so each makes a 90° angle with the ground.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Let the tower's height = h m. Both the pole and the tower are vertical, so each makes a 90° angle with the ground.
  2. The sun's rays hit both objects at the same angle of elevation.
  3. By AA similarity, △(pole, shadow, ray) ~ △(tower, shadow, ray).
  4. Therefore (pole height)/(pole shadow) = (tower height)/(tower shadow): 6/4 = h/28.
  5. h = 6 × 28/4 = 168/4 = 42.
  6. Height of the tower = 42 m.

Answer: Height of the tower = 42 m.

NCERT Ex 6.3 Q35 marks
Diagonals AC and BD of a trapezium ABCD with AB ∥ DC intersect at the point O. Using a similarity criterion for two triangles, show that OA/OC = OB/OD.
Step 1 Given: AB ∥ DC; diagonals AC, BD meet at O.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: AB ∥ DC; diagonals AC, BD meet at O.
  2. To prove: OA/OC = OB/OD.
  3. Construction: none needed — work in △AOB and △COD.
  4. Proof: ∠AOB = ∠COD (vertically opposite angles).
  5. AB ∥ DC with transversal AC ⇒ ∠OAB = ∠OCD (alternate angles).
  6. By AA similarity criterion, △AOB ~ △COD.
  7. Corresponding sides are proportional ⇒ OA/OC = OB/OD.

Answer: Proved: OA/OC = OB/OD using AA similarity criterion.

NCERT Ex 6.3 Q135 marks
D is a point on the side BC of △ABC such that ∠ADC = ∠BAC. Show that CA² = CB · CD.
Step 1 Given: D on BC; ∠ADC = ∠BAC.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: D on BC; ∠ADC = ∠BAC.
  2. To prove: CA² = CB · CD.
  3. In △ADC and △BAC, ∠ADC = ∠BAC (given).
  4. ∠ACD = ∠BCA = ∠C (common angle at vertex C).
  5. By AA similarity criterion, △ADC ~ △BAC.
  6. Corresponding sides: CA/CB = CD/CA (matching A↔B, D↔A, C↔C).
  7. Cross-multiplying: CA · CA = CB · CD ⇒ CA² = CB · CD.

Answer: Proved: CA² = CB · CD via △ADC ~ △BAC (AA similarity).

NCERT Example 3 (Ch 6)5 marks
In △PQR, S and T lie on PQ and PR respectively such that PS/SQ = PT/TR and ∠PST = ∠PRQ. Prove that △PQR is isosceles.
Step 1 Given: PS/SQ = PT/TR and ∠PST = ∠PRQ.

Step-by-step solution

  1. Given: PS/SQ = PT/TR and ∠PST = ∠PRQ.
  2. To prove: PQ = PR (△PQR is isosceles).
  3. Step 1: PS/SQ = PT/TR ⇒ by converse of BPT, ST ∥ QR.
  4. Step 2: ST ∥ QR ⇒ ∠PST = ∠PQR (corresponding angles).
  5. Step 3: Given ∠PST = ∠PRQ; combined with Step 2 ⇒ ∠PQR = ∠PRQ.
  6. Step 4: Sides opposite equal angles are equal ⇒ PQ = PR.
  7. Hence △PQR is isosceles.

Answer: Proved: △PQR is isosceles with PQ = PR.

CBSE board pattern · Assertion-Reason1 mark
Assertion (A): All congruent figures are similar, but the converse is not true. Reason (R): Two figures are similar if they have the same shape; they are congruent if they have the same shape and the same size.
Step 1 A: True. Congruent figures share shape and size — same shape ⇒ similar; but two similar figures can differ in size, so similar ⇏ congruent.

Step-by-step solution

  1. A: True. Congruent figures share shape and size — same shape ⇒ similar; but two similar figures can differ in size, so similar ⇏ congruent.
  2. R: True. Similarity = same shape; congruence = same shape AND same size.
  3. R directly explains why congruence implies similarity but not vice versa.
  4. Hence option (A).

Answer: Option (A).

CBSE board pattern · Case-based4 marks
Case study: A girl of height 90 cm walks away from the base of a lamp-post at a speed of 1.2 m/s. The lamp is fixed at 3.6 m above the ground. Answer the following: (i) After 4 seconds, how far is the girl from the lamp-post (in metres)? (ii) Using similar triangles, write the relation between the girl's height, the lamp-post's height, the distance of the girl from the post, and the length of her shadow. (iii) Find the length of the girl's shadow after 4 seconds. (iv) Which similarity criterion is used to set up the two similar triangles?
Step 1 (i) Distance from post in 4 s = speed × time = 1.2 × 4 = 4.8 m.

Step-by-step solution

  1. (i) Distance from post in 4 s = speed × time = 1.2 × 4 = 4.8 m.
  2. (ii) Lamp-post AB and girl CD are both vertical ⇒ ∠B = ∠D = 90°. They share ∠E (the angle at the tip of the shadow). By AA, △ABE ~ △CDE.
  3. Corresponding sides: AB/CD = BE/DE, i.e., 3.6/0.9 = (BD + DE)/DE = (4.8 + x)/x.
  4. (iii) 3.6/0.9 = 4 ⇒ 4 = (4.8 + x)/x ⇒ 4x = 4.8 + x ⇒ 3x = 4.8 ⇒ x = 1.6 m.
  5. (iv) AA similarity criterion (right angle + shared angle at E).

Answer: (i) 4.8 m; (ii) AB/CD = BE/DE; (iii) shadow = 1.6 m; (iv) AA similarity criterion.
